## Visiting Japan's "Unmanned" Shrines

Shrines with no resident priest — standing deep in the mountains, on remote islands, or in other places far from any town — can still be found all across Japan today. Many trace their roots back to forms of nature worship older than shrine architecture itself, such as sacred-rock (iwakura) veneration or the mountain asceticism of Shugendo, and what defines them isn't convenience but the journey required to reach them — a journey that becomes part of the visit itself.

If you're hoping for a goshuin or an amulet, it's common to find only pre-written copies available, or to need to travel to a main shrine or nearby tourist information center instead. Checking ahead of time will save you confusion once you're there.

## Things to Keep in Mind When You Visit

- **Research your route in advance**: Many of these shrines require walking mountain trails or unpaved roads, and in some cases car navigation can direct you to the wrong place entirely. Check your walking route and travel time beforehand.
- **Mind your footwear and gear**: Stone stairways, steep mountain paths, and unpaved approaches are common, so wear shoes built for walking. Avoid visiting after dark, and aim to arrive while it's still bright out.
- **Enjoy the legends as legends**: Several of these shrines carry urban legends tied to ancient artifacts, but most are unproven theories that spread in relatively modern times. Enjoy them alongside — not in place of — each shrine's own authentic history.

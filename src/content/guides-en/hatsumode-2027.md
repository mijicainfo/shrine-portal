## New Year's Day 2027 falls on a Friday

January 1, 2027 (Reiwa 9) is a Friday. The first three days of the year, January 1 to 3, are Friday, Saturday and Sunday.

"Hatsumode" is the first shrine visit of the new year. This guide uses announcements that Meiji Jingu, Tsurugaoka Hachimangu, Sumiyoshi Taisha, Ise Jingu and others have published on their official websites. Times, amulets and traffic restrictions for New Year are taken from the **announcements for New Year 2026 (Reiwa 8)**; the announcements for New Year 2027 had not been published as of November 2026. Shrines usually post them closer to the end of the year, so please always check the official website before you go.

## How long can you wait for hatsumode?

None of the shrine announcements we checked sets a deadline for the first visit. In the closing line of its Reiwa 8 hatsumode notice, Tsurugaoka Hachimangu says (in Japanese) that visitors need not limit themselves to the New Year period and are welcome whenever their health and schedule allow.

Meiji Jingu divides the reception hours for its New Year first prayer ceremony as follows (2026): on January 1, from 1:00 AM to 5:00 PM; from January 2 to 7, from 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM; and from January 8, from 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM.

Some people aim to visit within "matsu no uchi," the period when New Year pine decorations are displayed (often said to run to January 7 in eastern Japan and to January 15 in western Japan). This is mostly a custom of daily life, and we could not confirm it as an official deadline set by shrines. If you have a particular shrine in mind, check its official website for the dates of its New Year ceremony and amulet distribution.

## How to visit: from washing your hands to praying

The steps below follow the official pages of Ise Jingu ("Etiquette and manners for visiting") and Meiji Jingu ("For visitors").

### 1. Purify your hands and mouth at the temizu basin

The Ise Jingu website explains that washing your hands and mouth is a simplified form of misogi, a purification rite. The steps are:

1. Take a generous scoop of water with the ladle in your right hand and wash your left hand.
2. Switch the ladle to your left hand and wash your right hand.
3. Take the ladle in your right hand again, pour water into your left hand and rinse your mouth. Do not put the ladle directly to your mouth.
4. Wash your left hand once more.
5. Use the remaining water to rinse the handle of the ladle, then put it back.

Meiji Jingu's instructions are: first wash both hands, then pour water into your hands and rinse your mouth, then wash both hands again. Some shrines set up temporary flowing-water basins without ladles for the New Year period (Meiji Jingu's hatsumode page says it adds temporary flowing-water basins). Follow the signs on site.

### 2. Go before the sanctuary and make an offering

Meiji Jingu's guidance gives the first step of worship as "first offer a coin, and straighten your posture." For clothing, it asks visitors to dress in a way suited to visiting a shrine and to spend their time quietly.

### 3. Bow and clap: two bows, two claps, one bow

The steps published on the Ise Jingu website are:

1. Step before the sanctuary and straighten your posture.
2. With a flat back, bend at the waist to about 90 degrees and bow deeply twice.
3. Bring your hands together at chest height, with your right fingertips slightly lower.
4. Open your hands to about shoulder width and clap twice.
5. Bring your fingertips back together and bow deeply once more.

The site explains that a deep bow is called "hai" and clapping is called "hakushu," and asks visitors to pray with a heart of gratitude. Meiji Jingu also describes the same flow of an offering, two bows, two claps and one bow.

### Some shrines have their own etiquette

Etiquette is not the same at every shrine. In its FAQ, Izumo Taisha says that the common form is two bows, two claps and one bow, but that its own formal worship is two bows, four claps and one bow, and asks visitors to use this form at the other sanctuaries as well as the main hall.

Ise Jingu also gives guidance specific to its grounds, such as walking on the left side at the Geku (Outer Shrine) and the right side at the Naiku (Inner Shrine). When in doubt, follow the signs in the grounds or the shrine's official website.

## Tips for avoiding crowds

### Know the busy times

On its hatsumode page, Meiji Jingu says that "from 10:00 PM on New Year's Eve to 3:00 AM on New Year's Day" and "after 10:00 AM during the first three days" are usually crowded, and asks visitors to spread out their visits. Even at the same shrine, how crowded it is changes a lot with the time of day.

Some shrines can be visited all night from New Year's Eve into New Year's Day. For 2026, Meiji Jingu announced New Year's Eve 6:40 AM to 12:00 midnight, and New Year's Day from 12:00 midnight to 6:30 PM. Tsurugaoka Hachimangu announced 24-hour visiting from December 31 to January 3, midnight to 8:00 PM on January 4, and 6:00 AM to 8:00 PM from January 5.

### You do not have to go in the first three days

As noted above, Tsurugaoka Hachimangu closes its hatsumode notice by saying visitors are welcome whenever their health and schedule allow. If crowds worry you, you can shift your visit to a weekday in January.

### Take public transport, not a car

Meiji Jingu announced that vehicles cannot enter the grounds during the first three days (2026) and that from January 5 only the Yoyogi entrance (north entrance) is open to them. Some roads around the shrine are also under traffic control during the New Year period.

On the access page of its official website, Fushimi Inari Taisha asks visitors to use public transport and says the on-site parking lot is closed from December 30 to January 6. It refers visitors to the Kyoto Prefectural Police website for temporary traffic restrictions during hatsumode.

Sumiyoshi Taisha also announced that, because of traffic control around the shrine, visitors could not come by car from December 31 to January 3 (2026).

### Check crowding in advance

Tsurugaoka Hachimangu streams live camera images on its official website, so you can check how crowded the grounds are from home. It also says that police and guards may control traffic when it is crowded, that strollers may not be taken up the main stone steps at busy times, and it asks visitors not to bring pets.

## Amulets and goshuin you can receive at New Year

### Zodiac items

2027 is the Year of the Sheep. For the New Year of Reiwa 8 (Year of the Horse), Meiji Jingu offered a zodiac clay bell (eto suzu) and a zodiac votive plaque (eto ema) at 1,000 yen each. They are limited in number and the design changes every year. The 2027 (Sheep Year) items and their prices will be announced by each shrine around the end of the year.

For shrines and festivals connected to the Year of the Sheep, see our featured guide "[2027 Year of the Sheep Shrine Guide](/en/guides/hitsujidoshi-2027/)."

### Special New Year goshuin

Some shrines offer goshuin (shrine stamps) only at New Year.

- **Meiji Jingu**: In 2026 it offered a limited goshuin from the start of the year, featuring the "Meiji Jingu Painting" dedicated in 1930 (Showa 5) by the painter Yokoyama Taikan.
- **Sumiyoshi Taisha**: It offered the "Hattatsu goshuin" as a special New Year item from January 1 to 18 (1,000 yen). From January 1 to 17, the goshuin of all four shrines are given out at Nankunsha (Hattatsu-san).

Special goshuin and arrangements, such as pre-written sheets only (kakioki), differ from shrine to shrine and from year to year. Some shrines also stop writing directly into goshuin books for a period, so please check the official website beforehand.

## Returning old amulets and talismans

Some shrines collect the previous year's talismans and amulets at a dedicated collection point. Here are two examples from official announcements.

- **Meiji Jingu**: Old items are returned at the "koshinpu osamedokoro." Items accepted are ofuda (talismans), omamori (amulets), guardian arrows and kaburaya, and ema; dolls, household shrines, rakes (kumade) and New Year decorations are not accepted. The location changes by day: the one beside the south temizu hall is permanent, and the ones at each approach entrance were available until January 12 (2026). Remove paper and bags and take them home. Meiji Jingu also says it accepts only its own ofuda and omamori, not lucky charms from elsewhere.
- **Ise Jingu**: The FAQ on its official website says to return old talismans and amulets to the "furufuda osamedokoro." At the Geku it is across from the temizu hall on the main approach, and at the Naiku it is in front of the Hiyokebashi bridge. It says it cannot take shimenawa ropes, ornaments or anything other than talismans and amulets.

Both shrines limit what they accept to ofuda, omamori and similar items. New Year decorations and ornaments have to be disposed of in another way, so check the announcement of the shrine you plan to visit before bringing them. Where to return items and when they are accepted differ by shrine.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to visit during the first three days?

None of the shrine announcements we checked says you must. Tsurugaoka Hachimangu says visitors are welcome whenever their health and schedule allow. If crowds are a concern, you can also shift your timing (Meiji Jingu says "10:00 PM on New Year's Eve to 3:00 AM" and "after 10:00 AM during the first three days" are usually crowded).

### What should I do if I am unsure about the etiquette?

Ise Jingu and Meiji Jingu both describe purifying your hands and mouth at the temizu basin, making an offering, and then two bows, two claps and one bow. However, some shrines, such as Izumo Taisha, use two bows, four claps and one bow as their formal form. Follow the signs in the grounds and the shrine's official website.

### Where do I return old talismans and amulets?

Meiji Jingu and Ise Jingu accept them at collection points in their grounds (koshinpu osamedokoro and furufuda osamedokoro). Items are limited to ofuda, omamori and similar things, and some shrines accept only their own. The location can change by day, so check the official website beforehand.

### Can I go by car?

Meiji Jingu announced that vehicles cannot enter the grounds during the first three days, and Sumiyoshi Taisha announced that visitors could not come by car from December 31 to January 3 (both 2026). Fushimi Inari Taisha closes its on-site parking from December 30 to January 6. Public transport is recommended.

### Can I get a goshuin?

It depends on the shrine. Meiji Jingu and Sumiyoshi Taisha offered special New Year goshuin (2026). Some shrines offer only pre-written sheets, or stop writing directly into goshuin books for a period, so check the official website beforehand.

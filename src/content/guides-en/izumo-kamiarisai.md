## Kannazuki and Kamiarizuki

The tenth month of the old calendar is generally called "Kannazuki." Izumo Taisha's official website explains that this comes from the old tale that the gods of all of Japan gather once a year at Izumo Taisha for the council "Kamihakari," at which the bonds of happiness among people are arranged. For this reason, in the Izumo region the old-calendar tenth month is called "Kamiarizuki," the month the gods are present.

The idea that the yaoyorozu no kami, Japan's myriad deities, gather at Izumo is a long-standing tradition. Sada Shrine's official website explains that the Kamiarisai was originally thought to be related to the Niiname-sai (harvest festival), and that the tradition of gods from all provinces gathering in Izumo arose in the medieval period, after which the month came to be called "Kamiarizuki." Because there are several explanations of the origin, keeping this in mind can make a visit all the more interesting.

## The 2026 Schedule (Izumo Taisha)

On Izumo Taisha's official "Reiwa 8 Kamiarisai (Guide)" page, the 2026 Kamiarizuki period is given as the evening of November 18 to the evening of November 25 (as of November 2026). The main flow is as follows.

- **Evening of November 18, Kamimukae rite and Kamimukae-sai**: At Inasa-no-Hama, about 1 km west of Izumo Taisha, the Kamimukae rite welcoming the gods of all of Japan is held. The page says the gods who arrive are led to Izumo Taisha by the messenger deity Ryuja (龍蛇神), and the Kamimukae-sai is held at the Haiden (worship hall). White gohei are given out at the entrance to the Inasa-no-Hama ceremony site.
- **November 19, 23 and 25, Kamiarisai**: The gods' council, "Kamihakari," is said to be presided over by Okuninushi no Okami. At the Upper Shrine (上宮), a temporary shrine where the council is held, rites are offered each day.
- **November 23 and 25, Enmusubi Taisai**: Held with the Kamiarisai, at 10:00 AM on both days. The official site says applications to attend have reached capacity for both days.
- **November 25, 4:00 PM, Karasade-sai**: The gods move from their lodgings, the Jukusha (十九社), to the Haiden, where the farewell rite is held.
- **November 19 to 24, from 5:00 PM, Yokagura prayers**: Prayer rites held at the Kaguraden (sacred dance hall).

These are the 2026 dates. Because the Kamiarisai is held in the period corresponding to the old-calendar tenth month, the dates in the current calendar change from year to year. Hinomisaki Shrine's notice lists its 2026 Kamimukae-sai alongside "the 11th of the old-calendar tenth month." If you are going in a later year, please be sure to check that year's official guide for the schedule.

## What to Know About Izumo Taisha's Kamiarisai

Crowds are expected to be especially large during the Kamiarisai period. Izumo Taisha's official website says it is considering traffic regulation on the procession route from Inasa-no-Hama to Izumo Taisha, and lists 9:00 AM on November 19 and 10:00 AM on the 23rd and 25th under its traffic regulation heading for 2026. Details of the regulation can be found in the official traffic notice (<a href="https://izumooyashiro.or.jp/archives/news/12543" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Traffic Regulation and Nearby Parking</a>).

According to Hinomisaki Shrine's notice, on November 19 and November 21-23, Izumo Taisha's busy period, the Ichibata Bus Taisha and Hinomisaki lines run on a special timetable, and bus stops may differ from usual.

The regular visiting hours are given in an official notice dated May 3, 2026 as 6:00 AM to 7:00 PM. The area behind the main hall closes at 4:30 PM, and after 7:00 PM the inner sacred enclosure (御垣内) closes, so worship is from in front of the Bronze Torii. Please check the official website before visiting for how hours are handled during the Kamiarisai period. Full details of the schedule are on the official <a href="https://izumooyashiro.or.jp/r8_kamiari" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reiwa 8 Kamiarisai guide</a>.

## Kamiarisai Beyond Izumo Taisha

Izumo Taisha's official website lists the following four shrines as Kamiari shrines in the Izumo region, with their Reiwa 8 periods (as of November 2026).

- Asayama Shrine (Asayama-cho, Izumo): November 9 to 18
- Hinomisaki Shrine (Hinomisaki, Taisha-cho, Izumo): November 19 to 25
- Kumano Taisha (Yakumo-cho Kumano, Matsue): November 19 to December 4
- Mankusen Shrine (Hikawa-cho Hikawa, Izumo): November 25 to December 4

Of these, Hinomisaki Shrine's official notice gives the Kamimukae-sai as November 19 (the 11th of the old-calendar tenth month), the Ryuja Shrine festival as November 22, and the Kamiokuri-sai as November 25 (the 17th of the old-calendar tenth month). Kumano Taisha explains on its official website that it revived the Kamiarisai recorded in Edo-period documents.

Another that should not be missed is the <a href="http://sadajinjya.jp/?m=wp&amp;WID=4196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Kamiarisai at Sada Shrine</a> in Matsue. According to its official website, the festival dates are November 20-25 in the current calendar, with the Kamimukae rite at 8:00 PM on the 20th and the Karasade rite at 8:00 PM on the 25th. The site explains that the tradition that the gods stop at Izumo Taisha and then come to Sada Shrine is a mistaken account, which arose because the Kamiarisai at Izumo Taisha kept the earlier abstinence period (上忌) while Sada Shrine kept the later one (下忌).

## Tips for Visiting

- **Always check the official schedule**: Izumo Taisha's schedule follows the old calendar, so the dates in the current calendar change from year to year. The dates in this article are as of November 2026. Some shrines have not announced times.
- **Many events are held at night**: The Kamimukae rite is in the evening, the Yokagura prayers start at 5:00 PM, and Sada Shrine's Kamimukae and Karasade rites are at 8:00 PM. A November night by the sea or in the grounds is likely to be cold, so we recommend dressing warmly.
- **Arrange transport and lodging early**: Roads around Izumo Taisha are expected to be congested, and buses run on a special timetable on some days. It is safer to arrange your lodging and travel early.
- **If you are visiting several shrines, watch the overlap of dates**: Hinomisaki Shrine's Kamiarisai runs November 19-25 and Kumano Taisha's November 19 to December 4, for example, so the periods differ from shrine to shrine. Plan around the dates of Izumo Taisha's rites and the festival days of the other shrines.
- **Visit quietly**: Sada Shrine's official website explains that in the old days the Kamiarisai was a festival of abstinence in which music, dancing and noise were avoided. At the ritual sites today, too, please try to keep quiet.

You can find full details on each shrine's history, access, and goshuin information on its individual page.

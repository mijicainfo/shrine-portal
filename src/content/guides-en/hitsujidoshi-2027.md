## What Is the Year of the Sheep (Hitsuji-doshi)?

The Year of the Sheep (Hitsuji-doshi) is the year that falls on "hitsuji" (未) of the Chinese zodiac, and the next one is 2027 (Reiwa 9). According to the zodiac table in the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan's Reki Wiki, hitsuji corresponds to roughly 1 p.m. to 3 p.m. in terms of time, and the southwest direction between hitsuji and saru (monkey) is called "Hitsujisaru" (坤).

The character 未 itself has long had various explanations: it is said to represent plants growing thick and dark (昧) or fruit ripening and gaining flavor (味), and the animal sheep is often explained as having been assigned to it later as a memory aid. The shrines introduced here relate to the sheep in varied ways: a shrine with a sheep in its name, a shrine with sheep-related lore, a shrine whose festival is held in sheep years, and a shrine planning sheep-year ema.

## A Different Kind of Sheep-Year Festival: The Shimosa Sanzan Nana-nen Matsuri

In Chiba Prefecture, the "Shimosa Sanzan Nana-nen Matsuri" (Seven-Year Festival of the Three Mountains of Shimosa) is held in Ox and Sheep years. According to guidance from Chiba Prefecture, Funabashi City and Chiba City, nine shrines in Funabashi, Chiba, Yachiyo and Narashino take part, and the festival is designated an Intangible Folk Cultural Property of Chiba Prefecture (designated in March 2004). It is held every six years, in Ox and Sheep years, and is called the "Seven-Year Festival" because it falls on the seventh year when counted in the traditional way. It consists of a minor festival in September and a major festival in November, and Chiba City's guidance also introduces the saying "In the Sanzan festival, what comes later comes first."

2027 is a Sheep year, but the specific 2027 schedule could not yet be confirmed on the guidance pages of Funabashi City, Chiba City or Chiba Prefecture (as of October 2026). If you are thinking of visiting or watching, please check each city's latest information (<a href="https://www.city.funabashi.lg.jp/kurashi/gakushu/0005/p008851.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Funabashi City's guidance page</a>). This site does not yet have pages for the shrines that take part in the Shimosa Sanzan Nana-nen Matsuri.

## Another Event in Nagano That Year (a Temple)

It is not a shrine, but at Zenkoji Temple in Nagano City, the Maedachi Honzon Gokaicho (special public unveiling of the replica of the main image) is scheduled for April 4 to June 19 in Reiwa 9 (2027), according to the official Gokaicho website. Because the period overlaps with Togakushi Jinja's Shikinen Taisai (April 25 to May 25), it can be a useful reference when planning a trip to the Nagano area.

## Things to Know When Visiting in a Sheep Year

- **Zodiac items are often offered from the end of the year**: Many shrines begin offering zodiac items such as ema and amulets from the end of the year through around the New Year, and details and dates differ from shrine to shrine. As of October 2026, the only official announcement for 2027 that we could confirm among the shrines introduced here was Tanashi Jinja's ema. Please check each shrine's official website for whether items will be offered and when.
- **Hitsuji Shrine (Annaka) is an unstaffed shrine**: Goshuin and offered items are received at the Sakisaki Shrine office, which looks after it. Before visiting, please check the Sakisaki Shrine office's reception hours and other details.
- **Check for further news on the Togakushi Jinja Shikinen Taisai schedule**: The period has been announced officially, but the times and locations of each event are to be announced once decided. It may also be crowded during the period.
- **Tell the sources of introductory articles apart**: Items such as the "koma-hitsuji" and sheep ornaments are mainly introduced by tourism sites and visitor accounts rather than the shrines' official websites. Please see how things actually look when you visit in person.

You can find full details on each shrine's history, access, and goshuin information on its individual page.

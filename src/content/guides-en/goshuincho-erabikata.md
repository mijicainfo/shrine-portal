## What is a goshuincho?

A goshuincho is a book for recording and carefully keeping the goshuin you receive. On its official website, Chichibu Jinja describes the goshuincho as a book "for recording the goshuin that are bestowed and keeping them safe." Kitano Tenmangu introduces its own goshuincho as a "book for stamping the goshuin of shrines and temples."

<p class="mt-2">A goshuin is something you receive as proof that you have visited a shrine. When you want to keep a wish close at hand, there are items such as omamori (amulets). It may help to think of the goshuincho as a way to keep the memory of your visits safe. Each shrine has its own way of thinking, so if you are unsure, ask at the reception counter.</p>

## Where can you get one?

Within what could be confirmed on official websites, shrines give out goshuincho at their offering counters (juyosho) and goshuin counters. On its official website (English page), Meiji Jingu says that goshuincho are also given out at the goshuin counter. Kitano Tenmangu gives out its original goshuincho at its offering counter, and Dazaifu Tenmangu at its goshuin counter.

Whether you can buy a goshuincho by mail without visiting the shrine differs from shrine to shrine. Within what could be confirmed on official websites, Kashima Jingu says that goshuin and goshuincho "are given as proof of worship," so it cannot send them. Asakusa Jinja also says that it does not send goshuincho or goshuin by mail. Kitano Tenmangu likewise says that it does not send goshuin by mail.

Before you set out, it is reassuring to check how the shrine you are visiting gives them out on its official website.

## A price guide, and whether the goshuin is included

Within what could be confirmed on official websites, the hatsuho-ryo (offering fee) for a goshuincho ranges from 1,000 yen to 3,800 yen. Many of the shrines' original books were 1,500 yen to 2,000 yen (confirmed in November 2026).

| Shrine | Hatsuho-ryo for the goshuincho | Handling of the goshuin (official guidance) |
|---|---|---|
| Asakusa Jinja (Tokyo) | 1,000 yen (Edo komon pattern, 3 colors), 1,500 yen (Reiwa special goshuincho, 2 colors) | The book is not given out on its own; a goshuin is always written in it before it is handed over |
| Meiji Jingu (Tokyo) | 1,500 yen (English page) | Goshuin is 500 yen, separately |
| Kitano Tenmangu (Kyoto) | 2,000 yen (4 colors) | Not stated |
| Kifune Jinja (Kyoto) | 2,000 yen | Cannot be given out as a set with a goshuin |
| Iwashimizu Hachimangu (Kyoto) | Nishijin-ori 2,000 yen, maki-e 3,000 yen | Goshuin fee is separate |
| Yasaka Jinja (Kyoto) | 1,500 yen to 3,800 yen (9 types) | Not stated |
| Musashi Ichinomiya Hikawa Jinja (Saitama) | 2,000 yen | Goshuin fee included |
| Chichibu Jinja (Saitama) | Small 1,500 yen, large 2,000 yen | Includes the goshuin fee |
| Omiwa Jinja (Nara) | 2,000 yen (2 types) | Not stated |
| Kashima Jingu (Ibaraki) | 1,500 yen to 2,000 yen (5 types) | Cannot be sent by mail |

Whether the hatsuho-ryo includes the first goshuin differs from shrine to shrine. Check at the counter when you buy. Prices and types can change.

## Choosing the shape: jabara or watoji

The Japan Tourism Agency's multilingual commentary database (the commentary on the Chichibu pilgrimage temples) explains that there are two main shapes of goshuincho.

- **Jabara:** A shape that folds up like an accordion. The database introduces it as convenient for spreading out and displaying the goshuin you have collected.
- **Watoji:** A shape bound with thread, which opens like a book.

## Size and thickness

Few shrines state the size on their official websites. Among those researched for this article, Iwashimizu Hachimangu gave the Nishijin-ori goshuincho as 17.0 cm tall by 12.0 cm wide, and the maki-e goshuincho as 16.0 cm tall by 11.0 cm wide.

In addition, SOWALABO (早和製本), a bookbinding company in Kyoto, gives its jabara goshuincho (48 faces) as having a cover of 180 mm tall by 120 mm wide and a thickness of 15 mm. For its 24-face type, the company explains that it has half the number of pages of a typical goshuincho.

These are only examples. Sizes and the number of faces differ by book. Pick one up in person and check.

## Covers and design

The designs on the covers differ from shrine to shrine. Examples from official guidance include the following.

- **Kitano Tenmangu:** 4 colors
- **Asakusa Jinja:** an Edo komon pattern (3 colors: pink, navy and green) and the Reiwa special goshuincho (2 colors: purple and nureba-iro, a glossy black)
- **Iwashimizu Hachimangu:** a Nishijin-ori design of the shinko procession of the Iwashimizu Festival, and a maki-e design of the main shrine building, a National Treasure
- **Musashi Ichinomiya Hikawa Jinja:** a design of the two-story gate (romon) and clouds
- **Chichibu Jinja:** fireworks and kasaboko floats, a dragon and a tiger, three monkeys and an owl, and more
- **Omiwa Jinja:** two types: a design of three rabbits and sasayuri (lilies), and a pale green cover depicting the worship hall and Mt. Miwa
- **Sumiyoshi Taisha:** says that its zodiac goshuincho has a file on one side

Some covers use a shrine's festival or buildings as their design.

## Carrying it: bags and cases

Some shrines also give out bags and cases to hold a goshuincho.

- Kitano Tenmangu gives out a "Goshuincho (case)" (2,000 yen) as a bag to hold a goshuincho.
- Kifune Jinja gives out goshuincho bags for 5,000 yen (red and blue) and 5,500 yen (linen fabric), and gives their size as 16.5 cm tall by 21.5 cm wide with a 3 cm gusset.
- Iwashimizu Hachimangu gives out a drawstring bag (goshuincho bag) for 1,500 yen, and gives its size as 25.5 cm tall by 17.0 cm wide.

The Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association advises keeping a goshuincho in a clean place.

## Can shrines and temples share one book?

The Japan Tourism Agency's multilingual commentary database (the commentary on the Chichibu pilgrimage temples) introduces the fact that some people use separate books for shrines and temples. It adds that a book carrying the seals of another religion may be declined, and explains that whether to separate them is a matter of personal preference. Kitano Tenmangu introduces its own goshuincho as a "book for stamping the goshuin of shrines and temples."

If you are unsure, follow the guidance of the shrine or temple you are visiting. For more detail, please also see "[Goshuin for Beginners](/en/guides/goshuin-nyumon/)."

## How to paste in a paper goshuin

Kakioki is a goshuin that has been written on paper in advance. The Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association says that a goshuin received on paper can also be glued into your goshuincho later. However, some shrines do not prepare paper goshuin, so do not press the request.

Depending on the shrine, the season or the place, goshuin are not written in the book but given on paper. Examples confirmed on official websites are as follows.

- Chichibu Jinja says that during its Annual Grand Festival, from December 1 to 6, it gives only kakioki (printed).
- Iwashimizu Hachimangu says that the goshuin of its three sessha (武内社, 石清水社 and 高良神社) are given only as kakioki.
- Yasaka Jinja says that from 9:00 to 16:00 it writes directly into the goshuincho, and that after 16:00 it gives only a sheet of paper.

We could not find an explanation on the official websites about how to paste in a paper goshuin (the type of glue, or where to paste it).

## Things to keep in mind when you have it written

- **Open the page you want written and hand it over.** The Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association recommends handing over the book opened to the page you want written. Check which way is up, and tell the staff in advance of any request, such as "please leave one page blank."
- **Take off any cover with a button, and any bookmark.** Asakusa Jinja says that if the book has a cover with a button or a bookmark tucked in, it is hard to apply the seal, so it asks you to take them off beforehand. Loosen any binding cord as well.
- **Write your name in it.** The Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association advises writing your name in the book, because at some shrines you leave your goshuincho at the reception.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I do if I forget my goshuincho?

The Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association and the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) say that if you forget your goshuincho, you may be able to receive a goshuin on paper. Some shrines do not prepare paper goshuin, so do not press the request.

### Will they write in my goshuincho at New Year or on festival days?

Depending on the shrine, during crowded periods or on event days goshuin are not written in the book but given on paper. For example, Chichibu Jinja says that during its Annual Grand Festival, from December 1 to 6, it gives only kakioki. Check the official website before you go.

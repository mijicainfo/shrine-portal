## History

Hitsuji Shrine (ひつじじんじゃ) is a small shrine in Nakanoya, Annaka, Gunma. The reading follows Sakisaki Shrine's official site. The enshrined deities are Amenokoyane no Mikoto and Tago Hitsuji Dayu Fujiwara no Munekatsu. It is an unstaffed shrine, looked after concurrently by the priest of Sakisaki Shrine.

According to the Hitsuji Shrine page on Sakisaki Shrine's official site, it was founded in the Enpo era (1673–81). Members of the Tago clan who moved from Tago District are said to have opened new fields and enshrined their ancestral deity Hitsuji Dayu, and the page also records a formal enshrinement in 1802. In 1997 the parishioners built a new main hall and a "twelve shrines" hall and held the transfer ceremony, and the following year the guardian dogs, the Mitsumine shrine and the torii were added, the same page says.

Hitsuji Dayu is a legendary figure from Gunma. According to the official site, he is said to have traveled to and from the capital in a single day, accompanied by a young man with wing-like growths at his sides and a famous horse. He received the Fujiwara surname for his service, but in the end flew away as a golden butterfly. He is also said to have discovered native copper in Chichibu, been granted Tago District, and raised the Tago Monument in commemoration. The Gunma tourism site's article likewise links him to the Tago Monument and describes him as someone who brought copper mining and sericulture. All of this is tradition and has not been confirmed as history.

## Highlights

Stone guardian dogs stand in front of the torii, and statues in the shape of sheep line the front of the main hall. The Gunma tourism article describes them as "sheep komainu" with fluffy coats, round eyes and a bell at the neck. The same article says a sheep ema with a red nose and a plump body hangs to the right of the main hall. Beside the hall stands a signboard with a sheep illustration (installed in 2015, a Year of the Sheep, according to Japanese Wikipedia) that catches the eye along with the shrine's name.

The official site notes a monument inscribed "Tago-gu Hitsuji Dayu Munekatsu Shinzo-i" in the grounds. The shrine crest is "wisteria with a five-ring pagoda." The hall was rebuilt in 1997, and the shrine sits quietly among trees in a residential area.

The Gunma tourism site says people born in Years of the Sheep often visit, calling it a guardian of the Year of the Sheep. The next Year of the Sheep is 2027 (Reiwa 9).

## Visiting Tips

Hitsuji Shrine is unstaffed, with no shrine office or amulet counter. Goshuin, ofuda and omamori are all handled at the office of Sakisaki Shrine (3308 Sagimiya, Annaka), which looks after it. According to Sakisaki Shrine's official site, reception is 9:00 AM–4:00 PM with irregular closing days. Goshuin for affiliated shrines cost ¥300, and the "Hitsuji Daruma goshuin" costs ¥1,500, available pre-written or written on the spot. The writer is sometimes absent, so it is safest to call 027-381-2726. The official site asks people who have not visited, or do not plan to visit, an affiliated shrine to refrain from requesting its goshuin, so visit Hitsuji Shrine first and then go on to Sakisaki Shrine.

The Hitsuji Shrine items listed on the official site include a sheep omamori (¥800), a fluffy sheep charm (¥700), a body-health charm (¥700) and a strap charm (¥600). No 2027-only items were announced on the official site as of October 2026. Limited items mentioned on blogs could not be confirmed officially, so please ask Sakisaki Shrine directly. One article says the Hitsuji Daruma goshuin is also given at Hitsuji Shrine, but the official site says items are given at Sakisaki Shrine's office, so sources differ.

It is about 2.2 km (roughly 30 minutes on foot) from Isobe Station on the JR Shin'etsu Line, or about 6–7 minutes by taxi. Parking information is thin: one article says one space, and it is a residential area where quiet visits are asked for. Travel time to Sakisaki Shrine is given as about 5–10 minutes by car, differing by article; no walking time is given in the sources.

## Annual Festivals

- **Spring festival (March 15)**: Listed on the Hitsuji Shrine page of Sakisaki Shrine's official site. The Gunma tourism article says it began at 8:00 AM with a norito prayer, followed by tamagushi offerings by parishioners.
- **Autumn festival (October 15)**: Also listed on the Hitsuji Shrine page of the official site.
- **Taitai Kagura at Sakisaki Shrine (April 1)**: Offered on the day of its spring festival. It is designated an Important Intangible Cultural Property of Annaka City and is registered as a Gunma Silk Heritage item (Sakisaki Shrine's official site).
- **New Year's Festival at Sakisaki Shrine (from January 1)**: The official site says the festival begins at midnight.

The festivals are centered on parishioners. Dates and details can change, so check Sakisaki Shrine's official site or call ahead.

## Nearby Food & Sightseeing

- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sakisaki%20Shrine%20Sagimiya%20Annaka%20Gunma" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Sakisaki Shrine</strong></a>: The shrine that looks after Hitsuji Shrine and where goshuin and omamori are given. Its official site describes it as the predecessor of Nukisaki Shrine, the Ichinomiya of Kozuke. The grounds also hold the auxiliary Kinugasa Shrine, enshrining the sericulture deity Ukemochi no Mikoto, and a tradition of venerating a white snake as a guardian of silkworms.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Isobe%20Onsen%20Annaka%20Gunma" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Isobe Onsen</strong></a>: A hot-spring town around Isobe Station. Guide articles say it is regarded as the birthplace of the hot-spring map symbol, based on a mark drawn on an early Edo-period map (the symbol's origin is disputed). A good stop on the way back from your visit.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Isobe%20Station%20Annaka%20Gunma" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Isobe Station</strong></a>: The nearest JR Shin'etsu Line station, about 30 minutes on foot or 6–7 minutes by taxi to Hitsuji Shrine.

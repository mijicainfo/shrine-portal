## History

Shirahige Shrine stands in Higashi-Mukojima, Sumida Ward, Tokyo. It is read "Shirahige Jinja" (per the Sumida City page) and is a different shrine from the Shirahige Shrine in Takashima, Shiga. According to the official site, it began in 951 (Tenryaku 5), when a branch spirit of Shirahige Shrine in Omi Province (today's Takashima, Shiga) was enshrined here, and its main deity is Sarutahiko Okami. Sarutahiko is a deity of land development and of guiding people in the right direction, and is worshipped for safe departure, traffic safety, business prosperity and protection from directional misfortune. It is also the guardian shrine for the former Terashima Town area: Higashi-Mukojima, Sumida, Tsutsumidori, Kyojima, Yahiro and Oshiage.

The shrine enshrines Jurojin of the Sumidagawa Seven Lucky Gods. According to Sumida City, the pilgrimage began in the late Edo period with Sahara Kikuu (the owner of Mukojima Hyakkaen garden) and his literary friends, and it remains a popular New Year's visit. The pilgrimage's official site says it was devised by literati who gathered at Hyakkaen during the Bunka era and has a history of more than 200 years. The shrine's official site says it came to enshrine Jurojin because of the link with its name.

The present buildings are said to be a reconstruction after a fire. Japanese Wikipedia and other sources say the main hall was destroyed by arson in 1990 and rebuilt in 1992, but I could not confirm this on the shrine's official site.

## Highlights

The grounds hold three registered cultural properties of Sumida City. According to the city's list, all are owned by the Shirahige Shrine religious corporation: the stone guardian lion-dogs ("Shirahige Jinja no Sekizo Komainu," a pair, registered March 15, 1984; tangible folk cultural property), the Chinese-poem stele "Bokuta Sanzetsu" by Sawa Tansai (one, registered August 21, 2006; tangible cultural property, historical material), and the stele of the Yamadama Mukojima Kosha (two, registered October 19, 2011; tangible folk cultural property). A personal blog (Jinja Memo) says the lion-dogs in front of the worship hall and the stone monuments along the approach are among these.

According to the pilgrimage's official site, the grounds have a kagura hall, where festival music was offered at about 9:00 AM on January 2, 2026. Per Sumida City, the treasure-ship charms of the pilgrimage are made by hand by shrine maidens. The ship is a white ceramic boat about 20 cm long, 8 cm wide and 3 cm high. A small figure of a god (¥500 each) is placed on it and it is displayed at the entrance or on a household shrine. The ship cost ¥1,500 (2026 notice), and distribution begins at 12:00 AM on January 1 after a purification rite at the shrine on New Year's Eve night.

## Visiting Tips

The official site gives no gate hours for the grounds. Prayer-service reception is 9:00 AM–3:30 PM on the top page but 9:00 AM–4:00 PM on the prayer page, so the two differ. A service takes about 20–30 minutes and covers requests such as first shrine visits for newborns, household safety, business prosperity, traffic safety, safe travel, academic success, exam success, and protection from misfortune and from all directions. The offering is "whatever amount you feel," says the official site. Phone reception is 9:00 AM–5:00 PM at 03-3611-2750.

I could not find whether goshuin are offered, nor their fee or hours, on the official site. A 2018 personal blog mentions ¥300 and a counter to the left of the main hall, but the current details are unconfirmed. Please check at the counter when you visit.

The official site gives only the address and phone number for access. Personal blogs say it is about a 10-minute walk from Higashi-Mukojima Station on the Tobu Skytree Line (Isesaki Line). No parking information is given.

## Annual Festivals

- **First prayer and New Year's visit (from January 1)**: According to the official site, the first prayer service is held from 12:00 AM on January 1 as the hall doors open. Visiting hours are 12:00 AM–5:00 PM on January 1 and 8:00 AM–5:00 PM from January 2.
- **Sumidagawa Seven Lucky Gods pilgrimage (January 1–7)**: According to the pilgrimage's official site, in 2026 the matching shikishi board and goshuin book were available through January 7, and from January 8 each temple or shrine gives its own main-deity goshuin.
- **Setsubun Festival (February 3)**: The official 2026 notice lists the ritual at 3:00 PM and the bean-throwing at 3:30 PM.
- **Bonden Festival (May 5)**: The official site says gohei wands are made for a ritual, and a flood-prevention ritual is held on the bank of the Sumida River.
- **Annual festival (first weekend of June)**: The official site says that the "Honmatsuri," held once every three years, includes a procession of the shrine's main portable shrine through the neighborhood.
- **Minazuki Great Purification (June 30)**: From 8:00 PM (official site).
- **Shichi-Go-San (around November 15)**: On weekends and holidays from late October to late November, prayers are offered from 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM without reservation (official site).
- **Niiname-sai (November 23)**: A ritual of the great festival style.
- **Shiwasu Great Purification (December 31)**: From 7:30 PM (official site).

Festival dates can change from year to year, so check the official site for the latest.

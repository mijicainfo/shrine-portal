## History

Kamimeguro Hikawa Shrine stands on a hill in Ohashi, Meguro, Tokyo, and is read "Kamimeguro Hikawa Jinja." Meguro City's cultural-property guide introduces it under the name "Ohashi Hikawa Shrine" and describes it as the guardian shrine of the former Kamimeguro village. The official site names Susanoo no Mikoto (the deity who drives away plague), Amaterasu Omikami (the sun deity) and Sugawara no Michizane (the deity of learning), and Meguro City likewise describes Susanoo as the main deity, with Amaterasu and Michizane also enshrined.

According to the history on the official site, in the Tensho era (1573–1592) the Kato family, retainers of Takeda Shingen of Kai Province, are said to have brought the local guardian deity from Uenohara to this place. The parishioners have long been said to be free of epidemics, and the shrine is now cherished as a god of warding off misfortune. The top page of the official site also carries a request for support of a project marking the 450th anniversary of the shrine's enshrinement.

The old Oyama Road (Oyama Kaido, today's Route 246 / Tamagawa-dori) runs nearby, and according to the official site the front stone steps were remodeled into their present form when the road was widened in 1905 (Meiji 38).

## Highlights

Entering the approach from Route 246, a flight of stone steps runs straight up to the hilltop grounds. The official site says the steps are made of Komatsu stone and were built in 1816 (Bunka 13), and Meguro City notes that at the foot of the steps stand a monument recording the rebuilding of the steps and an 1842 Oyama Road milestone.

There are three subsidiary shrines in the grounds (per the official site). The Sengen Shrine enshrines Konohanasakuya-hime, a deity of fire prevention and safe childbirth, and was moved in 1878 from the Moto-Fuji mound at Mekirizaka. Moto-Fuji was a Fuji mound about 12 m high, built in 1812 (Bunka 9) by a local Fuji-ko group, and the group's monument still stands in the grounds. The Inari Shrine enshrines Ukanomitama no Kami, a deity of farming and industry in general; its founding date is unknown, but because the hill was once called Inari-yama it is said to be older than the Hikawa Shrine. The Kitano Shrine enshrines Sugawara no Michizane as a deity of learning, and was merged into this shrine in 1908 (Meiji 41) according to the official site (the tourism association's page gives Meiji 45, so records differ).

The official site also describes a sacred tree said to have been planted by a neighbor 150–200 years ago, which has grown to about 20 m tall with a trunk girth of over 5 m. A guide site presents the shrine as one that calms flooding of the Meguro River and wards off epidemics.

## Visiting Tips

The official site does not state opening hours for the grounds, though some guide sites say the grounds are open freely. Prayer services run 9:00 AM–4:30 PM (reservation required, about 20 minutes each), and the official site asks visitors to dress appropriately for the Shinto rite. When it is busy, several groups may be prayed for together.

According to guide sites, goshuin are available, including seasonal and event-specific designs. One visit report gives the fee as ¥500, but the official site states no fee or hours, and some are said to be pre-written copies only, so it is best to check in advance.

It is about a 5-minute walk from the north exit of Ikejiri-Ohashi Station on the Tokyu Den-en-toshi Line. Some guide sites say there is no dedicated parking, so train or bus is recommended.

## Annual Festivals

The official site's annual-events page (a month-by-month calendar) lists the following. Dates can change from year to year.

- **New Year's visit (January 1) and Sagicho (mid-January)**
- **Setsubun festival (February 3)**
- **Inari Shrine Hatsuuma festival (the Day of the Horse in March)**: The official history says it is held with banners, together with parishioners and worshippers.
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (June 30)**
- **Fuji Sengen Shrine grand festival (July 1)**: Held on the mountain-opening day, when parishioners climb the path together.
- **Hikawa Shrine grand festival (the Saturday and Sunday closest to August 25)**: The official calendar shows a photo of a votive entertainment event. Meguro City's list gives "the 4th Saturday and Sunday of August," so the descriptions differ.
- **Kitano Shrine grand festival (the Saturday and Sunday closest to September 15)**
- **Shichi-Go-San (November 15)**
- **Shiwasu Oharae (December 30)**
- **Monthly festival (the 1st and 15th of each month)**

For the latest schedule and information on prayer services and rites, please check the official site or contact the shrine directly.

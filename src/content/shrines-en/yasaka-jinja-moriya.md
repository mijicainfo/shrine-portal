## History

Yasaka Shrine stands in Honcho, Moriya, in Ibaraki Prefecture. The shrine calls itself "Moriya Sochinju Yasaka Shrine" (the guardian shrine of all of Moriya); its formal name is Yasaka Shrine (Gozu Tenno-gu), and its deity is Susanoo no Mikoto (Gozu Tenno). Older residents lovingly call it "Tenno-sama." Because there are many shrines of the same name across Japan, including the famous one in Kyoto, this site lists it as "Yasaka Shrine, Moriya."

According to the official site, tradition holds that on the 27th day of the 9th month of 806 (Daido 1), the priest Yoshinobu enshrined Gozu Tenno as the guardian of the Moriya district at Tennokubo in Honjuku, now part of the Takano area. The shrine's sacred mirror is said to bear an inscription to that effect. In 1598 (Keicho 3) the shrine buildings were moved to the present grounds by the Toki lord of Moriya Castle, and over the following centuries they were repaired, burned and rebuilt several times. It is said to have been renamed from Tenno-sha to Yasaka Shrine in the early Meiji era, and the shrine's goshuin still carries the old name, "Gozu Tenno-gu" (official site).

The founding date of 806 is the shrine's own tradition, and we could not find independent confirmation of it.

## Highlights

**Ancient trees in the grounds.** The official site describes the shrine's sacred keyaki (zelkova) as around 400 years old. In places its branches that once split have grown back together, and visitors say this brings good relationships and marital harmony. The grounds also have a large ginkgo tree (official grounds guide).

**The Gion Festival (annual grand festival).** This is the largest festival in Moriya, with the main day on the last Saturday of July. See "Annual Festivals" below.

**Yamata no Orochi painting.** According to the official site, the shrine owns an ema votive painting of Susanoo slaying the Yamata no Orochi, dedicated in 1866 (Keio 2) and painted by the Kano-school artist Kano Tomonobu. The site presents it under "cultural property"; we could not confirm whether it has any official designation.

**Strength stone (chikara-ishi).** To the left after passing through the torii on the main approach is a "strength stone" of about 105 kg (28 kan) that, the official site says, young men of Moriya once lifted to compete in strength. Touching it is said to bring health, healthy child-rearing and success in life.

**Media coverage.** TV Tokyo's "Shutsubotsu! Ad-machic Tengoku" (Adomachi) aired an Ibaraki Moriya episode on January 24, 2026, and the program's official page lists Yasaka Shrine at No. 8. It says the shrine was founded in 806, is the town's guardian, and that its July Gion Festival draws about 50,000 visitors and is Moriya's biggest festival (the page notes that its information reflects the time of broadcast).

## Visiting Tips

**Deity and blessings.** Susanoo is known from the myth of slaying the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi and is also revered as a deity who wards off plague and misfortune. The official site lists protection from misfortune and illness among the shrine's blessings, and explains that the Gion Festival is held with prayers to avert misfortune and sickness, in keeping with the legend of Somin Shorai. It also says Susanoo is enshrined as a deity of good relationships because of his union with Kushinadahime.

**Goshuin.** Available at the shrine office. An Ibaraki Shimbun article from March 2023 described a spread-style "Susanoo no Mikoto Yamata no Orochi slaying" goshuin for ¥1,000, a "Gozu Tenno-gu" goshuin for ¥500, and a July-only "Goryo-e" goshuin for ¥500, all hand-written, available 9:00 AM–5:00 PM every day (pre-written copies may be given when it is busy). This is 2023 reporting, and the official site does not list current fees or hours, so it is safest to phone ahead (0297-46-2788).

**Prayers.** According to the official FAQ, prayer reception is 9:00 AM–5:00 PM, and reservations can be made online, by phone or at the shrine office. Offerings start from ¥5,000. Amulets are available at the shrine office at any time, though it can be unattended if staff are out.

**Access.** Get off at Moriya Station (Tsukuba Express / Kanto Railway Joso Line) and walk about 10 minutes from the Yasaguchi exit (official site). By car, it is about 5 km from the Yawara IC on the Joban Expressway toward Moriya, and there is a parking lot next to the shrine.

**Pets and photography.** According to the official site, pets may visit the grounds but may not enter the hall, and photography inside the worship hall is discouraged.

## Annual Festivals

**Gion Festival (annual grand festival, last Saturday of July).** Said to be held at the start of summer to purify the shrine's community of the epidemics that spread in summer, it is also called the "Nobori Matsuri" (banner festival) and is Moriya's largest festival. The shrine's site describes it as one of the Hokuso Sandai Gion Matsuri (the three great Gion festivals of the northern Shimosa area) and says it draws well over 50,000 visitors. According to Moriya City's official site, the festival has records going back at least about 270 years and was designated in 2015 as Moriya's first intangible folk cultural property. It is carried out by about 950 parishioner households over five days starting on the Tuesday of the main-day week. Five floats, one from each district, carry musicians and dancers on a rotating stage and spin as if competing, and the portable shrine is carried for about six hours without relief. The main day in 2026 was July 25. Details change from year to year, so please check the official site beforehand.

The gallery photo shows a dancer in a hyottoko mask at the Gion Festival on the grounds of this shrine, taken on July 25, 2009.

Other main events listed on the official site (dates per the 2026 announcements):

- **New Year's Day rite, Kitansai (January 1)**
- **Archery rite, Hoshasai (January 4)**
- **Shichi-go-san (November 15)**
- **Niiname-sai harvest festival (November 23)**

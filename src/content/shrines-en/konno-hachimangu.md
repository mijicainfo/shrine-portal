## History

Konno Hachimangu is a Hachiman shrine in Shibuya 3-chome, Shibuya Ward, Tokyo. Its name is read "Konno Hachimangu," according to the Japanese Wikipedia (the official English page spells it "Konnoh Hachimangu," and the official Japanese page glosses 金王丸 as "Konnomaru"). The enshrined deity is Emperor Ojin (Homuda-wake no Mikoto), and the official site says the shrine was enshrined on the 15th day of the first month of Kanji 6 (1092).

According to the official history, Takezuna, the son of Chichibu Betto Taira no Takemoto, distinguished himself in the Gosannen War (1083–1087), was granted the name Kawasaki Motoie, and Minamoto no Yoshiie, crediting the victory to the Hachiman deity Motoie revered, founded a Hachimangu here using the moon banner of the sun-and-moon banners Motoie held, as the shrine records tell it. The Shibuya clan, who later received the Shibuya family name, built their residence around this Hachimangu, and the site says this is also said to be the origin of Shibuya. Shibuya Castle is said to have been burned down at the Battle of Takanawahara in 1524, and stones from the Shibuya castle fortress are still preserved on the grounds.

The name "Konno" comes from Shibuya Konnomaru Tsunemitsu. The official site says that Shibuya Shigeie and his wife, who had no children, kept praying at the Hachimangu; the wife dreamed that Kongo Yasha Myoo entered her womb, and they were blessed with a son, whom they named Konnomaru from two characters of the deity's name. The site explains that Konnomaru distinguished himself in the Hogen Rebellion, later took Buddhist vows and called himself Tosabo Shoshun, and that the Konnomaru Mieido hall on the grounds holds a wooden statue said to have been carved by Konnomaru himself at age 17, when he went to war, and left to his mother. The statue is specially opened to view at the Konnomaru Festival on the last Saturday of March.

## Highlights

The main hall and the Shinmon gate are Tangible Cultural Properties designated by Shibuya Ward. The ward's list of cultural properties gives them as "Konno Hachimangu Main Hall and Gate, with Connecting Corridor," and explains that the present building is said to have been donated in 1612 (Keicho 17) by Aoyama Tadatoshi and Kasuga no Tsubone, preserving architectural styles of the early to mid Edo period. The official site says the shrine building is in the Gongen-zukuri style and that carvings of a baku and a tiger decorate the openings on either side of the front of the worship hall. For the gate (popularly called the Aka-mon, or red gate), the official site cites 1769 (Meiwa 6) in an old record and an inscription of 1801 (Kyowa 1) on the back of its plaque in explaining when it was built.

The "Konno-zakura" cherry tree on the grounds is a natural monument designated by Shibuya Ward. The official site says it is a variety called Choshu-hizakura, a rare tree on which single and double blossoms are mixed on one branch, and that the shrine records tell of Minamoto no Yoritomo having it transplanted from a mansion in Kamakura. The official site counts it as one of the "Three Famous Cherry Trees of Edo," while the ward describes it as "one of the three famous trees of the suburbs," so the descriptions differ by source. The shrine also holds two ward-designated tangible folk cultural properties: the ema "Oeyama Oni-taiji no Zu" (dedicated in 1675, Enpo 3) and three sangaku (mathematical tablets).

The grounds include subsidiary shrines such as Tamatsukuri Inari Shrine (enshrining Ugamitama no Mikoto; its festival is on the first Horse Day of February), Mitake Shrine (with the Mitake-sai rite on the first Day of the Rooster in November), and the Konnomaru Mieido hall.

## Visiting Tips

According to the official site, a daily rite (Nikkusai) is held 9:00–9:30 AM and a monthly rite (Tsukinamisai) on the 1st and 15th, and anyone may attend (gather at the shrine office by 8:50 AM). Prayer services are accepted 9:30 AM–4:30 PM, with fees from ¥5,000 for individuals (¥10,000 for a first-visit blessing for babies), and the shrine asks that you reserve by phone. A Shichi-Go-San notice, however, says same-day reception is also possible, with reserved visitors given priority if times overlap (dated October 1, 2026). For the New Year period of 2027 (January 1–15), individual prayers are not taken by phone reservation, and visitors are asked to come directly to the shrine office on January 1–3 and 9–11 (notice dated October 21, 2026).

The fee and hours for the regular goshuin are not stated on the official site, and guide sites differ on both, so checking with the shrine office (03-3407-1811) is safest. An official notice announces a limited-edition flower goshuin (¥800, pre-written paper only) from April 26, 2026 to March 31, 2027.

It is a 5-minute walk from Exit C1 of Shibuya Station, behind Shibuya Police Station (official site). The official site gives no parking information for ordinary visitors.

## Annual Festivals

Main events from the official site's annual calendar (dates may change, so check with the shrine office):

- **Saitan-sai (January 1, 6:00 AM)**: A rite welcoming the new year and praying for peace in the world and the happiness of parishioners and devotees. New Year's first prayers are offered from midnight on an ongoing basis.
- **Setsubun-sai (February 3, 4:00 PM)**: Men and women born in the year's zodiac sign receive purification and become "fortune men and women," then scatter beans and sweets.
- **Konnomaru-sai and Konno-zakura Matsuri (last Saturday of March, 11:30 AM)**: The festival of the Konnomaru Mieido hall. The wooden statue of Konnomaru is specially shown, and a cherry-blossom viewing banquet is held beneath the Konno-zakura.
- **Reisai (September 14, 11:00 AM)**: The official site calls it the largest festival of the shrine, the festival of the Aoyama and Shibuya parishioner districts. Neighborhood mikoshi parade, and around 2:00 PM on the Sunday there is a joint mikoshi procession: in the Shibuya district in front of 109 on Dogenzaka, and in the Aoyama district on Aoyama-dori.
- **Mitake-sai (first Day of the Rooster in November, 9:30 AM)**: The festival of the subsidiary Mitake Shrine, said to bring business prosperity.
- **Niiname-sai (November 23, 9:00 AM)**: Open to all. The 2026 notice asks attendees to gather at the shrine office by 8:50 AM, and offerings (rice, sake, vegetables, fruit and so on) are accepted on November 20–22.
- **Yaki-imo Event (December 6, 2026, from 10:00 AM)**: Held on the grounds, with a pork-miso soup and a quiz rally planned. Canceled in rain; the shrine says it will announce any cancellation at 8:00 AM that day in the news section of its official site (notice dated November 7, 2026).

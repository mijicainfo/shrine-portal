## History

Hitsuji Shrine stands in Tsuji-machi, Kita Ward, Nagoya. According to Nagoya City's tourism site, the date of founding is unknown, but because the Engishiki shrine register records "Hitsuji Jinja, Yamada District, Owari Province," it is regarded as an old shrine more than 1,000 years old. In fact, the view that this shrine is the Hitsuji Jinja listed in the Engishiki was put forward by scholars such as Amano Nobukage in the mid-Edo period and has been widely accepted, which makes it a "candidate" (ronsha) for the Engishiki-listed shrine. On the other hand, the late-Edo local historian Tsuda Shosei held that the original Hitsuji Jinja was the present Takamu Shrine (Moriyama Ward), so there is a dissenting view.

As to the origin of the name, Nagoya City's guide gives this tradition. Hitsuji-dayu, a lord of Tago District in Kozuke Province (now Gunma Prefecture), whose name is carved on the Tago Stele, had a residence on this site while traveling to the capital at Nara, and enshrined a fire deity here wishing for the peaceful lives of the people, so the shrine came to be called Hitsuji Jinja. This is a tradition and has not been confirmed as historical fact. The guide also notes a place-name record saying that the "Tsuji" of Tsuji-machi was originally called "Hi-tsuji Village". A shrine of the same name (in Annaka) also exists in Gunma Prefecture.

The enshrined deities are said to be Hinokagutsuchi no Kami and Amaterasu Omikami. Until the Edo period the shrine was known as "Shinmei" or "Tsuji-mura Shinmei" and was the guardian shrine of Tsuji village. A ridge-beam plaque dated 1613 (Keicho 18) reads "respectfully rebuilt Hitsuji Jinja," and a record says the shrine buildings were completed in 1838 (Tenpo 9). It became a village shrine in 1872 (Meiji 5). Because it enshrines a fire deity, it is revered as protection against fire, and locally it is said to have survived the Nagoya air raids of World War II.

## Highlights

The grounds are small, but they are known for the many sheep-themed items. At the purification fountain on the left after the torii, water flows from the mouth of a sheep. In the grounds sits a parent-and-child pair of sheep statues (komahitsuji). The bansei (screen wall) in front of the worship hall has carvings of sheep and dragons, and there are sheep figurines on the offering box and around the shrine office, so looking for them as you walk is part of the fun (per visitor reports).

The grounds also hold seven subsidiary shrines: Hakusan, Akiba, Tsushima, Mitake, Yama (mountain), Suijin (water) and Inari. The main sanctuary is said to be in the nagare-zukuri style. In autumn the ginkgo trees in the grounds are said to turn yellow.

## Visiting Tips

**Visiting in the Year of the Sheep:** 2027 is the Year of the Sheep. Meitetsu announces a hiking course, "New Year's Sheep Shrine Pilgrimage Course," running from Monday, Jan. 11 to Sunday, Jan. 31, 2027. It is about 8 km and about 2 hours 10 minutes, starting at Kamiiida Station and passing Hitsuji Shrine, Nex Plaza (Nagoya Expressway public relations center), the SAKUMACHI shopping street and Tokugawa-en before ending at Ozone Station. Meitetsu's page describes the course as one that visits "one of the few shrines in Japan with ties to sheep" (this is Meitetsu's wording). The same page gives the shrine's charm-distribution hours as 9:00-15:30. Please check Meitetsu's guide for how to take part. One visitor report from New Year 2015, the previous Year of the Sheep, describes queuing about 30-40 minutes to reach the torii.

**Shrine office, goshuin and charms:** Visitor reports give the shrine office hours as roughly 9:00-15:00 (or 9:30-15:30) on Mon, Thu, Sat and Sun, and Nagoya City says it is unstaffed on Tue, Wed and Fri in normal times. It may also be empty at other times, so if you are set on a goshuin or charm, calling (052-912-0063, Japanese only) is the surest way. A visitor blog gives the goshuin fee as 300 yen (no official notice could be confirmed, and it may change). Visitor reports mention small clay-bell charms in the zodiac animal motif, a children's charm ("kodomo-mamori") with a sheep illustration (mentioned at 500 yen), and sheep ema, but offerings and fees can change. We could not confirm any special goshuin or offerings for the 2027 Year of the Sheep.

**Access:** It is about a 10-minute walk northwest from Exit 3 of Kamiiida Station (Subway Kamiiida Line / Meitetsu Komaki Line). There is no parking for visitors, so please come by public transportation. The surrounding area is said to have many narrow one-way streets.

## Annual Festivals

- **Annual festival (Oct. 18):** According to references based on the Aichi shrine register and similar sources, the annual festival is held on October 18. The date may change, so please check beforehand.
- **Sagicho (Jan. 15):** The New Year sagicho (bonfire) ritual is given as the main rite on January 15.
- **Hatsumode (January):** New Year visits are expected to be busier in the Year of the Sheep. Crowds and the availability of offerings vary from year to year.

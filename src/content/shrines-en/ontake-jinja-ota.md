## History

Ontake Shrine (御嶽神社, read "Ontake Jinja") is in Kitaminemachi, Ota Ward, Tokyo, and is also known locally as "Mine no Ontake Jinja." The enshrined deity is Kuninotokotachi no Mikoto, together with Kunisazuchi no Mikoto and Toyokumonu no Mikoto (according to shrine-listing sites and Wikipedia; the history page of the official site did not name the deities). Its former shrine rank is described as a village shrine.

According to shrine tradition, it began around 1535 (Tenbun 4), when a small shrine was enshrined in Mine Village (this date rests on tradition rather than firm documentary evidence). In the late Edo period, the ascetic Ichiyama Gyoja, who had trained on Mt. Ontake in Kiso, set this site up as a place where the power of Mt. Ontake manifests. Ota Ward's newsletter says the present hall was built in 1831 (Tenpo 2) and that it is also called the first Kanto branch shrine of Mt. Ontake in Kiso.

From then on, worshippers of the Ontake faith gathered here from Edo and across the Kanto region. The official site records a saying: "Three visits to Mine's Ontake Shrine are the same as one trip to Mt. Ontake in Kiso." Pilgrim groups (kosha) are said to still visit today. The nearby Ontakesan Station is said to take its name from the shrine.

## Highlights

**Carvings on the shrine hall (Ota Ward cultural property)**: The hall, built in 1831, is decorated with detailed carvings that wrap around the main sanctuary. According to Ota Ward's website, they are traditionally attributed to Fujiwara Atsuoki and depict tales and anecdotes from Japan and China, such as Urashima Taro, the Yoro Waterfall and Sima Guang smashing the water jar. The official site presents them together with virtues such as benevolence, wisdom and filial piety. The TV program's official page (see below) also points out the carvings of the hall built in Tenpo 2.

**Registered Tangible Cultural Property buildings**: The shrine office and the Suigyo-do (a hall for water-purification practice) are listed in the Agency for Cultural Affairs' database as nationally Registered Tangible Cultural Properties (please check Ota Ward's or the Agency's guides for details such as construction dates). Suigyo means ascetic practice of purifying the body with water.

**Sacred grove of spirit monuments**: The official site mentions a grove called "Reijin no Mori" in the grounds, with monuments linked to the Ontake faith. Listing sites also mention subsidiary shrines in the grounds: Ichiyama Shrine (honoring Ichiyama Gyoja), Otori Shrine and Shichifuku Inari Shrine.

**Wolf-like guardian dogs and the spring**: Visitor accounts introduce komainu with a wolf-like look (the "Oinu-sama" of mountain faith) and a spring in the grounds called "Mori no Reijin-sui." They add to the mountain-faith atmosphere.

**On TV**: The shrine was featured on TV Tokyo's "Shutsubotsu! Ad-machic Tengoku" in its September 9, 2023 (Saturday) broadcast on the Yukigaya-Otsuka area, where the program's official page lists "Yukigaya Hachiman Shrine & Ontake Shrine" together at No. 3. The page describes it as a mountain-faith shrine dedicated to Mt. Ontake in Kiso, Nagano, and highlights the carvings of its hall, built in Tenpo 2. Please note that this was a 2023 broadcast and the information is described as current at the time.

## Visiting Tips

This is a neighborhood shrine in a residential area. A stone marker reading "御嶽神社" stands beside the torii gate, and the approach leads to the main hall. After paying your respects, you can take your time looking at the carvings, the spirit monuments and the Suigyo-do. Please take care not to disturb other worshippers or festivals and events.

**Benefits**: The official site and the sources we could check do not list specific benefits. The benefit tags shown on this page are only a rough guide based on the general character of the Ontake faith and Shinto shrines.

**Goshuin**: One visitor account says it is issued at the shrine office on the left side partway up the approach, and the shrine-listing site Hotokami gives the fee as ¥300. The amount and office hours could not be confirmed on the official site. Details can change, so it is safest to call the shrine office (03-3720-3333) before visiting.

**Access**: It is about a 3-minute walk from Ontakesan Station on the Tokyu Ikegami Line (per Ota Ward's cultural-property guide; the official site says "right next to the station"). The Ad-machic feature area was "Yukigaya-Otsuka," but this shrine's nearest station is Ontakesan.

**Hours and parking**: Opening hours for the grounds and whether parking is available are not stated in the sources we could check. Please contact the shrine office (03-3720-3333).

## Annual Festivals

The main events on the "Festivals and Events" page of the official site are as follows (dates are as listed there and may vary by year):

- **Ganjitsu-sai New Year's Day rite (January 1)**
- **Setsubun festival (February 3)**
- **Gogatsu-sai May festival (late May)**: The official site lists May 21, 2017 as an example, with a flea market, food stalls and other events.
- **Pilgrimage to Mt. Ontake in Kiso (early August)**: Presented as a pilgrimage organized by the Tokyo Mine Ichiyama-ko group.
- **Reitaisai annual grand festival (a Saturday and Sunday in mid-September)**: The official site lists September 16 (Sat) and 17 (Sun), 2017 as an example, with the ceremony, festival floats, children's and women's portable shrines on Saturday, and the main portable shrine on Sunday.
- **Shichi-go-san festival (November 15)**
- **Tori-no-Ichi market (Days of the Rooster in November)**
- **Great Purification (December 31)**: The official site says a rice-cake-pounding event is held on New Year's Eve night.

## History

Akasaka Hikawa Shrine stands in Akasaka 6-chome, Minato Ward, Tokyo (read "Akasaka Hikawa-jinja," per the furigana on the official site's Akasaka Hikawa Festival exhibition hall page). The official site names three enshrined deities: Susanoo no Mikoto, Kushiinada Hime no Mikoto and Oonamudi no Mikoto (also known as Ookuninushi no Mikoto). Its listed blessings are protection from misfortune, matchmaking, household safety and business prosperity.

According to the official history, the shrine was founded in 951 (Tenryaku 5). The priest Renrin, travelling in the eastern provinces, spent a night in Hitotsugi Village (near today's Akasaka 4-chome) and received a divine message in a dream, whereupon he built a shrine to Hikawa Myojin there. In 1066 (Jiryaku 2), when villagers suffering from drought prayed for rain, it is said to have begun raining at once.

In 1716 (Kyoho 1), when Yoshimune of the Kishu Tokugawa family became the 8th shogun, the shogunate's reverence for the shrine grew, since the Kishu domain's middle residence stood in Akasaka. The official site says that in 1729 (Kyoho 14) Yoshimune ordered a shrine building constructed at the present site, that the shrine was relocated from Hitotsugi Village on April 26, 1730, and that Yoshimune himself visited on the 28th. In the first year of Meiji it was designated a "Jun-chokusai-sha" (quasi-imperially-sponsored shrine), and today it is counted as one of the "Tokyo Ten Shrines."

## Highlights

According to the official site, the main shrine building survived the Ansei Great Earthquake, the Great Kanto Earthquake and the Great Tokyo Air Raids of 1945, preserving its appearance from when it was built. The Japanese page says it was designated a Tokyo Metropolitan tangible cultural property (building) in 1976 (the official site's English page also says "intangible cultural property," so the pages differ). Because it was built during the shogunate's austerity policy, the building is plain and simple, but the site explains that it also incorporates heavy-looking designs such as large cloud-shaped bracket sets and fan-arranged rafters.

The grounds hold many stone monuments, lanterns and guardian dogs bearing Edo-period dates, and the official site says there are as many as seven pairs of komainu. The pair beside the middle gate carries an inscription dated June of 1675 (Enpo 3), which the official site introduces as the oldest surviving stone komainu at a Tokyo shrine. The stone lantern in front of the middle gate was dedicated by the senior councillor Mizuno Tadayuki, who oversaw the relocation, to mark the building's completion, and is a Minato City registered tangible cultural property.

The great ginkgo tree is estimated to be 400 years old, with a trunk about 7.5 m around, and the official site calls it the shrine's symbol. It lost most of its trunk to burning in the 1945 air raids but turns a vivid yellow each year in late November. The grounds also hold the auxiliary shrine Shiawase Inari (which, according to the official site, was named by Katsu Kaishu on the occasion of the merger of shrines in Meiji 31), Saigyo Inari and Kujinsha, along with the Hocho-zuka (a mound for worn-out chefs' knives) and an exhibition hall displaying Edo-style festival floats for the Akasaka Hikawa Festival.

## Visiting Tips

The gates open at 6:00 AM and close at 5:30 PM, and the shrine office is open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM. Individual prayer services are by reservation (every 15 minutes from 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM), with the fee starting at ¥5,000 (from ¥10,000 for first-shrine-visit prayers and car purification), according to the official site. The official site says a matchmaking visit (En-musubi Mairi) is held once a month and that matchmaking amulets and other items are available (as of November 2026, the December 23 (Wed) 6:00 PM session is posted as full and closed).

The goshuin comes as regular stamps for the main shrine "Hikawa Jinja" and the auxiliary shrine "Shiawase Inari," plus seasonal goshuin (Hatsu-akane, Kinen-sai, Sakura Mairi, Hoshiai Mairi, annual festival, ginkgo and Niiname-sai) and a monthly "Kasane" design, according to the official site. Colors and designs change each year, and as of November 2026 the ginkgo goshuin is listed from early November and the "Kareno" Kasane for December. The fee and goshuin-only hours are not stated on the official site, so please check at the shrine office.

Several subway stations serve the shrine: Akasaka, Roppongi, Roppongi-itchome and Tameike-sanno, and the official access page lists the exit number for each (no walking times are given). There are 9 parking spaces on the grounds.

## Annual Festivals

The main events on the official site's annual festival list are as follows.

- **Saitan-sai (January 1)**: Prayers for the prosperity of the Imperial family, the peace of the nation and abundant harvests.
- **Setsubun-sai (February 3)**: Prayers to ward off misfortune, with bean-scattering.
- **Kinen-sai (February 17)**: Prayers for a good harvest in the coming autumn.
- **Shiawase Inari Annual Festival (April 15)**: Prayers for abundant harvests and business prosperity.
- **Nagoshi no Oharae-shiki (June 30)**: Passing through a ring of reeds to pray for good health through the rest of the year.
- **Annual festival (September 15)**: Described as the most important rite of the year.
- **Akasaka Hikawa Festival (mid-September each year)**: According to the official site, in the Edo period two portable shrines were escorted by 13 festival floats. In recent years a joint procession of the portable shrine and Edo-style floats has also been realized (in 2016, the first in about 100 years, per the official site).
- **Hocho-zuka-sai (early October)**: A rite unique to Akasaka, with its restaurant district, praying for the safety and business prosperity of chefs.
- **Niiname-sai (November 23)**: Offering new rice and giving thanks for abundant harvests.
- **Year-end Great Purification (December 31)**: Purifies the accumulated sins and impurities of the half year.

Dates and details can change from year to year, so check the shrine's annual festival page on the official site for the latest.

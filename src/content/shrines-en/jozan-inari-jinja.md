## History

Jozan Inari Shrine stands in Shiroyama Park, just north of Matsue Castle. Its principal deity is Ukanomitama no Kami, with Hondawake no Mikoto (the Hachiman deity) as a second enshrined deity, and it has long been known as "Gojonai Inari" (the Inari inside the castle). According to the Shimane Prefecture Association of Shinto Shrines, when Horio Yoshiharu built Matsue Castle he venerated the Wakamiya Hachiman Shrine already on the site as its guardian deity. Later, when Matsudaira Naomasa, a grandson of Tokugawa Ieyasu, entered Matsue as domain lord, an Inari shrine was founded, and the two came to be called the "Inari and Hachiman Shrines of the Castle," enshrined at the domain's expense as guardians of both Izumo and Oki provinces.

Accounts of the founding differ: some say Naomasa enshrined Inari in 1639, the year after entering Matsue in Kan'ei 15 (1638), while others give 1638. The Shimane Tourism Federation and others relate that Naomasa founded the shrine after a message in a dream. A well-known legend adds that a handsome youth calling himself "Inari Masaemon" appeared in the dream and vowed to protect the domain from fire if he were enshrined inside the castle — a tale that reflects the shrine's long-standing role as a protector against fire.

## Highlights

The shrine's best-known feature is its stone foxes, standing in all sizes and expressions. According to the Shimane Tourism Federation and other sources, there are more than 1,000 of them, and there are said to have been over 2,000 in the Meiji era (counts vary by source today). There is a saying that finding a stone fox holding a jewel makes a wish come true, so searching for one is part of the fun. According to the Matsue Tourism Association, the shrine's treasures also include wooden foxes attributed to the master craftsman Kobayashi Jodei.

The shrine is also known for its ties to Lafcadio Hearn (Koizumi Yakumo). Hearn spent about a year and three months in Matsue, wrote a "Kitsune" (fox) chapter in *Glimpses of Unfamiliar Japan*, and is said to have been so fond of the shrine's stone foxes that he often visited on his way to work. He especially liked a pair that stood in front of the Zuishinmon gate; because of their deterioration they are now preserved in a small shrine building beside the worship hall. (We could not confirm any record stating that he loved this shrine "most" of all.)

The shrine drew attention recently as a setting in the NHK morning drama *Bakebake*. The Matsue Film Commission and the Shimane Tourism Federation (Shimane Film Commission Network) announced that the drama was filmed here in September 2025 and that the shrine appeared in the episode broadcast on November 28. Many of the shrine's actual votive stone foxes are said to have been shown on screen.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds are free to enter, and there is no admission fee. The shrine office's hours could not be confirmed from official sources. Visitors report that goshuin are available from about 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM for ¥300, but the office can be unattended at lunchtime. If you are coming for amulets or a goshuin, it is safest to call (0852-21-1389) in advance.

From JR Matsue Station, the usual route is the Gurutto Matsue Lake Line bus to "Koizumi Yakumo Kinenkan-mae" or "Kencho-mae," followed by a walk (5–15 minutes depending on the source). It is about a 7-minute walk from Matsue Castle. Sources disagree on whether the shrine has its own parking lot, so if you want to be sure of a space, use one of the paid lots around Matsue Castle, such as Shiroyama West Parking. The grounds include stone steps, so comfortable shoes are a good idea.

## Annual Festivals

- **Reisai annual festival (September 15)**: The shrine's annual festival day.
- **Horan Enya (Jozan Inari Shrine Shikinen Shinko-sai; every 10 years, most recently May 2019, next expected in 2029)**: Counted among Japan's three great boat festivals and said to have begun in Keian 1 (1648), this is a once-in-a-decade procession in which the spirit of the shrine is carried by boat to Adakaya Shrine. It runs over nine days, with more than 100 decorated boats on the waters of Matsue. The Kaidenma oar dance, performed on the boats, is designated an Intangible Folk Cultural Property of Matsue City.

*Dates may change. Please check the latest information before visiting.*

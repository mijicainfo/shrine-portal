## History

Oyama Jinja enshrines Maeda Toshiie, celebrated as the founder of the mighty Kaga Domain — the "Kaga Hyakumangoku," one of the wealthiest domains of the Edo period — together with his principal wife, Lady Matsu. After Toshiie's death in 1599, his son Maeda Toshinaga wished to enshrine his father as a deity, but the political climate of the time, shaped in part by the domain's relationship with the Tokugawa shogunate, made it difficult to openly deify a former lord.

It was not until 1873, in the early Meiji era, that former Kaga Domain retainers came together to found a shrine dedicated to Toshiie on the former site of the Kanaya Goten mansion, within what had been the Kanaya-demaru bailey of Kanazawa Castle. The shrine took the name "Oyama" from an old alternate name for Kanazawa Castle itself, "Oyama-jo." Two years after its founding, in 1875, the shrine's now-famous Shinmon gate — later designated a National Important Cultural Property — was completed.

In 1879, the second and third lords of the domain, Maeda Toshinaga and Maeda Toshitsune, were added as secondary deities, and in 1998 Toshiie's wife, Lady Matsu (posthumous Buddhist name Hoshun-in), was enshrined alongside him. Because husband and wife are worshipped together, the shrine today is known for blessings of marital harmony, as well as for good fortune, career success, and victory in competition — fitting tributes to a man who rose from a low-ranking page to found one of Japan's greatest domains.

## The Shinmon Gate — A Unique Fusion of Japanese, Chinese, and Western Styles

No description of Oyama Jinja is complete without its Shinmon gate, completed in 1875 under the Kanazawa master carpenter Tsuda Kichinosuke. Its ground floor is built from a traditional Japanese triple-arch wooden framework; its middle floor takes on the appearance of a southern Chinese temple; and its top floor rises in the form of a Western-style dome — a one-of-a-kind fusion of Japanese, Chinese, and Western architectural traditions found nowhere else in the country. The exterior is faced with Tomuro stone, a material closely associated with the Kaga Domain, and the gate reaches roughly 25 meters in height including its lightning rod.

Its most striking feature is the colorful stained glass (giyaman) set into all four sides of the top floor. After dark, a lamp was once lit on this floor, and the illuminated gate reportedly served as a de facto lighthouse, guiding ships sailing along the Sea of Japan. Introducing bold Western engineering and design into a Shinto shrine building during Japan's civilization-and-enlightenment (bunmei kaika) era was a remarkably progressive undertaking for its time. Taller buildings in the area and reduced atmospheric clarity mean the light can no longer be seen from the sea, but the gate is illuminated from sunset until around 22:00, taking on an especially dreamlike appearance after nightfall. The lightning rod fixed to the roof's peak is said to be the oldest surviving example in Japan, and the gate itself was designated a National Important Cultural Property in 1950.

## The Shin'en — A "Garden of Musical Instruments"

After passing through the Shinmon gate and paying respects at the haiden, it's well worth continuing on to the Shin'en, the garden spread out behind the worship hall. This strolling pond garden dates to the late Edo and early Meiji periods, when it formed the grounds of the Kanaya Goten, a Maeda family mansion that once stood on this site; it was carried over into the shrine's precincts when Oyama Jinja was founded.

The bridges and islands dotting the pond bear names drawn from the instruments of gagaku, Japan's ancient court music — Koto Bridge, Yatsuhashi, Biwa Island, and Hosho Island among them — giving the garden its affectionate nickname, the "Garden of Musical Instruments." Designated a Kanazawa City Place of Scenic Beauty in 1954, it offers a quiet, seasonally changing stroll and stands alongside the Shinmon gate as one of the shrine's two signature attractions.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds can be visited at any hour, but the shrine office handling goshuin and prayer requests is open 9:00–17:00. The compact precincts — Shinmon gate, haiden, and Shin'en garden together — can be fully explored in thirty minutes to an hour, making for a rewarding stop that combines striking hybrid architecture with a tranquil Japanese garden. Right next door stands the Nezumita-mon gate and its bridge, reconstructed in 2020 after being lost for roughly 140 years; crossing it leads on foot to the Gyokusen'inmaru Garden inside Kanazawa Castle Park. The shrine's location also makes it easy to fold into a walking route that takes in Kenrokuen Garden and Kanazawa Castle Park.

## Annual Festivals

- **Otsuitachi Mairi (1st of every month)**: A monthly rite praying for peace in the coming month.
- **Tsukinamisai (27th of every month)**: A monthly service held on the 27th, the date of Maeda Toshiie's death each month.
- **Reitaisai Grand Festival (April 27)**: Held on the anniversary of Maeda Toshiie's death in 1599, this is Oyama Jinja's most important annual festival.
- **Oyama Matsuri (late May–early June)**: Held in cooperation with the Kanazawa Hyakumangoku Festival, which commemorates Maeda Toshiie's entry into Kanazawa Castle, featuring drum performances and other events.
- **New Year's Visit / Hatsumode (January 1–3)**: One of Kanazawa's most popular New Year destinations, drawing large crowds over the first three days of the year.

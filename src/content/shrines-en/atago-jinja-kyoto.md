## History

Atago Jinja stands on the summit of Mt. Atago (924 m) in Ukyo-ku, Kyoto. The official site describes it as the head shrine of the roughly 900 Atago shrines across Japan, long known as a shrine with divine power against fire. The official site does not list the enshrined deities; Japanese Wikipedia gives five: Izanami no Mikoto, Haniyamahime no Kami, Ame no Kumahito no Mikoto, Wakumusubi no Kami and Toyoukehime no Mikoto.

According to the official history, the "Atagoyama Shinto Engi" and the Hakuun-ji chronicle in the "Yamashiro Meisho-shi" record that in the Taiho era (701–704) En no Gyoja, regarded as the founder of Shugendo, and Taicho, known as the founder of Mt. Haku, received court permission and built a sanctuary on Asahi-ga-mine (Mt. Atago). In 781 (Tenno 1) Keishun is said to have revived it, and Wake no Kiyomaro built Hakuun-ji there, making it a center for protecting the nation as Atago Daigongen. It was renowned as a mountain training site of combined Shinto and Buddhism and, around the 9th century, was counted among the "Seven High Mountains" alongside Mt. Hiei and Mt. Hira.

In the era of combined Shinto and Buddhism, the main hall enshrined the Buddhist original form Shogun Jizo, and the inner sanctuary (today's Wakamiya) honored the tengu Taro-bo of Mt. Atago. With the Shinto-Buddhist separation order of the early Meiji years, Hakuun-ji was abolished and became Atago Jinja. Shogun Jizo was moved to Konzo-ji (Oharano, Nishikyo-ku, Kyoto) and is still enshrined there, the official site says. In September 2003 (Heisei 15) the 1,300th anniversary festival of the shrine's enshrinement was held. All of this is the shrine's own account.

## Highlights

The official site says the summit is about 10 degrees cooler than the city, so cherry trees bloom about a month later in spring. The summit is described as offering fine views of Kyoto city, Mt. Hiei and the Hira range.

The shrine's emblem is the "Hi no Yojin" (火迺要慎, "mind the fire") fire-prevention talisman. The official site explains it as a reminder from past generations to keep a sense of awe toward fire, which gives blessings but can also destroy everything. It also describes a household custom of throwing one shikimi leaf from Atago into the hearth fire first thing in the morning, praying for the household's safety and care with fire; today the shikimi is enshrined alongside the talisman. According to Japanese Wikipedia, there is also the "Atago no Mitsu-mairi": the belief that a child taken to visit by age three will never suffer a fire. The official site also lists a prayer service for three-year-olds (offering ¥3,000).

According to Japanese Wikipedia, the Kuro-mon (black gate), once called the Kyoguchi Somon, is one of the few remains from the Hakuun-ji era; the same source says the main hall was rebuilt in 1836 (Tenpo 7) and that the sword Kawatsutsumi no Tachi (Sasamaru) is a national Important Cultural Property (deposited at the Kyoto National Museum). The official site also lists goshuin for Wakamiya, Okumiya, Shogun Jizo and Mitsuhide-ko. Japanese Wikipedia further says that in May 1582 (Tensho 10), just before the Honno-ji Incident, Akechi Mitsuhide is said to have stayed in seclusion at Atago and held a renga poetry session (Atago Hyakuin).

## Visiting Tips

There is no road for cars; you walk up from a trailhead. The official site recommends the main approach from Kiyotaki, which has an easy-to-find start and a wide trail, at about 2–3 hours on foot. From Mizuo it is about 2 hours, and from Shikimigahara about 1 hour 30 minutes. The official site states that the trail via Kubinashi Jizo is not an official approach and has no connection to the shrine.

Prayer services are accepted on request every day from 9:00 AM to about 4:00 PM, with offerings of ¥5,000, ¥7,000, ¥10,000 or ¥20,000 and up at your discretion (¥3,000 for the three-year-old service only). For those who cannot climb, talismans and charms can be mailed, and there is also the "Atagokai" membership (¥3,500 the first year, ¥3,000 from the second) under which monthly prayers are offered on the mountain on your behalf. The main-shrine goshuin is ¥500, and a limited gold boar goshuin exists.

Kyoto Buses run to Kiyotaki from Kyoto Station, Keihan Sanjo Station and the Arashiyama area. The only car park is the paid Sakuraya Parking at Kiyotaki; the road is narrow and street parking is not possible. As it is a mountain trail, plan for cold-weather and rain gear and for getting back down before sunset.

## Annual Festivals

- **Monthly festival (the 1st, 15th and 23rd of every month)**: The official site says it is held three times a month.
- **Chinka-sai (April 24)**: The official site's 2026 schedule lists the Chinka-sai and the Chinka-ko-sai.
- **Senichi-mairi (night of July 31 to early morning of August 1)**: Also called Senichi Tsuyasai, it is said to bring 1,000 days of fire-prevention blessings. According to the official site, tens of thousands visit each year and the roughly 4 km main approach from Kiyotaki to the summit is lit (in 2024 it was held in the daytime instead; it varies by year, so check the official site).
- **Annual festival (September 28)**: Listed in the official 2026 schedule as "Reisai / Shikinen Zotai Commemorative Grand Festival."
- **Hitaki-sai, Hiusu-sai, autumn festival / Fuigo festival (November)**: In the official 2026 schedule, November 8 is the Hitaki-sai, the 9th the Hiusu-sai, and the 23rd the Autumn Festival and Fuigo-sai.
- **Oharae and Joya-sai (December 31)**: Listed as the year-end festival.

According to the official site, the present hall was renovated in 1929 (Showa 4), and a periodic reconstruction project, including a new roof for the main hall, has been under way to coincide with the grand festival of the 60-year Hinoe-uma year in 2026 (Reiwa 8). Dates change from year to year, so check the "News" section of the official site for the latest.

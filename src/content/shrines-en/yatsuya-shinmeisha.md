## History

Yatsuya Shinmeisha stands near Kyowa Station on the JR Tokaido Line in Kyowa-cho, Obu, Aichi. According to the Aichi tourism site "Aichi Now," the main deity is Amaterasu Omikami, and it is a Shinmei shrine whose head shrine is the Naiku of Ise Jingu. It is introduced as founded in the Edo period and rebuilt in the Taisho era to become the present worship hall. The Obu City Tourism Association page says it was renewed in June 2022 (Reiwa 4).

No founding year, origin of the name, or other detailed history could be found among the confirmed sources, so this page's history is limited to the short account above. Gaps have not been filled with guesses.

What the shrine is known for is prayer before contests. "Aichi Now" calls Kyowa-cho in Obu a town famous as a "town of gold medals," and says that the town is home to Shigakkan University, the alma mater and training base of athletes such as <a href="https://yoshidasaori.jp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Saori Yoshida</a> (<a href="https://x.com/sao_sao53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">X</a> / <a href="https://www.instagram.com/saori___yoshida/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Instagram</a>) and Kaori and Chiharu Icho, and that the athletes are also known to visit the shrine to pray for victory. The Obu City Tourism Association page says that, besides Saori Yoshida, Risako Kawai (<a href="https://x.com/risako_kawai" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">X</a> / <a href="https://www.instagram.com/risako_kawai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Instagram</a>) and Yukako Kawai, among others, have visited. This is based on what those tourism pages say, and details such as when each person visited could not be confirmed.

## Highlights

"Aichi Now" describes the shrine as "a small, old-fashioned shrine with a slide and a horizontal bar." It says offering banners and votive plaques (ema) bearing the names of famous Olympic athletes hang in the grounds (whose names is not stated).

Besides the main shrine, the grounds hold the auxiliary shrines Tsushima Shrine, Yamanokami Shrine and Konpira-sha, and there is another auxiliary shrine, Toko-ji, outside the grounds. According to the same site, you can pray to a variety of gods, including gods of the sea, of business, of fire prevention and of Izumo. Consider visiting them together with the main deity, Amaterasu Omikami.

## Visiting Tips

"Aichi Now" says many people visit this god of winning before exams, job hunting and matches. It makes sense as a place to stop by when you want to settle your mind before a big test or tournament.

Visiting hours, and whether goshuin are available and how they are issued, are not stated on the tourism pages checked. The Obu City Tourism Association page lists a contact for the shrine's priest, so if you are visiting for a goshuin or similar, it is safest to check in advance (callers are asked to say they are asking about "Yatsuya Shinmeisha in Obu City").

It is about a 3-minute walk from Kyowa Station on the JR Tokaido Line (per Aichi Now; one travel article gives about 5 minutes). No dedicated parking could be confirmed, so going by train is the safer choice. A toilet is listed.

## Annual Festivals

The Aichi Now and Obu City Tourism Association pages checked do not give dates for this shrine's annual festival or other yearly events, so none are listed here. Please check with the contact listed on the Obu City Tourism Association page for whether festivals are held and when.

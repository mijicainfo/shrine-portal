## History

Genkuro Inari Jinja is an Inari shrine in Tosenji-cho, Yamatokoriyama, Nara. Its name is read "Genkuro Inari Jinja," and the official site names Ukanomitama no Kami (also called Uke-mochi no Kami) and Genkuro Inari Daimyojin as the enshrined deities. Its blessings are given as household safety, business prosperity and good fortune, and it is known for its fox statues and fox legends.

According to the "History" page of the official site, Yamato Dainagon Toyotomi Hidenaga moved the shrine to this place from the banks of the Yoshino River when he built Koriyama Castle, and enshrined it as the castle's guardian (no year is given for the move). The name "Genkuro" is said to come from an old tradition that Minamoto no Yoshitsune (Kuro Hogan) was given the name "Genkuro" when he headed north to Oshu. The Yamatokoriyama Tourism Association also tells the story that when Yoshitsune fled to Yoshino, a white fox escorted Shizuka to him and the name "Genkuro" was given in thanks. The official site adds that the origin of the name rests on old verses and traditions and has not been confirmed as historical fact.

The "Legends" page of the official site tells how in 1585 (Tensho 13) Hidenaga entered Koriyama Castle and took the monk Hoyo Shonin as his teacher, and a white fox calling itself Genkuro appeared before the monk and said it would become a guardian. Hidenaga is also said to have had a small shrine built in the grounds of Tosenji temple to enshrine the fox. The same page also tells of 1615 (Genna 1), around the Summer Siege of Osaka, when the temple's head priest prayed to the fox and a sudden heavy rain saved the castle town from a great fire. These are things that are "said to" have happened, and are not presented as facts backed by historical records.

To mark the broadcast of the 2026 NHK Taiga drama "Toyotomi Kyodai!," the official site announces a limited-edition goshuin, "Hidenaga-san."

## Highlights

The shrine is known for its red torii gate and the fox statues dotted around the grounds. Past the torii, fox statues wearing red bibs stand before the worship hall, a small Inari shrine tucked into a quiet residential area. Although it sits in town, the grounds are surrounded by trees.

The official site introduces several legends. These include the story of a small fox that, with the help of the fox Genkuro, slew a giant serpent, and whose tail was found to hold a sword that was named "Ko-gitsune-maru" and dedicated to Isonokami Shrine, as well as the tale of a woman who bought a cotton hood at a hat shop. Many fox stories remain here.

The official site also mentions a folk belief that touching the jewel brings luck with money and touching the scroll brings wisdom.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds are open to visit freely, and a Keihan Electric Railway guide page gives the shrine office as 10:00 AM–4:00 PM, closed Wednesdays (except holidays) (the official site does not say). If you want amulets or a goshuin, it is safest to allow plenty of time.

According to the official site, goshuin include "Genkuro Inari Daimyojin" at 500 en, a "paper-cut" goshuin at 1,300 en, and the 2026 limited-edition "Hidenaga-san" at 1,000 en ("en" is written with the character 縁 on the official site). Mail orders are accepted only when visiting is difficult, and only by registered cash mail. The official site also asks people to reconsider buying shrine items that are being resold online at high prices.

It is about a 10-minute walk from Koriyama Station on the Kintetsu Kashihara Line or the JR Yamatoji Line. The official site does not mention parking.

## Annual Festivals

These dates follow the official site's 2026 (Reiwa 8) event schedule.

- **Monthly service (1st and 15th of each month)**: A regular monthly ceremony.
- **New Year's Day service (January 1, 10:00 AM)**: A ceremony to celebrate the new year.
- **Annual festival and Byakko Togyo (March 29, 10:00 AM / 1:30 PM)**: The shrine's main yearly festival, with the white-fox procession in the afternoon of the same day. According to the Yamatokoriyama Tourism Association, it is an event held around the castle festival in which children dressed in white with fox masks parade through the town.
- **Summer purification rite (June 30, 3:00 PM)**: A rite to purify the sins and impurities of the first half of the year.
- **Hitaki fire festival (November 8, 2:00 PM)**: A ceremony of thanks for fire.
- **Year-end purification rite and New Year's Eve service (December 31, from 7:00 PM)**: A closing ceremony for the year.

Dates may change from year to year. Check the "Events and Festivals" page on the official site for the latest.

## History

Shakujii Hikawa Shrine stands next to Shakujii Park in Shakujiidai, Nerima, Tokyo. Its shrine name is "Hikawa Shrine" (氷川神社); the history page of the official site calls it "Shakujii Hikawa Shrine" and introduces it as the chief tutelary shrine of Shakujii-go (石神井郷), the Shakujii district. The reading of the name with the place name, "Shakujii Hikawa-jinja," is taken from the Japanese Wikipedia. It is a different shrine from the similarly named Shakujii Shrine (石神井神社). The main deity is Susanoo no Mikoto, enshrined together with Inadahime no Mikoto and Onamuchi no Mikoto. Even today it is commonly and affectionately known as "Shakujii no Ohikawa-sama."

According to shrine tradition, it was founded in the Oei era (1394–1428) of the Muromachi period. The Toshima clan, then powerful in this area, is said to have first enshrined it inside Shakujii Castle as the castle's guardian deity, receiving a divided spirit from Hikawa Shrine in Omiya, the first-ranked shrine (ichinomiya) of Musashi Province. According to the shrine's history, the Toshima were attacked by Ota Dokan, Shakujii Castle fell in 1477 (Bunmei 9), and the lord of the castle, Toshima Yasutsune, and his daughter Princess Teru (照姫) drowned themselves in the pond. The tragedy has been handed down as "the legend of the golden saddle and Princess Teru." Nerima City describes its Teruhime Festival as a festival based on the local legend of Yasutsune and Princess Teru.

The villagers remained devoted to the shrine after the castle fell, and according to the official site it is the tutelary shrine of an area that includes present-day Shakujiidai, Shakujii-machi, Kami-Shakujii, Shimo-Shakujii and Tateno-cho. It long received the devotion of the five former villages of Yahara, Tanaka, Kami-Shakujii, Shimo-Shakujii and Seki. It was ranked as a village shrine in November of Meiji 5 and as a district shrine in April of Meiji 7 (the shrine ranking system was abolished after the war).

## Highlights

According to the official site, both the main hall and the worship hall are in the nagare-zukuri style. The worship hall was completed in September of Meiji 34. The main hall had been presumed to date from the Bunsei era, but because it had aged badly it was renovated, together with the shrine office and the heiden (offering hall), in a precinct improvement project that began in Heisei 1. The new shrine buildings were completed in Heisei 4, and in September of that year the ceremony of transferring the deity to the main hall was held for the first time in about 150 years. The kagura stage, to the right as you face the shrine hall, was built in Showa 12, and the official site describes its graceful form as outstanding even among the kagura stages of Tokyo.

The pair of stone lanterns offered by descendants of the Toshima clan is a Nerima City designated cultural property. The city's description gives their total height as 154 centimeters, and the shaft of the right-hand lantern is inscribed with a date in December of Genroku 12 (1699) and the name of the donor, Toshima Yasumitsu (豊嶋泰盈). The donor inscription on the left-hand lantern has worn away, but it is said to have been given by Yasumitsu's son Yasutaka (泰音). The right-hand lantern has lost its light chamber. The water basin is also a city-registered cultural property. It is inscribed "石神井郷 鎮守社 御手水鉢" (water basin of the tutelary shrine of Shakujii-go) on the front and with the date Kyoho 12 (1727) on the back, and the city describes it as the oldest water basin at any shrine in the ward.

The grounds also have a small sacred rice field about the size of a flower bed. According to the official site, the shrine grows ancient rice there, including black rice and "Tsushima red rice" obtained from the sacred rice of 多久頭魂神社, a shrine in Tsushima, and offers it to the deities at Niiname-sai. Old trees grow thickly across the grounds of more than 2,000 tsubo, where you can sense something of the old Musashino landscape.

## Visiting Tips

The official site introduces Susanoo no Mikoto as a deity of protection from misfortune, also known as a deity of good relationships. Prayer services in the shrine hall are accepted every day unless there is a special shrine event, and no booking is needed. They start at 10:00, 11:00, 13:00, 14:00 and 15:00, and registration closes 15 minutes before each start. A 12:00 session is added in November, and January times also differ. The offering is ¥5,000 or more per prayer. As of November 2026, the official site says reservations for the Reiwa 9 New Year prayers for the start of the work year (お正月の仕事始め新年祈祷) are accepted by phone or at the shrine office from 9:00 on November 1.

For a small purification there is "tama-harai." You breathe into an unglazed clay ball to transfer impurities to it, then throw it against a purification stone to break it; the offering is ¥500. Visiting hours and goshuin are not covered on the official site, so check at the shrine office if you need them.

If you take the bus from Shakujii-koen Station, get off at "JA Tokyo Aoba," pass Dojo-ji Temple, and bear right at the three-way junction with the traffic light at Sanpo-ji Temple. At the top of the slope, the intersection with the road surface painted vermilion marks the shrine's approach (directions from the official site). Cars can drive straight up the approach. There are 25 parking spaces, but the official site's Shichi-Go-San guide says that on November weekends it is busiest from about 11:00 to 13:00, when the wait for a space can be around an hour if long.

## Annual Festivals

The following is based on the official site.

- **Saitan-sai (January 1)**: Held from midnight on New Year's Day, with a New Year miko dance offered.
- **Setsubun-sai and Kinen-sai (February)**: The public can also attend Setsubun-sai. Kinen-sai prays for a good harvest that year.
- **井のいち (May)**: A craft market run mainly by shops from Shakujii and the surrounding area, with music performed on the kagura stage (held rain or shine).
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (July 1)**: A chinowa ring of grass is set up in the grounds. Check the official site or phone to find out whether there will be a rite in which all attendees pass through the ring.
- **Chagauma Tanabata (August)**: A "month-late" Tanabata festival. After the 4:00 PM rite, events begin at 5:00 PM, including a workshop on making horses from kaya grass.
- **Annual festival (third Sunday of October)**: The largest festival of the year. The Urayasu-no-Mai dance is offered at the eve festival and the Yukyu-no-Mai on the day itself, and sato-kagura and Shakujii-bayashi (a Nerima City registered cultural property) liven up the grounds.
- **Niiname-sai (November 23)**: The Asahi-mai dance is offered, with threshing and hulling of rice from the sacred field, direct sales of vegetables by farmers, and tasting of doburoku (unrefined sake).
- **Toshikoshi no Oharae (New Year's Eve)**: Hand in your hitogata (paper figures) during December, with the 31st as the guideline. The rite itself is performed by the priests alone in the evening.
- **Antique market (fourth Sunday of every month)**: Held in the shrine grounds.

Dates and contents can change, so check the official site for the latest information.

According to Nerima City, earthworks and a dry moat of the inner enclosure still remain.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sanpoji%20Pond%2C%20Shakujii%20Park%2C%20Nerima%2C%20Tokyo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Sanpoji Pond (Shakujii Park)</strong></a>: The "Marsh Plant Community of Sanpoji Pond" was designated a national Natural Monument in 1935. Shakujii Park is also a venue of the Teruhime Festival, usually held on the Sunday of the fourth week of April.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sanpoji%20Temple%2C%20Shakujiidai%2C%20Nerima%2C%20Tokyo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Sanpo-ji Temple</strong></a>: A temple on the way to the shrine. Its main gate and temple bell are Nerima City designated cultural properties, and a tradition attached to the gate says Tokugawa Iemitsu stopped by while out hunting.

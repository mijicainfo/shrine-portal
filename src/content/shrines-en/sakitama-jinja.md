## History

Sakitama Jinja (前玉神社) stands in Sakitama, Gyoda, Saitama Prefecture, beside Sakitama Kofun Park. According to the official site, the shrine buildings sit atop an ancient burial mound called Sengen-zuka, about 8.7 m high and about 92 m around. The website of the Saitama Prefectural Sakitama Museum likewise says the main buildings stand on Sengen-zuka Kofun. The official history page says the shrine is built "directly on top of" the Sakitama kofun group, which is worded differently from the top page's "adjoining the kofun group."

The official page on the deities says the shrine enshrines two deities of the Izumo lineage who appear in the Kojiki, Sakitamahime-no-mikoto and Sakitamahiko-no-mikoto (spellings such as Sakitamahime and Sakitamahiko-no-kami also vary within the official site). Because a goddess and a god are enshrined together, many visitors pray for love, marital harmony and good relationships. The official site also says the shrine was once written "Saiwai-no-mitama Jinja."

The year of founding is unknown. The official site says one theory holds that deities were first enshrined here in the Kofun period, the late 400s to early 500s, and that the shrine faces the kofun group as a relic of this. Gyoda City's cultural-property page says the shrine is an ancient one listed in the Engishiki Jinmyocho as "Sakitama Jinja, two seats, minor," and that it was probably originally a land deity worshipped by the powerful clan that built the Saitama kofun group.

The official history says the district name "Sakitama-no-kori," given in the 700s after this shrine, later changed in its characters to "Saitama-gun" and led to today's Saitama Prefecture, adding that the shrine "is said to be the origin of the name of Saitama Prefecture."

For later times, Gyoda City's page cites the Shinpen Musashi Fudokiki-ko, which says that during the time of the Narita clan the Sengen Shrine inside Oshi Castle was invited here. The shrine was called Sengen Shrine in the Edo period but returned to the name Sakitama Jinja in the Meiji era. The official site also says the Sengen Shrine was invited from inside Oshi Castle in the early modern period.

## Highlights

At the foot of the stairs stand a pair of stone lanterns offered on October 15, 1697 (Genroku 10) by the parishioners of Saitama Village. They are designated cultural properties of Gyoda City, with Manyoshu poems about "小埼沼" and "Sakitama-no-tsu" carved in Manyogana on their shafts, and the city describes them as among the oldest in Japan as monuments inscribed with Manyoshu poems.

The shrine's stone torii is a tangible cultural property designated by Gyoda City (designated March 25, 1999). The city says it is 4.5 m tall overall and 5.8 m wide at its widest, made of Shirakawa stone, and that the inscription on a pillar shows it was erected in November of 1676 (Enpo 4) by retainers of Abe Masayoshi, lord of Oshi Castle, and parishioners.

The sub-shrines are varied. The Sengen Shrine, whose upper shrine is on the mound and lower shrine on its slope, enshrines Konohanasakuya-hime and is described on the official site as a deity of child rearing, safe childbirth and industry. Each July, "Hatsuyama" is a traditional event in which babies born during the past year visit and have a vermilion seal stamped on the forehead to pray for good health. There are also the Meiji Shrine, into which shrines of the Saitama district were merged in the Meiji era (16 deities, according to the official site), the Tenjin Shrine for Sugawara no Michizane, and Ebisu, a god of business prosperity.

According to Gyoda City, the flower water basins (hana-chozu) that began at Gyoda Hachiman Shrine in April 2020 spread to Sakitama Jinja too, and are now displayed at about 100 places in the city.

## Visiting Tips

Prayer services are by reservation, accepted on a rolling basis from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM, and take about 15 minutes (official site). As a guide, the tamagushi offering is from ¥5,000 for individuals, ¥10,000 for companies and ¥7,000 for traffic-safety prayers. Weekends and holidays fill up, so the shrine suggests weekdays if possible.

For goshuin, the official site announces a limited-edition "cat series" around the 22nd of each month. The November 2026 edition runs for five days, Friday, November 20 to Tuesday, November 24, 9:00 AM–4:00 PM (closed 12:00–1:00 PM), at ¥500 (as of November 2026). To avoid crowding every goshuin is dated the 22nd. The official site does not describe a regular goshuin. Distribution may be cancelled depending on social conditions.

Visiting hours for the grounds are not stated on the official site, and its access page is a map only; the shrine adjoins Sakitama Kofun Park. For parking, a notice for the 2026 Tanabata event says to use the dedicated parking lot reached from Prefectural Route 77 (Kofun-dori), with no number of spaces or fees given, and the official site asks visitors to be considerate of neighbors.

## Annual Festivals

Main events from the official site's seasonal calendar (dates may change from year to year):

- **Gantan-sai, New Year's Day (January 1)**: A rite marking the start of the new year.
- **Kangaku-sai (early April)**: A rite praying for success in studies.
- **Annual festival (April 15)**: Described in the official calendar as the most important festival for the shrine.
- **Hatsuyama (July 1–15)**: According to the official site, held on July 1, 14 and 15 and the second Sunday. A traditional event for babies born in the past year, who have a vermilion seal stamped on the forehead to pray for good health (the Sengen Shrine page says the foreheads of children from newborns to age three).
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (July 30)**: A rite in which the sins and impurities of the past half year are transferred to paper figures and sent down a river.
- **Tanabata-no-gi (early August)**: In 2026, on Saturday, August 1, a Kikkoden-style Tanabata display was lit up at the kagura hall, with gagaku and folktale offerings (per the official notice).
- **Toro-sai (October 15)**: Said to be the annual festival of the Meiji Shrine.
- **Niiname-sai (late October)**, **Shichi-Go-San (late October to November)**, **Hoshi-matsuri (winter solstice, December)**, and **Oharae and Toshikoshi rite (December 31)**.
- **Limited-edition goshuin (around the 22nd each month)**: The calendar lists a special edition on February 22 and a Sakitama Fire Festival edition in early May.
- **Sakitama Fire Festival (May)**: Held at Sakitama Kofun Park. According to Gyoda City, fire is made at the Sengen Shrine (Sakitama Jinja) and used in the festival. The 41st was held on Monday, May 4, 2026.

## History

Aguchi Shrine (read "Aguchi-jinja") stands in Kainocho-higashi, Sakai-ku, Sakai, and is affectionately known as "Otera-san" (大寺さん). The official site names three enshrined deities: Shiotsuchi-no-Oji-no-Kami (formerly the deity of Aguchi village), Susanoo-no-Kami (formerly the deity of 木戸 village) and Ikutama-no-Kami (formerly the deity of 原 village). Shiotsuchi-no-Oji is introduced as the deity of seafaring and salt who is also revered for safe childbirth, Susanoo as the deity who wards off epidemics and misfortune and brings good fortune, and Ikutama as the deity who is the foundation of the land's development and growth (the readings of the deities' names follow the Sakai Tourism Guide).

According to the history on the official site, Empress Jingu landed from the Three Han (the Korean Peninsula) at Ishizu beach, and while praying for victory at 塩穴松原 to put down the rebellion of Prince Oshikuma, an old fisherman presented her with a red sea bream (赤目魚). The Empress rejoiced at this as an auspicious sign and ordered that the spirit of Shiotsuchi-no-Oji be enshrined, which is said to be how the shrine was founded. The official page on prayers for safe childbirth also records a tradition that the name "Aguchi" (開口, "opening the mouth") comes from the Empress opening her mouth for the first time to eat after landing.

In 746, Gyoki is said to have built the temple Nenbutsu-ji, beginning the blending of Shinto and Buddhism, and Sakai City explains "Otera" as the popular name of Nenbutsu-ji, formerly the shrine's temple. According to the official chronology, Kukai built a treasure pagoda in 806, the shrine was listed in the Engishiki in 927, and in 1113 the shrines of the three villages of Aguchi, 木戸 and 原 were merged, after which it was also called 三村宮 or 三村明神. In 1586 it received a vermilion-seal document granting 80 koku from Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and in 1902 it became a prefectural shrine. All the buildings in the grounds burned down from war damage on July 10, 1945, and the main hall and worship hall were rebuilt in 1964. The Sakai Film Office, in its introduction of "filming locations and related sites" for the Taiga drama 豊臣兄弟！ (dated March 22, 2026), presents Aguchi Shrine as an old shrine where wealthy merchants of Sakai gathered.

## Highlights

According to Sakai City's cultural property pages, the "Otera Engi" (大寺縁起) owned by the shrine is a set of three illustrated scrolls on its origins, produced in 1690, with paintings by Tosa Mitsuoki. Together with the Kamakura-period "tanto signed Yoshimitsu" and "Emperor Fushimi's autograph poem collection (one hundred winter poems)," it is designated an Important Cultural Property. However, the scrolls and the poem collection are listed as deposited with the Osaka City Museum of Fine Arts, and the tanto with Osaka Castle Museum, so these are not treasures you can see in the grounds at any time. The Aguchi Shrine documents, a Tangible Cultural Property designated by Osaka Prefecture, include a document from 1535 that bears names such as that of Sen no Rikyu.

The Yakushi-sha in the grounds enshrines the Yakushi Nyorai that was the principal image of Nenbutsu-ji, the shrine's former temple. The official site introduces the statue as one that "is said to be the work of Gyoki and counts among the oldest in the city," and says it escaped the Meiji-era movement to abolish Buddhism and wartime destruction. It is not normally on view, but it is unveiled for the Setsubun festival on February 3, when a goma fire rite is held. The water pavilion (temizuya) survived the 1945 war damage, and its water basin was donated by a resident of 大町浜 in the third month of 1710.

An old well near the west gate, 金龍井, has a legend that clear water gushed out when a monk of the temple 海会寺 dug where an old man (actually a dragon) had told him to. The grounds also have Sanbo Kojin Shrine (also called Kamado Shrine), beloved as the deity of the kitchen; 豊竹稲荷神社, an Inari shrine; 白一龍神社, which the official site introduces as a deity of teeth; a fan mound (扇塚) of the deity of the performing arts; a poem monument to Yosano Akiko; and the guardian lion-dogs at the west gate, donated in 1863. The Mugean tea house (無礙庵) burned in 1945 and was rebuilt in 1989; normally only its exterior can be viewed.

## Visiting Tips

There is no time limit if you are only visiting to worship. Prayer services are accepted at the shrine office at any time from 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM, with no booking needed. Amulets are available 9:00 AM–5:00 PM. Note that the Sakai Tourism Guide lists business hours of 9:00 AM–5:15 PM (different at New Year's, major festivals, Setsubun and so on), which differs from the official site, so this page gives the official site's hours. The prayer for safe childbirth is associated with the Day of the Dog in the fifth month of pregnancy, but it is also accepted every day, not only on Dog days.

By train, the Oshoji and Shukuin stops on the Hankai Line are close, about a 5-minute walk, and it is also about a 10-minute walk from Sakai Station on the Nankai Main Line. There is parking, but on days with rites or festivals, public transport is the safer choice.

## Annual Festivals

Here are the main events from the annual calendar on the official site (as of November 2026).

- **Saitan-sai (January 1)**: New Year's visits begin at midnight, and the ceremony is held from 2:00 AM.
- **Setsubun Festival (February 3)**: Includes bean throwing and the burning of goma sticks. On February 8, a needle memorial service (hari kuyo) is held for worn-out needles.
- **Hatsu-uma Festival (late March)**: Held on a Day of the Horse between March 20 and 31, with rice-cake scattering after the ceremony (March 21 in 2026).
- **Satsuki Festival (May 5)**: A festival praying for children's healthy growth, with stalls and dedicatory performances.
- **Summer Great Purification (June 30)**: Includes passing through the chinowa grass ring. In 2026, a wind-chime approach on the west approach was also announced.
- **Hassaku Festival (September; annual grand festival on the 12th)**: Futon-daiko drum floats, which the shrine describes as having the oldest history in Sakai, enter the shrine, and a procession goes to the 芦原 otabisho (resting place). According to the official site, a record of the annual events of 1375 mentions a goryo-e rite on the first day of the eighth month.
- **Fan Festival (early November)**: Worn-out fans are given a memorial service in front of the fan mound, with prayers for progress in the arts.
- **Kamado Festival (November 28)**: The annual festival of Sanbo Kojin, called the Kojin of Sakai. A free serving of oden after the ceremony is announced, but there have been years when it was not held, so check this year's details with the shrine office.

## Nearby Food & Sightseeing

- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Yamanokuchi%20Shopping%20Street%20Sakai%20Osaka" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Yamanokuchi Shopping Street</strong></a>: The shopping street that the shrine's west gate (torii) faces. According to the official site, this torii was donated by the forebears of 大甲濱, a group that carries on a futon-daiko tradition.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sakai%20Plaza%20of%20Rikyu%20and%20Akiko%20Sakai%20Osaka" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Sakai Plaza of Rikyu and Akiko</strong></a>: A museum in Shukuin-cho Nishi, Sakai-ku, themed on Sen no Rikyu and Yosano Akiko, both connected with Sakai. It also offers a tea experience in a tea room.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Nanshuji%20Temple%20Sakai%20Osaka" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Nanshuji Temple</strong></a>: A Zen temple in 南旅篭町東, Sakai-ku, which according to the Sakai Tourism Guide has, among other things, a dry landscape garden designated a national Place of Scenic Beauty. Aguchi Shrine's official site says that the temple 海会寺, once near the west gate of Otera, later moved into the grounds of Nanshuji. There is an admission fee.

## History

Koyasu Shrine (Koyasu Jinja) stands in Itabashi 2-chome, Itabashi Ward, Tokyo. Its deity is Konohanasakuya-hime no Mikoto, and it was the guardian shrine of the former village of Kanaikubo. Its founding date is unknown, but according to the Itabashi Ward Board of Education sign, the grounds appear in a 1674 (Enpo 2) land register, so the shrine is thought to be older than that. Tradition holds that a branch spirit of Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha (Fuji Sengen Shrine) was enshrined here.

In the Edo period it was called "Koyasu-gu" or "Koyasu Myojin" (子安), and its administering temple was the Shingon temple Fukusho-ji. In the Anei (1772–1781) and Kansei (1789–1801) eras it is said to have been known for its protective talismans and to have drawn many worshippers as a deity of safe childbirth and child-rearing. With the separation of Shinto and Buddhism in the early Meiji period it separated from Fukusho-ji and took the name "Koyasu Jinja" (子易神社). Fukusho-ji was later abolished, and the Kannon image that belonged to the temple is now enshrined in the Koyasu Kannon hall on the grounds.

## Highlights

In the Shimo-Itabashi episode of TV Tokyo's "Shutsubotsu! Ad-machic Tengoku" broadcast on October 7, 2023, Koyasu Shrine was introduced together with Ikebukuro Hikawa Shrine as No. 2 (per the program's official page, where the heading is a single combined entry, and the information dates from 2023). The page says the shrine is said to have been founded in the Edo period and that the sacred kaya (Japanese nutmeg-yew) tree on the grounds is said to be over 600 years old. By contrast, the Itabashi Ward Board of Education sign gives the founding date as unknown.

One visitor report notes that the raised ground holding the shrine buildings is surrounded by large rocks, which, together with the shrine's Fuji Sengen origins, is reminiscent of a Fuji-zuka (a miniature Mt. Fuji mound). Guardian dogs (komainu) sit on rocks on either side of the stone steps of the approach.

Other things to see around the grounds and torii:

- **Koyasu Kannon (Koyasu Kannon hall)**: said to have been moved to the shrine from Fukusho-ji when the temple was abolished. The shrine (zushi) is reported to carry an inscription dating its consecration to 1765 (Meiwa 2).
- **Munetsuki Jizo (Migawari Jizo)**: beside the torii; said to have been erected in 1695 (Genroku 8). The base is inscribed with place names, and it is said to have also served as a road marker.
- **Subsidiary shrines**: an Inari shrine and a Suwa shrine (the Inari shrine is reported to have been moved from another shrine and merged here in 1907).

## Visiting Tips

This is a shrine long venerated for safe childbirth and child-rearing, and many visitors come to pray for a safe birth or a child's growth. However, the sources I could check say nothing about how safe-childbirth prayer services are handled or about belly bands (hara-obi) and Dog Day visits. If you would like a prayer service, it is safest to ask the shrine office (03-3963-0383) beforehand.

**Goshuin**: Available at the shrine office to the left of the worship hall, behind the kagura hall. According to a visitor report, only pre-written sheets are offered, for ¥500 (an older listing says ¥300). One account says you press a buzzer at the office. Official office hours could not be confirmed; one guide gives visiting hours of 9:00 AM–5:00 PM.

**Access**: Itabashi-kuyakushomae Station (Toei Mita Line), and Shimo-Itabashi and Oyama stations (Tobu Tojo Line), are each roughly 5–10 minutes on foot (times vary between sources). One guide gives about 12 minutes on foot from Itabashi Station (JR Saikyo Line). Sources disagree on whether there is parking, so if you are coming by car, check with the shrine office first.

## Annual Festivals

No official list of annual events could be confirmed, so the following comes from visitor reports and guides. Dates can change from year to year, so please confirm details with the shrine office.

- **Grand Festival (third Saturday and Sunday of September)**: On Sunday evening, neighborhood mikoshi are said to enter the shrine together.
- **Munetsuki Jizo memorial service (July 24)**
- **Koyasu Kannon memorial service (October 16)**

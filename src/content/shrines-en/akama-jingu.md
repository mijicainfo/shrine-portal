## History

Akama Jingu stands in the Amidaji-cho district of Shimonoseki, Yamaguchi, overlooking the Kanmon Strait, and enshrines Emperor Antoku, the 81st emperor. On March 24, 1185 (Juei 4), the Taira clan was defeated in the Battle of Dannoura, the last great battle of the Genpei War, and the eight-year-old emperor, held in the arms of Nii-no-Ama (Taira no Tokiko), entered the sea together with the Taira. According to the shrine's official website, the imperial court then ordered a memorial hall to be built in Nagato Province in 1191 (Kenkyu 2), and a Mieido hall was raised over the tomb. The temple that guarded it, Amidaji, was an imperially sponsored temple that later became a Shingon-sect temple and is said to have been revered by powerful figures including Toyotomi Hideyoshi and the Ouchi and Mori clans.

After the Meiji-era order separating Shinto and Buddhism, Amidaji was abolished and the Mieido was first renamed Tenno-sha, then established on October 7, 1875 (Meiji 8) as the Kanpei Chusha shrine Akama-gu. In 1940 (Showa 15) it was raised to Kanpei Taisha and renamed Akama Jingu. The main hall and the other buildings were destroyed by wartime damage in July 1945, and were rebuilt in stages after the war: the main hall and norito-den in 1949, the Suiten-mon gate in 1958, and the worship halls and cloisters in 1965, completing the present Ryugu-zukuri (dragon-palace-style) shrine.

## Highlights

**Suiten-mon** is the shrine's main gate, a vermilion-lacquered two-story gate that evokes the Dragon Palace. The official website calls it "the world's only Ryugu-zukuri" structure, and its design draws on a poem said to have been composed by Nii-no-Ama that speaks of a "capital beneath the waves." It was completed in April 1958 and was registered as a national Registered Tangible Cultural Property in 2018.

**The worship halls** were completed in 1965 and consist of an outer hall with vermilion pillars and white walls and an inner hall with a karahafu (curved) gable. Because the deity is also called "Suiten-no-Okami" (Great Deity of Water and Heaven), the courtyard holds a "water garden" flooded with water, where the dragon-palace style shows most clearly.

**Nanamori-zuka (the Taira clan tombs)** is where warriors such as Taira no Tomomori, who were defeated at Dannoura, and Nii-no-Ama (Taira no Tokiko) are enshrined. Next to it is **Antoku Tenno Amidaji no Misasagi**, the imperial mausoleum maintained by the Imperial Household Agency, where the Senteisai mausoleum ceremony takes place every May 2.

**Hoichi-do (the Hall of Hoichi the Earless)** houses a wooden statue of Hoichi, the blind biwa player of a tale that was handed down at Amidaji (today's Akama Jingu). The story became widely known when Lafcadio Hearn (Koizumi Yakumo) translated it into English in his *Kwaidan*, and the hall was built in March 1957.

**The treasure hall** holds the Nagato-bon manuscript of the Tale of the Heike and the Akama Jingu documents (both Important Cultural Properties), as well as an illustrated scroll of Emperor Antoku's legend, portraits of the Taira clan and Heike biwa lutes. It is open 9:00 AM–4:30 PM, with an admission fee of ¥100.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds are free to enter, and the shrine office is open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM. The goshuin costs ¥500 and is issued only as a pre-written sheet of paper, so entries in a goshuincho book are not accepted. Prayer requests (for household safety, business prosperity, maritime safety, traffic safety, safe childbirth, protection from misfortune and more) are accepted from 9:00 AM to around 3:00 PM at the shrine office without a reservation.

It is about a 10-minute ride on a Sanden Bus (bound for Chofu) from JR Shimonoseki Station to the "Akamajingu-mae" stop, right at the shrine. There is a seaside parking lot for about 50 standard cars, but on the day of the Senteisai Jorō Sanpai there is no general parking, so public transport is recommended.

## Annual Festivals

- **Senteisai (every year, May 2–4)**: A special Shinto ritual held over three days from the memorial day of Emperor Antoku (the 24th day of the third lunar month, fixed as May 2 in the new calendar at the Meiji calendar reform). On May 2 there is the mausoleum ceremony at Antoku Tenno Amidaji no Misasagi and a memorial service for the Taira clan. On May 3, after the main ritual and dedicated performances, comes the **Jorō Sanpai**, in which five groups of Jorō (court ladies) cross a special bridge set up in the grounds in the "outer eight-figure" gait and bow before the deity. May 4 is the Goshinko-sai and Otabisho-sai; in recent years the procession has been cancelled and only the rites are held (no procession in 2026 either). The Jorō Sanpai draws large crowds and entry is restricted (doors opened at 9:30 AM in 2026).
- **Hoichi Biwa Memorial Service (July 15)**: The festival day of Hoichi-do, when a biwa memorial service is held.
- **New Year's rites and first visit (January 1)**: The first drum of the year is struck on the drum tower every New Year's Day.
- Other annual events include **Setsubun (February 3)**, the **Heike Hina-nagashi rite (early March)** and the **tea offering ceremony (April 24)**.

## Nearby Food & Sightseeing

It has statues of Minamoto no Yoshitsune leaping between ships and of Taira no Tomomori, who sank carrying an anchor, with close-up views of the Kanmon Bridge and passing ships.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kanmon%20Bridge%20Shimonoseki%20Yamaguchi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Kanmon Bridge</strong></a>: The 1,068 m bridge linking Honshu and Kyushu, visible across the Kanmon Strait from the Dannoura battlefield site.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Karato%20Market%20Shimonoseki%20Yamaguchi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Karato Market</strong></a>: A market for Shimonoseki seafood. On Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays, the "Ikiiki Bakkan-gai" event is held, where wholesale fishmongers sell sushi.

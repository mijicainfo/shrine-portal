## History

Imizu Jinja stands on the site of the old keep (Honmaru) of Takaoka Castle in the heart of Takaoka Kojo Park, Takaoka, Toyama. Its name is read "Imizu," and the official site styles it "Etchu Sochinju Ichinomiya" (the chief shrine of Etchu Province). The official site names Futagami no Kami as the enshrined deity and says that since the Meiji era this deity has been worshipped as Ninigi no Mikoto.

According to the official site, the shrine is thought to have begun as a place of worship for the sacred Mt. Futagami itself, with its founding placed in ancient times. It first stood at the foot of Mt. Futagami about 5 km north of its present site, and until the Edo period was revered as "Futagamiyama Daigongen" under the blend of Shinto and Buddhism. The name "Imizu" is said to derive from the "Imizu no Kuni no Miyatsuko" recorded in the early Heian-period "Kokuzo Hongi." The shrine's chronology notes that in 905 (Engi 5) the Engishiki register of shrines (Izumo manuscript) lists it as the only Myojin Taisha in Etchu Province, and that in 1163 (Chokan 1) it is recorded as the "Etchu Ichinomiya."

It was ranked a Kokuhei Chusha (national mid-rank shrine) in 1871 (Meiji 4), and on September 16, 1875 (Meiji 8) it moved from Mt. Futagami to the Honmaru site of Takaoka Castle. The shrine explains that it is the only ichinomiya in Japan whose buildings stand on a castle site from the Edo period. The Great Takaoka Fire of 1900 spread to the shrine, and in 1902 (Meiji 35) the four buildings (main hall, worship hall, offering hall and shrine office) were completed. According to the official site, the present shinmei-style buildings were designed by Ito Chuta and built by Matsui Kakuhei (松井角平). In 2025 (Reiwa 7), to mark 150 years since the relocation, the periodic grand festival events were held from September 13 to 17.

## Highlights

The centerpiece of the grounds is the worship hall and main hall built in 1902. According to the official grounds guide, the main hall has six katsuogi (roof billets) against four on the worship hall. As part of the 150th-anniversary project, the copper roofs of the buildings were re-covered and restored. The plaque on the middle gate is by Prince Arisugawa Taruhito, and the grounds also hold the First Torii of Kiso cypress (about 4.8 m high), given by the Ise Jingu in 2015, and a torii granted in 1929 on the occasion of the Showa Emperor's enthronement ceremonies.

The sacred red plum tree was donated in 1875 and is said to be over 400 years old (official site); its hollow trunk is described as forming a heart shape. According to the official site, the grounds guide also covers the purification pavilion completed in 2015, the sumo ring that was first built in Kojo Park in 1923 and later moved to its present site, and Chushun Bridge (commonly called Taiko-bashi) over the outer moat of Kojo Park.

The shrine's home, Takaoka Kojo Park, is the site of Takaoka Castle, associated with Maeda Toshinaga, the first lord of the Kaga domain, and is a nationally designated Historic Site (designated April 2015). The official site says it covers about 210,000 square meters, roughly a third of it water moats, and is a park listed among the "100 Best Cherry Blossom Spots in Japan," with about 2,700 cherry trees. The shrine also has four auxiliary shrines on and around Mt. Futagami, and the Takaoka City Gokoku Shrine (a massha) stands at Tsubakiyama (椿山) inside Kojo Park.

## Visiting Tips

The official site lists the shrine office and amulet counter as open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM and prayer reception as 9:00 AM–4:30 PM, with the first prayer of the day at 9:30 AM. Individuals do not need to book a prayer. The Sanshuden hall is currently being renovated: according to the shrine's notice dated September 26, 2026, a new amulet counter and a prayer reception and waiting area are to be built facing the purification pavilion (no completion date is given). The shrine office roof is also reported to be re-covered, so counter locations may change during the work. Calling the shrine office at 0766-22-3104 is recommended.

The official site lists a kirie goshuin (¥1,000, limited quantity), a shrine original in pale green (¥1,500), and a gold goshuin available only on Ichiryumanbai days (¥1,500). The kirie design changes by month and season, and the shrine says the latest information is posted on the <a href="https://www.instagram.com/imizujinjya/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">shrine's official Instagram</a>. The price of goshuin books and goshuin-only hours are not stated on the official site.

It is about a 10-minute walk from Takaoka Station on the Ainokaze Toyama Railway. By car, a visitors' lot inside the grounds (in front of the archery range, etc.) is available, but the shrine sits in the middle of Kojo Park, the entrance is hard to find, and car navigation may end its guidance outside the park, the official site warns. During the New Year period (January 1–3) a free temporary parking area opens, and from 11:00 PM on December 31 to 7:00 PM on January 1 traffic is controlled on the surrounding roads and inside the park. A notice dated October 21, 2026 also sets out rules for photographers on the grounds, including collecting a photography permit at the prayer reception and amulet counter before shooting.

## Annual Festivals

Main events from the official annual-events page (dates as stated there):

- **Tsuitachi-sai (1st of every month)**: Monthly "tsukitate" sweets themed on the season, Etchu Province or the Manyoshu are offered and then sold after the ritual.
- **Sagicho, the Imizu fire festival (January 14)**: A sacred fire kindled by the ancient friction method called maigiri is used to burn New Year decorations and old amulets.
- **Setsubun festival with the grand ceremony for good fortune and warding off misfortune (開運厄除大祭・節分祭) (February 3)**
- **Reisai, the main spring festival (April 23)**: The most important ritual of the year, in which two shrine maidens perform the "Urayasu no Mai." On the same day at 2:00 PM, the Tsukiyama ritual (築山行事, a Toyama Prefecture intangible folk cultural property) is held at Futagami Imizu Jinja at the foot of Mt. Futagami, the shrine's original site.
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (June 30)**
- **Tanabata-sai (August 1–7)**
- **Autumn festival (September 16)**: Held on the anniversary of the relocation, it gives thanks for the deity's blessings and the harvest.
- **Shichi-Go-San visits (October 1–November 30)**
- **Niiname-sai (November 23)**
- **Toshikoshi no Oharae (December 29)**
- **New Year's visit (January 1–3)**: The Saitan Daikigan-sai is held on New Year's Day, and a free temporary parking area opens.

Event dates can change, so check the "Festivals and Annual Events" page on the official site for the latest.

## History

Sakuradasan Shrine stands in the Sakurada district of Kurikoma (the former town of Kurikoma), Kurihara, Miyagi. Its registered name in the Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association's database is "Yama Jinja" (read Sanjinja), and it is called "Sakuradasan Jinja" with the district name added. The main deity is Emperor Buretsu.

According to the Shrine Association's history, Kano Kamonnosuke and Koga Daimuraji (狩野掃部之祐 and 久我大連; readings not given in the source), who had served Emperor Buretsu, made a pilgrimage to the foot of Higashi-Komagata-yama after the emperor's death on December 8, 506, and built a shrine in remembrance, honoring it as "Yama Jinja." It was ranked as a village shrine in June 1879 (Meiji 12), merged with the Hachiman Shrine of Sakurada Nakayashiki in December 1908 (Meiji 41), and designated a shrine receiving offerings (heihaku kyoshin-sha) in July 1926 (Taisho 15). All of this is according to the Shrine Association's entry.

The buildings are said to have burned in the Tenki and Tensho eras and again in 1883 (Meiji 16). In the Meiji fire the worship hall and old documents were lost, leaving only the main hall and the shrine plaque (made in 1813, Bunka 10). The Shrine Association explains that, with donations from parishioners and devotees, a worship hall with carvings and a ceiling decorated with paintings (the paintings dating from 1791, Kansei 3) was begun in October 1924 and completed in April 1925.

**Connection with Kano Eiko**: The profile on the Miyagi Prefectural Government's interview page with Kano Eiko ("Miyagi 12 Samurais") describes him as the 39th head priest of Sakuradasan Shrine, who obtained his priest qualification in 2016 and often works as a priest at his family's shrine. In the interview he says that, alongside his comedy career, he now spends more time serving as a priest at his family home in Kurihara and wants to carry on the family business properly, and he invites people to visit Kurihara. Daily Sports (October 17, 2026) reports that he said on the radio that he takes time off from entertainment work to help prepare for New Year and with the first days of January. The Shrine Association's entry lists no head priest's name, so this page simply introduces the public information above.

## Highlights

The Shrine Association records that the shrine buildings were designated a tangible cultural property of Kurikoma Town in October 1986 (Showa 61) and of Kurihara City in April 2005 (Heisei 17). The carvings of the worship hall and the ceiling paintings, said to date from 1791, are among the shrine's highlights.

The grounds contain a cherry tree known as the "Oyako-zakura" (parent-and-child cherry). According to the Shrine Association, the "child cherry" is said to be 2,000 years old, citing a Miyagi forestry document of 1915 (Taisho 4), and parishioners look after it as a historically valuable tree. This figure is the Shrine Association's statement and is not something this page has verified.

The "Sakurada Bayashi" is a local performing art offered at the spring grand festival. The Shrine Association says it has been designated a cultural property of the former town of Kurikoma and draws many visitors from various places.

## Visiting Tips

Visiting hours, parking and the hours of the amulet office are not listed in the Shrine Association's entry, and no official website could be found. If you want amulets or a goshuin, it is safest to call the contact number listed there (0228-45-3623) beforehand.

The existence of goshuin is confirmed through media reports. The offering amount and whether they are always available are unknown, and it is reported that Kano Eiko sometimes writes them around New Year. He is not necessarily at the shrine when you visit.

According to a guide site, the nearest bus stops are "Takagi" on the Kurihara City bus Sakurada Line or "Arigasawa" on the Iwagasaki Line, and the shrine is about 14 km from Kurikoma-kogen Station on the Tohoku Shinkansen. These are not official details, so check current bus information beforehand.

## Annual Festivals

- **Spring Grand Festival (April 12) and Autumn Grand Festival (September 12)**: The festival dates listed in the Miyagi Prefecture Shrine Association's entry. One guide site instead says the annual festival is held on the second Sunday of April; sources differ, so confirm the details with the shrine.
- **Offering of the Sakurada Bayashi**: According to the Shrine Association, it is offered at the spring grand festival.
- **New Year's visit (January)**: Media report that Kano Eiko takes time off from entertainment work to help prepare for New Year and with the first days of January (details of the schedule are unknown).

Festival dates may differ between the Shrine Association's entry and guide sites, so check before your visit.

## History

Yanagisawa Jinja stands on the former Honmaru (main compound) of the Koriyama Castle ruins in Yamatokoriyama, Nara. This page follows the Yamatokoriyama City Tourism Association in writing it with the character 澤, but it is also written 柳沢神社. The reading "Yanagisawa Jinja" comes from the smartphone page of the Yamatokoriyama Goshuin Map, which introduces shrines and temples in the city; no official shrine website could be found.

According to the Yamatokoriyama City Tourism Association, the enshrined deity is Yanagisawa Yoshiyasu, a chamberlain (sobayonin) to the fifth shogun, Tokugawa Tsunayoshi, and the shrine was founded in 1880 (Meiji 13) by former domain retainers. The map's shrine description says that Yoshiyasu's son Yoshisato was transferred from Kofu to Koriyama in 1724 (Kyoho 9), and that the Yanagisawa family then governed the area for about 140 years until the Meiji Restoration. Yoshiyasu was Yoshisato's father.

The castle-information site Kojodan (攻城団) says in its commentary that the founding was on October 29, that the shrine was first built on the site of the Ninomaru (where Nara Prefectural Koriyama High School now stands), and that it moved to the present Honmaru site in June 1882 (Meiji 15). This account comes from that one site and could not be confirmed on the tourism association's page.

## Highlights

For the Koriyama Castle ruins where the shrine stands, the tourism association says the restored Otemon (追手門) gate and turrets evoke the past, and that the stone walls of the keep base include reused stones such as the "Sakasa Jizo" and foundation stones of the Rajomon gate. It also notes that on November 10, 2022 (Reiwa 4), areas including the Honmaru, along with the inner and middle moats, were designated national historic sites.

On the shrine itself, Kojodan writes that a torii and a stone shrine marker stand at the entrance of the bridge 竹林橋, which once spanned the gap between the Honmaru and Ninomaru (the page gives no reading for the bridge's name, so it is left in kanji). The same page's shrine table lists the grounds as "1,583 tsubo" and lists among the buildings an ancestral-spirit hall described as being for "Yanagisawa Yoshisato and others."

It is also known as a cherry-blossom spot. Rurubu's page on the Koriyama Castle ruins says that after the castle buildings were removed in the Meiji era, cherry trees were planted around the shrine when it was built and bonfires were lit, which is said to be the beginning of night cherry-blossom viewing (this is described as "said to be" an origin). The same page puts the current number of cherry trees at about 600.

## Visiting Tips

According to the Yamatokoriyama City Tourism Association, the grounds can be viewed freely, until 19:00 in summer and 18:00 in winter (the switch dates are not given). During the Castle Festival the hours extend to 21:00. There are no closing days and admission is free. The page lists the shrine's phone number as 0743-55-1100.

No official explanation of the benefits was found. A privately run guide site says the shrine is revered as a god of "rising in the world and learning," but that is one article, based on a 2016 visit. The benefit tags on this page are only a rough guide following that description.

The tourism association's page does not say whether goshuin are offered, when, or at what cost. A user-posting shrine-visit site has goshuin posts, but that is user-submitted information and could not be confirmed. Checking by phone before you go is the safe approach.

It is about 15 minutes on foot from JR Koriyama Station and about 10 minutes from Kintetsu's Koriyama Station. Parking is 5 standard-size car spaces, available only to those visiting for a prayer service.

## Annual Festivals

- **Annual festival (November 2)**: Kojodan's shrine table gives the annual festival date as November 2. The tourism association's page does not mention it, and this year's (2026) date has already passed. Please check with the shrine on how it is held.
- **66th Yamatokoriyama Castle Festival (March 24 – April 7, 2027)**: According to the tourism association's page, it is held at the Koriyama Castle ruins and across the castle town, with a dedication ceremony and a prayer-bead (juzu) rite plus the Queen Himiko handover ceremony on March 24, a goldfish contest on the 27th, a period procession and the White Fox Procession on the 28th, and a Castle Festival concert on April 4. During the festival, the shrine's viewing hours extend to 21:00.

## Nearby Food & Sightseeing

- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Koriyama%20Castle%20Ruins%20Yamatokoriyama%20Nara" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Koriyama Castle Ruins (historic site)</strong></a>: The castle site where the shrine stands, with the Otemon gate and the keep base. According to the tourism association, a volunteer "Koriyama Castle stone-wall storyteller" explains and guides at the keep base on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, 10:00 to 16:00 (15:00 in winter).
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Yanagisawa%20Bunko%20Yamatokoriyama%20Nara" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Yanagisawa Bunko</strong></a>: A museum-library on the site of Bishamon-kaku, holding tens of thousands of old documents, including calligraphy and paintings donated by the Yanagisawa family and the official records of the Koriyama domain, according to the tourism association. It is open 9:00 to 17:00 (last entry 16:30), closed every Monday and the fourth Tuesday (open if that day is a holiday), and costs ¥300 for adults (tourism association).
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Eikeiji%20Yamatokoriyama%20Nara" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Eikeiji</strong></a>: An Obaku-sect temple that the tourism association describes as the family temple of the Yanagisawa lords of Koriyama Castle. Its gate is a city-designated cultural property and is said to be a relocated gate of the old Koriyama Castle, and the seated statues of Yanagisawa Yoshiyasu and his wife are also city-designated cultural properties. About 10 minutes on foot from Kintetsu's Koriyama Station (tourism association).

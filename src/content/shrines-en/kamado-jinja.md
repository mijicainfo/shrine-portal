## History

Kamado Jinja stands on Mount Homan, a sacred peak rising 829.6 meters above the town of Dazaifu. According to shrine tradition, it was founded in 673, when the monk Shinren, practicing austerities on the mountain, was visited by the goddess Tamayori-hime, prompting the construction of a sanctuary at the summit; archaeological evidence suggests the site was already a place of worship by the latter half of the 8th century. When the Dazaifu government headquarters was established here in the Nara period, Mount Homan lay to its northeast — the "kimon," or spirit gate, a direction traditionally considered inauspicious — and the shrine came to be deeply revered as the guardian protecting the government seat, and all of Kyushu, from misfortune.

The shrine's principal deity is Tamayori-hime no Mikoto, worshipped alongside Empress Jingu and Emperor Ojin. Tamayori-hime is the mother of Japan's legendary first emperor, Jimmu, and her divine virtue has long made her an object of devotion as a goddess of good relationships. The shrine is structured as two sanctuaries — an upper shrine (Kamigu) at the summit and a lower shrine (Shimogu) at the mountain's base; a middle shrine once stood partway up the mountain but was abolished in the Meiji era. Today the lower shrine serves as the main place of worship, its striking modern architecture and famous autumn foliage drawing steady crowds of visitors.

## Highlights

The lower shrine of Kamado Jinja is cradled in the lush greenery of Mount Homan, and its greatest appeal lies in being able to worship amid beautiful natural surroundings through every season. During the autumn foliage season in particular, the grounds turn brilliant shades of red and gold, making it one of Kyushu's premier spots for maple viewing and drawing photographers and sightseers alike. The shrine's recently rebuilt amulet office, with its glass-walled modern design blended with traditional shrine architecture, has become a popular subject of conversation and draws younger visitors seeking charms for good relationships.

For those with the time and stamina, a proper mountain trail leads from the lower shrine up to the summit's upper shrine; the path, part of the Kyushu Nature Trail, passes landmarks such as the "Aikei no Iwa" rock, associated with love fortune-telling, and the ruins of the former middle shrine. From the summit, a sweeping panorama unfolds over the town of Dazaifu and all the way to Hakata Bay, rewarding those who make the climb.

## Visiting Tips

A visit to the lower shrine alone takes about 30 minutes, but allow an hour if you plan to explore the grounds or view the autumn colors. If you intend to climb to the upper shrine, budget 2–3 hours round trip, and be sure to wear comfortable walking shoes and bring water. Goshuin stamps are available at the amulet office from 9:00 to 17:00, and staff at the shrine office can sometimes accommodate requests until 19:00. Since the shrine is an easy trip from Dazaifu Tenmangu by bus or on foot, many visitors combine the two shrines in a single outing.

## Annual Festivals

- **Setsubun Festival (February 3)**: A rite to drive away evil spirits and pray for good health through the year ahead, including a bean-scattering ceremony.
- **Iwato Kagura Dance Dedication (spring and autumn)**: A sacred dance rooted in Mount Homan's ancient mountain worship, performed in a classical style for visitors to witness.
- **Autumn Foliage Illumination (November)**: The grounds are lit up at night during the peak of the autumn colors, transforming the approach into a dreamlike scene very different from its daytime appearance.
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (June 30)**: A purification rite that clears away the first half-year's impurities and prays for continued health, featuring a ritual passage through a large woven grass ring.

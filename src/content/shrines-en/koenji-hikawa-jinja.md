## History

Koenji Hikawa Shrine stands in a residential neighborhood of Suginami, about a two-minute walk from Koenji Station, and enshrines Susanoo no Mikoto. According to the shrine's official site, a legend says that a warrior who took part in Minamoto no Yoritomo's campaign in the north settled in Koenji village and became a farmer. Following a divine message from the Hikawa Shrine of Musashi Province (Omiya), the villagers built a shrine on this high, pine-covered ground, so it has also been regarded as a deity of agriculture. The date of founding is unclear. In the Edo period the Soto Zen temple Koenji looked after the shrine, and in 1874 it was ranked as a village shrine. The main hall was rebuilt in 1916, burned down in the air raids of May 1945, and was replaced by a temporary shrine in 1947. The present buildings were completed in 1971.

The subsidiary Kisho Shrine began in April 1944, when it was built inside the compound of the Army Meteorological Department in what was then Umabashi, Suginami (now Koenji-kita). After the war it was supposed to be removed under the Shinto Directive, but it was overlooked in the survey and survived. The head priest at the time agreed to take it in, and it was moved to Koenji Hikawa Shrine in 1948. The deity, Yagokoro Omoikane no Mikoto, is the god of wisdom who devised the plan to lure Amaterasu out of the heavenly rock cave, and he is also enshrined here as a god of the weather.

## Highlights

The main draw is Kisho Shrine, said to be the only weather shrine in Japan. It is a small shrine in a corner of the grounds, visited by people who work with the weather, by candidates for the weather forecaster exam, and by those hoping for fine weather on a wedding day or a sports day. Amulets and fortunes themed on sunny weather are on offer.

The shrine is also known as a place that appears in Makoto Shinkai's 2019 film Weathering with You. TOKYO MX reported that the geta-shaped ema also appear in the film, that visitors roughly doubled after its release, and that the head priest said he was glad to see people come on film pilgrimages who do not usually visit shrines. The film's depiction includes creative license, and the real Kisho Shrine is a small subsidiary shrine.

Rows of geta-shaped ema hang on the racks in the grounds. They recall the old custom of kicking a geta clog into the air to predict the next day's weather, and they are one of Kisho Shrine's most popular items.

## Visiting Tips

Two goshuin are offered, one for Koenji Hikawa Shrine and one for Kisho Shrine, and the design changes every month. They are reported to cost ¥500 each, but the official site does not list the fee, so please confirm on the day. They are usually given as pre-written sheets. The shrine office is on the right as you face the main hall.

Kisho Shrine's main items, with the offering amounts listed on the official site, are the Sunny-Weather Charm (Haremamori), the geta ema (red or yellow), the Weather Card Charm, the Fine-Weather Charm (Kotenmamori), and the Weather-Sickness Charm (Otenki-byo-yoke), each for ¥1,000, the Teru-teru Charm (red or blue) for ¥1,000, and the teru-teru-bozu-shaped fortune slip called Teruteru-mikuji for ¥500. A charm for weather forecaster exam candidates is also available. Items are meant to be received in person.

The shrine office is open 9:00 AM–4:00 PM, but the hours are flexible and it closes on irregular days, so it is safest to check the official site or X account before you go. Prayer services must be booked in advance, by phone (03-3314-4147) or through the official site's contact form.

Koenji Station on the JR line is about a two-minute walk away. On weekends and holidays Chuo Line rapid trains skip the station, so take the Sobu Line local. There is parking on the grounds for worshippers, though it may be unavailable during shrine events or construction.

## Annual Festivals

- **Kisho-sai (June 1)**: The annual festival of Kisho Shrine, held to coincide with Meteorological Day, which marks the founding of the Tokyo Meteorological Observatory in 1875. In 2026 an eve-of-festival event was also held on May 31.
- **Grand Festival (last Sunday of August)**: The shrine's summer festival, held on August 29–30 in 2026. According to the official site, the Tokyo Koenji Awa Odori, held the same weekend, began as a dance offered to this shrine.
- **New Year's Day Festival (January 1)**: The shrine's New Year ceremony.
- **Hinamatsuri Mochi-pounding (first Sunday of March)**: A rice-cake pounding event organized by the parishioners' youth association.
- **Summer and Year-End Purification (June 30 and December 30) and New Year's Eve Service (December 31)**: Two great purification rites and a ceremony on New Year's Eve.

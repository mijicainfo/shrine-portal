## History

Ōuo Shrine (Ōuo Jinja) stands on the Ariake Sea coast in Tara, in southern Saga Prefecture. Its principal deity is said to be Ōuo Daimyojin. The year of its founding is unknown, but according to the Saga Prefecture Heritage database, Shigemoto, the sixth lord of Isahaya, rebuilt the shrine in 1682 and erected the torii in 1693.

As for the origin, a story recorded in the town history is well known. Villagers left a corrupt local magistrate on Okinoshima; he prayed to the dragon god and was carried back on the back of a great fish. Enshrining that great fish is said to be how the shrine began. What kind of fish it was varies by source (yellowtail and "namiuo" are both given). The on-site sign (put up by the Sakaemachi district) and the Tara Town Tourism Association's page also present the story as an event of "about 300 years ago (around 1693)," tying the legend to the year the torii were erected.

The shrine and its sea torii are designated Saga Prefecture Heritage No. 2017-1 ("Approach to the Sea God: Ōuo Shrine and the Sea Torii"), and, according to Tara Town, were certified in 2021 as one of Japan's "Hundred Moons" (the moon lighting the sea torii).

## Highlights

The main attraction is the set of three vermilion sea torii standing in the shallows of the Ariake Sea. According to the Tara Town Tourism Association, they are the shrine's second torii, built between the shrine and Okinoshima and extending about 200 m from the shore; the plaque reads "Oki no Kami." The Saga heritage description notes that the sacred Mount Tara, Ōuo Shrine, and Okinoshima (the stage of the legend) lie on a single straight line, so the torii form what it calls "the very approach to the god of the sea."

The Ariake Sea has what Tara Town describes as "Japan's largest-class tidal range, up to 6 m," and the scene changes completely with the hour. At high tide the torii appear to float on the water; at low tide you can walk beneath them. According to Nippon Tabi Magazine, the outermost torii is more than half submerged at high tide, and the different depths at which the three are submerged show how deep the water is. Morning and evening light, moonlight, and starry skies add to the atmosphere, and the spot is known as a favorite for photography.

The torii are rebuilt about every 30 years by custom, most recently on February 6–7, 2024 (the 6th year of Reiwa) because of age, as announced by the Tara Town Tourism Association. Some of the photos on this page show the torii before that rebuild.

## Visiting Tips

It is about a 10-minute walk from Tara Station on the JR Nagasaki Line, or about 5 minutes by car from Michi-no-Eki Tara. From the Kashima side, take the Yutoku Bus (bound for Takezaki Port) and get off at "Tara Sakaemachi (Kaichu Torii)," right beside the site. The official Saga tourism site Asobo-Saga lists parking for roughly a dozen or so cars and 3–4 large buses, but vehicle entry may be restricted during the spring and autumn nori-harvest seasons and during the August lantern event. Please follow the signs on site for where to stop.

Choose your timing by the view you want. For torii floating on the water, aim for around high tide; to walk out to the torii, aim for around low tide. Exact times change daily, so check them in advance, for example with the Japan Meteorological Agency tide table linked from the Tara Town page. Mudflats and shorelines can be muddy and the tide comes back in, so watch your footing and the time.

The land around the torii is used by fishers. Tara Town asks visitors to be considerate when fishing work is underway; work periods are given as roughly April (early to late) and from September to around the end of October (the dates vary by source and year). Please observe quietly and keep out of the way of vehicles and work.

We could not confirm from official information whether goshuin are offered, and some listings say there are none. If you are hoping for one, please ask the Tara Town Tourism Association (+81-954-67-0065).

## Annual Festivals

- **Sen-no-Toro Matsuri (Thousand Lanterns Festival, every August)**: A community event by local residents that, according to the Saga Prefecture Heritage database, is held every August. Tourism sites describe several hundred lanterns lined up beside the sea torii. Vehicle entry may be restricted at this time, so please check dates and access with the Tara Town Tourism Association.

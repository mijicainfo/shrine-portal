## History

Chokaisan Omonoimi Jinja is one of the great ancient shrines of the Tohoku region, venerating Mount Chokai itself (2,236 meters) — an active volcano straddling the border of Yamagata and Akita prefectures — as its sacred object of worship. Its founding is traditionally dated to 564 (Kinmei 25) or the reign of Emperor Keiko, though accounts vary. The earliest solid documentary evidence appears in the Nihon Sandai Jitsuroku, which records in its entry for 871 (Jogan 13) that an Omonoimi shrine stood "atop the mountain in Akumi District."

According to shrine tradition, because worship at the summit itself was so difficult, a lowland shrine was established at Fukura in 806, becoming the origin of today's Fukura base shrine. On the opposite side of the mountain, the Warabioka base shrine traces its founding to a mountain-opening legend involving En no Gyoja, the semi-legendary founder of Shugendo, and it went on to flourish as a major center of Mount Chokai's ascetic mountain-worship tradition. The shrine received early imperial recognition, being elevated to official shrine status in 862, and was listed as a Myojin Taisha (major shrine) in the Engishiki shrine register. It later became the ichinomiya (chief shrine) of Dewa Province, drawing devotion from nobility and commoners alike.

## The Enshrined Deities

The principal deity is Omonoimi no Okami, a deity unique to Mount Chokai that appears in neither the Kojiki nor the Nihon Shoki, and who is regarded as identical to Toyouke-hime no Mikoto, goddess of grain and sustenance. The Fukura base shrine additionally enshrines Tsukuyomi no Mikoto, the moon deity — a detail that varies between the two base shrines and is one of the small but distinctive features of this shrine's structure.

## One Shrine, Three Sites: The Summit Shrine and Two Base Shrines

The single most important thing to understand about Chokaisan Omonoimi Jinja is that it isn't a single building, but the collective name for three places of worship built around Mount Chokai itself.

- **The Summit Shrine (Sancho Go-Honsha)**: Located near the top of Mount Chokai, between the Gyoja-dake and Shichiko-san peaks, this is the literal heart of the shrine. Like Ise Jingu, it follows a tradition of rebuilding its sanctuary every 20 years (shikinen sengu), most recently in 1997. Reaching it requires a full mountain climb from a trailhead such as Hokodate, Yunodai, or Harakawa, and the mountain lodges near the summit and at the 7th station open only during the summer climbing season, roughly early July through September.
- **Fukura Base Shrine (Fukura Kuchi-no-miya)**: Located in Fukura, Yuza Town, and easily reached on foot from JR Fukura Station in about 5 minutes — this is where most visitors actually go. It also serves as the goshuin office for both base shrines. Its annual festival on May 4 features rites including the Kanagayu (gruel-divination) and Ohamade ceremonies.
- **Warabioka Base Shrine (Warabioka Kuchi-no-miya)**: Located in Kami-Warabioka, Yuza Town, this site developed as a center of Mount Chokai's Shugendo ascetic tradition following En no Gyoja's mountain-opening legend. Its annual festival on May 3 features the Otanemaki rice-planting rite and Warabioka Ennen, a nationally designated Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property. It has no resident priest, so its goshuin is issued from the Fukura base shrine's office.

For an ordinary sightseeing visit or prayer, a trip to the well-connected Fukura base shrine is more than enough to experience this shrine's full significance.

## A Living Volcano's Faith

Mount Chokai is not only Yamagata Prefecture's highest peak but an active volcano that the Japan Meteorological Agency keeps under continuous monitoring. Its historical eruptions are well documented: the Konin era (810-823), a major eruption in 871 that produced a lava flow and — according to the Nihon Sandai Jitsuroku — caused significant damage downstream, the Manji era (1659-1663), the Genbun era (1740-1741), and 1821, with the most recent activity a small phreatic (steam) eruption in 1974.

These eruptions were traditionally understood as expressions of the mountain deity's anger, which only deepened devotion to Omonoimi Jinja as the shrine responsible for pacifying the mountain and securing bountiful harvests and safety at sea. Counted among Japan's 100 Famous Mountains and historically nicknamed "Dewa Fuji" or "Akita Fuji" for its graceful, Fuji-like silhouette, Mount Chokai's beauty carries the memory of real volcanic power beneath it.

## Annual Festivals

- **Saitansai (January 1)**: A New Year's rite praying for bountiful harvests and national peace.
- **Warabioka Base Shrine Annual Festival (May 3)**: The Warabioka base shrine's largest festival, featuring the Otanemaki rice-planting rite and the Warabioka Ennen performance, a nationally designated Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property.
- **Fukura Base Shrine Annual Festival (May 4)**: The Fukura base shrine's festival, including the Kanagayu gruel-divination rite (predicting the year's harvest) and the Ohamade beach ceremony praying for safety at sea.
- **Summer Mountain-Opening Festival (July 1)**: Marks the start of the summer climbing season and prays for climbers' safety; the summit lodges open shortly after.
- **Chokaisan Hiawase Rite (July 14)**: A traditional fire-signaling ceremony in which beacon fires are lit and matched between the summit and the foothills.
- **Niiname-sai (November)**: An autumn rite of thanksgiving for the year's harvest.

## Goshuin

A goshuin is available at both the Fukura and Warabioka base shrines, each for a fee of ¥300. Since Warabioka has no resident priest, its goshuin is also handled through the Fukura base shrine's office (0234-77-2301). If you'd like to collect both in one visit, going to Fukura is the surest option.

## Access & Visiting Information

**Fukura base shrine** (1 Fukura Nunokura, Yuza, Akumi District, Yamagata) is about a 5-minute walk (3 minutes by car) from Fukura Station on the JR Uetsu Line, and about 25 minutes by car from the Sakata-Minato interchange on the Nihonkai-Tohoku Expressway. Free parking for about 30 vehicles is available on the grounds.

**Warabioka base shrine** (51 Matsugaoka, Kami-Warabioka, Yuza, Akumi District, Yamagata) is best reached by car — about 10 minutes from Yuza Station, and about 20 minutes from Sakata-Minato IC — since the nearer Minami-Chokai Station is some distance away. Free parking for about 20 vehicles is available.

Both base shrines are free to visit and open at all times. If you plan to reach the **summit shrine**, go only during the summer climbing season (roughly July through September) via one of the established trailheads — Hokodate, Yunodai, or Harakawa — and with proper mountaineering preparation.

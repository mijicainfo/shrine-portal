## History

Osaka Tenmangu enshrines Sugawara no Michizane, the god of learning, and is affectionately known locally as "Tenma no Tenjin-san." The site's roots go back to 650, when the Daishogun Shrine was enshrined as a guardian for the capital as Emperor Kotoku built the Naniwa Nagara-Toyosaki Palace. It stood in the northwest of the present grounds, and tradition says Michizane visited it before leaving for Dazaifu. The surrounding area came to be called the "Daishogun Forest" or "Tenjin Forest," and the names of the nearby Minami-morimachi and Kita-morimachi districts preserve that memory.

About 50 years after Michizane died at Dazaifu, seven pine trees are said to have sprung up overnight in front of the Daishogun Shrine, their tips glowing each night. In response to this auspicious sign, Emperor Murakami ordered that Michizane's spirit be enshrined here, and Tenmangu was founded in 949. In the Edo period the shrine suffered seven fires, including the great fires of 1724 and 1837 that burned it to the ground; the present main hall dates from its rebuilding in 1843.

## Highlights

The main hall at the heart of the grounds was rebuilt in 1843. Entering through the Omote-daimon (front gate), you see a large hall with a sweeping roof ahead. The grounds also hold more modern buildings such as the Baikaden and Sanshuden, which were registered as national cultural properties in 1999, along with the Hanjotei rakugo theater and many subsidiary shrines including the Daishogun Shrine — a shrine forest in the middle of the city, surrounded by buildings.

The plum, so closely tied to Michizane, is also treasured here, and plum trees grow in the grounds. A Plum Festival is held from mid-February to early March with displays of bonsai plums and swords (admission is charged; in 2025 it ran February 11 to March 2, with adult tickets at ¥700, and may vary by year).

## Visiting Tips

Osaka Tenmangu is in central Osaka and easy to reach by train. You can walk to it along the Tenjinbashi-suji shopping street from either JR Tozai Line Osaka-Temmangu Station or Osaka Metro Minami-morimachi Station (Tanimachi and Sakaisuji lines), so it is pleasant to browse the shops on the way. There is parking, but it can be unavailable when full or during ceremonies, so taking the train is safest at New Year and during Tenjin Matsuri.

The amulet office and goshuin counter are open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM, and a standard goshuin is ¥500. Prayer services are received 9:00 AM–5:00 PM (last reception 4:30 PM) at the amulet office, and usually take around 60 minutes from reception (longer during festival periods). Photography is prohibited inside the main hall and the corridors connected to it.

## Annual Festivals

- **Tenjin Matsuri (July 24 Yomiya, July 25 Honmiya)**: A festival with a history of a thousand years, said to have begun in 951 when a sacred spear was floated from the shore in front of the shrine. Counted as one of Japan's three great festivals and a defining summer event in Osaka, it involves rites for about a month from late June to July 25. On the 24th, the eve festival includes the Hokonagashi ritual, and on the 25th the main festival includes the main service, the transfer of the deity's spirit, the land procession, the boat procession, and votive fireworks. The Osaka tourism bureau describes about 3,000 costumed participants and more than 100 boats taking part.
- **Tenma Tenjin Ebisu Festival (January 9–11)**: Held over three days (Yoi-Ebisu, Hon-Ebisu / the Toka Ebisu Grand Festival, and Nokori-fuku), with Fukumusume "fortune maidens" giving out lucky bamboo branches and tokens. It revives the Edo-period "Toka Ebisu," brought back in 2006 with the opening of the Hanjotei theater.
- **Hatsu-Tenjin Baikasai and Uso-kae Ritual (January 24–25)**: The first Tenjin observance of the year, with a plum blossom rite and the Uso-kae ritual.
- **Setsubun Festival (February 3)**: A Setsubun observance held together with a rite at the Reifu Shrine.
- **Autumn Grand Festival and Yabusame (October 25)**: The shrine's autumn festival, at which yabusame mounted archery is offered.
- **Shu-Tenjin (December 25)**: The year's final Tenjin observance, when ema plaques are burned in a purification rite.

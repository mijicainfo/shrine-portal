## History

Samuhara Shrine sits in the middle of an office district in Osaka's Nishi Ward — a small shrine, but one with nationwide name recognition. Its three enshrined deities are Amenominakanushi no Okami, Takamimusubi no Okami, and Kamimusubi no Okami; "Samuhara" is said to be a collective name for these three, who according to the Kojiki were the very first deities to come into being at the dawn of creation.

The shrine's history began with the 1935 rebuilding of an old, small shrine that had stood in Okayama Prefecture. In 1950, its spirit was divided and enshrined as a subsidiary shrine of Hokoku Shrine on Nakanoshima in Osaka. Later, when Hokoku Shrine relocated to the grounds of Osaka Castle, Samuhara Shrine was in turn relocated to its present site in Itachibori, Nishi Ward, in 1961, where it remains today.

A reputation as a guardian against misfortune and danger spread by word of mouth over the years, and after the war the shrine grew into one revered nationwide for that protection. In recent years its ring-shaped amulets have become a topic of conversation on social media, drawing worshippers from far and wide.

## The Sacred Character "Samuhara"

"Samuhara" has long been believed to be a set of characters with the mysterious power to ward off disaster. According to tradition, soldiers heading off to war once carried amulets inscribed with these characters, and the shrine has drawn deep devotion as a guardian against misfortune and danger. Especially well known is the ring-shaped amulet inscribed with the sacred "Samuhara" characters, called the "Goshinkan-mamori," a popular item that can be hard to obtain and has become a topic of conversation on social media (availability changes depending on the season, so it's a good idea to check the official website in advance).

It's worth noting that the true sacred characters of "Samuhara" are a special script that cannot be displayed on most ordinary computers or smartphones. On this site, as in much printed material and on many other websites, the katakana rendering "サムハラ" (Samuhara) is used as a practical substitute, but it differs from the actual characters inscribed on the shrine's amulets and its wooden nameplate (gaku). We encourage you to see the authentic characters for yourself when you visit.

## Visiting Tips

The shrine sits in a corner of a residential block surrounded by office buildings — compact in scale, but with a dignified, solemn atmosphere. Visiting hours are limited, from 9:00 to 16:30, so allow yourself plenty of time. There's no parking on site, so public transportation is recommended.

## Annual Festivals

- **Spring and Autumn Grand Festivals**: Held twice a year, each over two fixed calendar days regardless of the day of the week; special prayer rites and tamagushi offerings are held during these periods, drawing large numbers of worshippers (check the official website for exact dates).

## Nearby Food & Sightseeing

The shrine is close to Osaka's central Honmachi district, easily reached from lively areas like Midosuji and Shinsaibashi. Many worshippers stop by during a break from the surrounding business district, or while sightseeing elsewhere in Osaka.

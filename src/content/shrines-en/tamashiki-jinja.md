## History

Tamashiki Shrine stands in Kisai, Kazo City, Saitama Prefecture. Its name is read "Tamashiki Jinja." According to the official site, until the Edo period it was styled "Chokugansho Tamashiki Jinja, Hisaizu Daimyojin," and as the chief tutelary shrine of the old Saitama District and the guardian shrine of the 48 villages of Kisai-ryo (騎西領) it was affectionately known as "Kisai no Myojin-sama." The enshrined deity is Onamuchi no Mikoto, also known by the name Okuninushi no Mikoto. The official site lists blessings of warding off misfortune and bringing good luck, matchmaking and safe childbirth, and explains that the deity is also deeply venerated as a god of good harvests and business prosperity.

According to the history on the official site, the shrine is said to have been founded in 703 (Taiho 3), when Tajihi no Mahito Miyakemaro went down to Musashi Province as pacification envoy of the Tosando circuit, and another account dates the founding to the 6th year of Emperor Seimu (136). It is an old shrine whose name is recorded in the Engishiki, promulgated in 927 (Encho 5). In 1574 (Tensho 2), during Uesugi Kenshin's campaign into the Kanto region, the shrine, then located in Shono Village (today's Shono, Kazo City), burned in the fighting, and its buildings, old records and treasures were lost. It was later rebuilt in front of the main gate of Kisai Castle in Negoya Village, and is said to have moved to its present site around 1627. However, the entry on 宮目神社 on the same official site gives the time of the move as "around 1620."

Of the present buildings, the main hall and offering hall date from 1816 (Bunka 13) and the worship hall from a restoration in 1898 (Meiji 31). The carvings around the outside of the main hall and offering hall are attributed to the fifth-generation 後藤茂右衛門.

## Highlights

The Tamashiki Shrine kagura was designated a national Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property on March 13, 2008 (Heisei 20). The main text of the official site calls it a national "Important Intangible Cultural Property" (国の重要無形文化財), but the category used by the Agency for Cultural Affairs' Cultural Heritage Online and by Kazo City is Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property. According to Cultural Heritage Online, dancers repeat the "shihogatame" movements oriented toward the four corners of the stage, performing in masks to flute and drum accompaniment without songs or spoken lines. The official site describes it as dance that preserves the original form of Edo kagura, and says it certainly has a history of more than 400 years. The number of pieces differs between sources: 17 including an extra piece on the official site, and 18 including extra pieces on Cultural Heritage Online. Its stage, the thatched kagura hall, was built in 1836 (Tenpo 7) and is a Kazo City-designated Tangible Cultural Property.

The two great ginkgo trees in the grounds, "Tamashiki Shrine Ginkgo," are a Kazo City-designated Natural Monument. According to Kazo City, they are male trees about 30 meters tall and about 500 years old.

Tamashiki Park, on the east side of the shrine, was laid out in 1924 (Taisho 13) as a shrine garden of more than 12,000 square meters and is now managed by the city (official site). The great wisteria in its northeast corner is a Saitama Prefecture-designated Natural Monument under the name "Tamashiki Shrine Wisteria." According to the official site, it is more than 400 years old, and branches from a trunk over 1 m in diameter spread across about 700 square meters. Kazo City estimates its age at 450 years and says the wisteria was originally at the Wakayama family's in Tomuro and was donated in 1933 (Showa 8).

In the grounds is the auxiliary shrine 宮目神社, dedicated to the deity Omiyanome no Kami. According to shrine tradition, it is an old shrine, also recorded as a "Shikinaisha," that stood here as the guardian deity of the land until Tamashiki Shrine moved to this site. There are also subsidiary shrines such as Tenjin-sha, Yasaka Shrine, Matsuo Shrine and Hakusan Shrine.

Another distinctive tradition is "Oshishi-sama," a purification observance in which communities receive the shrine's sacred lion head and pray for good harvests and household safety. According to the official site, a lending register from 1828 (Bunsei 11) survives, and more than 170 communities receive it.

## Visiting Tips

Shrine office reception hours are 8:30 AM–4:30 PM (October 2026 event flyer). The April 2025 "Tamamusubi" newsletter introduced a goshuin and goshuin book expressing the beauty of wisteria. The official site does not state the goshuin offering fee.

On the 1st of every month from 7:00 AM to 4:00 PM, you can draw water ("omizutori") on the right side as you face the shrine buildings. The official site says it has been valued since the early Edo period as sacred water with medicinal properties, but because it is untreated natural groundwater, it recommends boiling it before drinking. Bring your own container.

By route bus, it is an 8-minute walk from "Kisai 1-chome." By the Kazo City community bus "Kazo Kizuna-go," it is a 10-minute walk from "Kisai Sogo Shisho" (Kisai branch office). According to the official site, the service linking Shin-Koga Station West Exit and the Kisai branch office runs Monday to Saturday (except December 29 to January 3).

## Annual Festivals

- **Saitan-sai (January 1)**: The first shrine visit of the year.
- **Early Spring Festival (初春祭, February 1)**: Kagura performance and a daruma market.
- **Setsubun Festival (February)**: Prayers to ward off misfortune and bean throwing.
- **Ouma Kuguri (late April)**: A once-a-year event.
- **Spring Grand Festival (May 5)**: Kagura performance. The official site's annual event list also mentions the wisteria festival (藤祭り) alongside it.
- **Chinowa Kuguri (last Sunday of June)**
- **Summer Festival (July 15)**: Kagura performance.
- **Ginkgo illumination (late November to early December)**: According to the shrine's notice as of November 2026, it runs November 21 to December 6, 5:00–8:00 PM. It may be extended depending on leaf fall, or canceled in case of rain.
- **Annual Grand Festival (December 1)**: Kagura performance.
- **Oshishi-sama (January to November)**: The lion head is lent out to communities.

Dates may change, so check the shrine's official site and official X account for the latest.

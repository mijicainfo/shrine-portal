## History

Takayama Inari Shrine is an Inari shrine in Ushigata-cho, Tsugaru, Aomori Prefecture, close to the Shichiri Nagahama beach on the Sea of Japan. According to the Japanese Wikipedia, its name is read "Takayama Inari Jinja." The official site writes it 高山稲荷神社 in page titles and elsewhere, and also 髙山稲荷神社 (with the "ladder" variant of the first character) in its logo and notices; the Aomori Prefecture tourism website notes that the ladder form is the formal one.

The official site names three enshrined deities: Omiyame no Mikoto (also called Ame-no-Uzume no Mikoto), Satahiko no Mikoto (also called Sarutahiko no Mikoto) and Ukanomitama no Mikoto. They are introduced, in that order, as a deity of family harmony and success in the performing arts, a deity of purification and guidance (opening the way), and a deity who protects the harvest of the five grains, food, clothing and shelter, and prosperity in one's livelihood.

The year of founding is unclear, but according to the official site, the shrine is said to have been founded by the Ando, a powerful clan that ruled this area from the Kamakura into the Muromachi period. The tradition of Sanno Shrine, a shrine within the grounds, says that a great sacred site where temples stood around 山王坊日吉神社 flourished as a place of prayer for the Ando clan, but was burned down in an attack by Nanbu forces around 1443 (Kakitsu 3) or 1432 (Eikyo 4). At that time, it is said, the great deity Sanno gave off a golden light and came down like a shooting star to rest on the sacred ground of Takayama.

According to the Inari shrine's tradition, in 1701 (Genroku 14) the Ako domain was abolished over the sword-wounding incident involving its lord, Asano Takuminokami Naganori, inside Edo Castle. A retainer, Terasaka Sangoro (寺坂三五郎), took up the sacred spirit of the Inari deity enshrined in the domain's castle and wandered with it; by way of the Hirosaki castle town in Tsugaru he moved to Ajigasawa, where he prospered as a brewer under the trade name "Akoya." Later, when his descendant was moving, an oracle said to enshrine the deity at the sacred ground of Takayama, and it was transferred there. The official site sums up that Sanno Shrine was enshrined first, that the Inari shrine founded in the Edo period flourished as Inari worship grew, and that the original Sanno shrine is thought to have receded.

## Highlights

According to the Aomori Prefecture tourism website, a flight of 96 stone steps lies beyond the approach, and the gentler onna-zaka slope is recommended for those unsure of their legs. At the top, on high ground, stands the worship hall. Descending the steep stairs beyond it, the view opens onto the sacred garden with Ryujingu and the senbon torii, or "thousand torii gates." Ryujingu stands beside Ryujin Pond, and an arched bridge leads toward the torii.

The vermilion gates wind in a snaking line, and the same website says that seen from the viewing platform at the far end they look like a red dragon. The line of gates is said to have begun with offerings by local farmers, and to have taken its winding shape because it was difficult to line them up straight in the garden. As for the number of gates, the official FAQ keeps it "a secret" so that visitors can enjoy counting them.

Near the viewing platform stand many fox statues. According to the same website, they are statues from Inari shrines around the country that finished their service and were placed here after a prayer rite, and they are said to have gradually gathered from around the Meiji era. The official grounds map also marks Sanno Shrine, Shinmeigu, Myobu-sha and several Inari shrines.

## Visiting Tips

- **Hours**: The shrine is open every day, but gate times differ by season and by page. In the 2026 summer hours, prayer services run 8:30 AM–4:00 PM (last reception 3:30 PM).
- **Prayer services**: You can apply on the day; groups need to contact the shrine in advance. According to the official FAQ there is no set fee, though many people offer ¥5,000 or more.
- **Telephone**: On November 7, 2026, the shrine announced that strong winds had cut the phone line and its telephone was out of service. As of November 2026, no follow-up on restoration could be found on the official site; the notice directs inquiries to the site's contact form.
- **Bears**: On July 21, 2026, a bear cub was sighted in Ushigata-cho, Tsugaru, about 4 km from the shrine. While stating that there had been no sightings on the grounds at that time, the shrine urged visitors not to walk from the bus stop. Check the latest information before you set out.
- **Winter**: A February 2026 heavy-snow notice said that nearby roads had become narrow and that the steep Prefectural Road 228 carried a risk of vehicles getting stuck; it asked drivers to fit winter tires and dress warmly. Parts of the sacred garden and senbon torii may be temporarily closed for snow removal, and the shrine says it will report this in the Stories of its <a href="https://www.instagram.com/takayamainari/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official Instagram</a>.
- **Manners and facilities**: Pets are not allowed. Observe good manners when taking photos and be considerate of other worshippers. The November 2026 Shichi-Go-San notice says there is "only one" restroom, on the right as you go up the stairs, and none inside the worship hall or deeper in the grounds.

## Annual Festivals

The main rites listed on the official site are as follows. There is also a Toka-sai on the 10th of every month, a day known in Tsugaru as "Oinari-san's day."

- **January–February**: Saitan-sai (January 1), Genshi-sai (January 3), Setsubun-sai (the day before the first day of spring), Hatsu-uma-sai (the first Day of the Horse in February), Kigen-sai (February 11), Tencho-sai (February 23)
- **Spring Grand Festival (10th day of the 3rd lunar month)**: For 2026, the Kaijin Shogei-sai (a rite welcoming the sea deity) was announced for April 25 on Shichiri Nagahama, the Kinen-sai and Kamiuranai-sai for the 26th, and the Ryujingu annual festival for the 27th.
- **April–June**: Showa-sai (April 29), Traffic Safety Prayer Festival (a Sunday in June), Summer Great Purification (June 30)
- **Summer**: Summer Grand Festival (10th day of the 6th lunar month), Chinkon-sai (August 10)
- **Annual grand festival (September 25)**: An eve festival is held the day before.
- **Autumn to year-end**: Niiname-sai (November 23), Year-end Great Purification (December 30)

Dates may change from year to year, so check the notices on the official site.

## Nearby Food & Sightseeing

- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Takayama%20Small%20Park%20Cheseborough%20Memorial%20Tsugaru%20Aomori" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Takayama Small Park (Cheseborough Memorial Park)</strong></a>: On a hill in front of the great torii. According to the official grounds guide, the American ship Cheseborough ran aground off Shichiri Nagahama in October 1889 (Meiji 22), and people of Shariki Village rescued 4 of the 23 crew members. A memorial to those lost and a commemorative monument stand in the park.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Shichiri%20Nagahama%20Beach%20Tsugaru%20Aomori" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Shichiri Nagahama</strong></a>: A beach on the Sea of Japan beside the shrine, introduced in an official notice as a sacred shore where a rite welcoming the sea deity is held in spring.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tsuru%20no%20Maihashi%20Bridge%20Tsuruta%20Aomori" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Tsuru no Maihashi Bridge</strong></a>: A 300 m wooden triple-arched bridge in the town of Tsuruta. In a model course on the Aomori Prefecture tourism website, it is about 35 minutes by car from here to the shrine.
- <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tachineputa%20Museum%20Goshogawara%20Aomori" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Tachineputa Museum</strong></a>: A facility in Goshogawara displaying giant tachineputa floats, introduced in the same model course.

## History

Nakano Hikawa Shrine stands in Higashi-Nakano 1-chome, Nakano Ward, along Yamate-dori. It is read "Nakano Hikawa-jinja" and affectionately known locally as "Ohikawa-sama." The shrine's own name is simply "Hikawa Shrine"; it is called "Nakano Hikawa Shrine" after the place name, and Nakano Ward's cultural-property list writes it as "Higashi-Nakano Hikawa Shrine." Because there are other Hikawa shrines in the ward (such as Nakano Numabukuro Hikawa Shrine), this page calls it "Nakano Hikawa Shrine (Higashi-Nakano)." It has long been revered as the chief guardian shrine of the former Nakano Village. The three enshrined deities are Susanoo no Mikoto, Kushinadahime no Mikoto, and Onamuchi no Mikoto (also known as Okuninushi no Mikoto).

According to tradition, the shrine began in 1030 (Chogen 3), when Minamoto no Yorinobu, after suppressing the Taira no Tadatsune rebellion, invited the deity of Omiya Hikawa Shrine, the Ichinomiya (chief shrine) of Musashi Province, and built a small shrine here. It is also said that in 1477 Ota Dokan prayed here for victory in his campaign against the Toshima brothers (Yasutsune and Yasuaki) and, after returning victorious, built the shrine buildings. The former administering temple was Hosenji. These are traditions of the shrine, and details vary by source.

One travel account states that the present buildings were built in 1969 to mark the centennial of the Meiji era. Some sources also say it was ranked as a village shrine in 1872 and absorbed Sanchu Shrine and Arasawa Shrine in 1911, but these dates could not be confirmed from the shrine's own information.

TV Tokyo's "Shutsubotsu! Ad-machic Tengoku" featured the shrine at No. 4 in its Higashi-Nakano episode broadcast on February 19, 2022 (according to the program's official page). The page says the shrine was founded in the Heian period as the guardian shrine of the former Nakano Village, and that since a station-front plaza was completed in 2015, the festival uses that space, where six neighborhood mikoshi gather as a regular event (information as of the broadcast in 2022).

## Highlights

There are two approaches: the main approach, which runs straight through the trees from the residential street to the south, and a west approach facing Yamate-dori. The main approach is a stone-paved path with a torii and a shrine-name stone, and it feels quiet and removed from the surrounding houses. The buildings are vermilion-toned, and the grounds also have a kagura-den (hall for sacred dance).

The grounds hold several Nakano Ward cultural properties. According to the ward's official list, items located at 1-11-1 Higashi-Nakano include four registered tangible cultural properties (a Koshin monument, the "Nakano Koen no Ki" stele, the torii donated by Nabeya Kan'emon, and the paving-stone memorial of Kakujun; all July 1994) and four designated tangible cultural properties (a pair of komainu, a water basin, a stone-bridge memorial, and five strength stones; all November 1997). The Koshin monument carries carvings of the three wise monkeys. "Hikawa Shrine in Higashi-Nakano" is also listed on the ward's cultural-property signboard list as a certified tourism resource. Visitor accounts mention several pairs of komainu in the grounds, so comparing them is part of the fun.

Subsidiary shrines include Kitano Shrine, 御嶽神社 (reading unconfirmed), Inari Shrine, and Shiogama Shrine. One source also notes a pre-war naval mine on display (its origin is described differently by different sources). Behind the main hall is Nakano Ward's Hikawa Park.

## Visiting Tips

**Hours and fees**: The grounds are free to enter. Gate hours and shrine-office hours could not be confirmed from official information; for certainty, call the shrine office (03-3361-2465) before you go.

**Goshuin**: These appear to be suspended at present. A 2023 visitor report mentions a notice at the shrine office reading "suspended for the time being," and a February 2024 note reports a notice saying there are no goshuin and a priest saying there were no plans to offer them. If a goshuin is the purpose of your visit, please check with the shrine office first.

**Access**: It is about a 5-minute walk from Higashi-Nakano Station (JR Chuo-Sobu Line, Toei Oedo Line) and about 5–7 minutes from Nakano-sakaue Station (Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line, Toei Oedo Line). Sources disagree on parking (free parking versus none), so arriving by train is recommended; if driving, check with the shrine office first.

## Annual Festivals

- **Reitaisai (grand annual festival; mid-September)**: An autumn festival, said to be one of the larger ones in Nakano Ward, with mikoshi from the parish neighborhoods of the former Nakano Village. Local media announced 2026 as Saturday–Sunday, September 19–20 (Nakano Keizai Shimbun reported the festival on the 20th), and a report says it was held on Sunday, September 14 in 2025. Dates change from year to year. More than 20 mikoshi were reported from the former Nakano Village area, joint mikoshi processions take place in various places, and events are held on the kagura-den in the grounds. TV Tokyo's "Shutsubotsu! Ad-machic Tengoku" (Higashi-Nakano episode, February 19, 2022) noted that the station-front plaza completed in 2015 is used for the festival, where six neighborhood mikoshi gather (according to the program's official page).

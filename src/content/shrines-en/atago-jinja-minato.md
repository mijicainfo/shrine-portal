## History

Atago Shrine stands on the summit of Atago Hill, said to be 26 meters above sea level, in Atago 1-chome, Minato Ward, Tokyo. According to the official site, it was enshrined in 1603 (Keicho 8), by order of Tokugawa Ieyasu, who had set up the shogunate in Edo, as a fire-protection deity. The chief deity is Homusubi no Mikoto, the fire deity. The accompanying deities are Mizuhanome no Mikoto (water), Oyamazumi no Mikoto (mountains), and Yamato Takeru no Mikoto (martial virtue), together with Shogun Jizo-son and Fugen Daibosatsu (official site).

The official site says that in Keicho 15 the main shrine, Nio gate and other buildings were erected with donations from the shogun's family, and that the shogunate's reverence was so deep that funds were granted for festivals. Later the shrine burned down entirely in a great fire of Edo, and the main hall, offering hall, worship hall and shrine office were rebuilt in September of Meiji 10. After the Great Kanto Earthquake (September 1, Taisho 12) and the air raid of May 24, Showa 20, the buildings burned down except for Tarobo Shrine, and the main hall, offering hall, worship hall and others were rebuilt in September of Showa 33 with donations from parishioners, as the site records. Because many shrines share this name, the official site also asks visitors to confirm that this is the Atago Shrine in Tokyo (Minato Ward).

## Highlights

The great highlight is the steep front staircase, the "Shusse no Ishidan" (Stairs of Success; also called Otoko-zaka). The official site says it has "86 steps." Its name comes from a story that the official site tells: in the 11th year of Kanei, on the way back from a visit to Zojoji Temple, the third shogun Tokugawa Iemitsu saw the plum trees on Atago Hill and ordered someone to fetch the blossoms by horse. Magaki Heikuro, a retainer of the Marugame domain in Shikoku, rode up and down the steps and presented the blossoms, and was praised as "the best horseman in Japan." The tale is known from kodan storytelling, but the official site also lists people who later succeeded in riding up and down the Otoko-zaka after the Edo period, in Meiji 15, Taisho 14 and Showa 57. In front of the worship hall, on the left, stands a plum tree said to be the one Heikuro presented. To the right of the stairs is the gentler "Onna-zaka."

At the top of the stairs stands the first torii, and a triangulation point is to the right once you reach the top. The official site describes Atago Hill as the highest hill in the 23 wards among those with natural terrain (the elevation is written both as 26 meters and as 25.7 meters). The grounds also hold the vermilion gate (shinmon), the "Maneki-ishi" stone said to bring good fortune to those who stroke it, a pond with carp, and the subsidiary shrines Tarobo, Fukuju Inari, Benzaiten and Daikokuten. The official site presents the grounds as a place near Tokyo's office district where you can enjoy the seasons: cherry blossoms in spring, cicadas in summer, and autumn leaves.

## Visiting Tips

For benefits, the official site lists "things related to fire, fire prevention and disaster prevention," "printing and computers," "business prosperity," and "romance, marriage and good relationships." The name "Shusse" (success) comes from the story of the stairs and does not appear in the benefits list on the official site. The official site gives the way to pray as "two bows, two claps, one bow." The front doors of the shrine building are opened at New Year and on the monthly festival days of the 1st, 15th and 24th, when you can pray from closer, according to the official site.

Visiting hours are not stated on the official site (GO TOKYO lists 9:00 AM–4:00 PM). Prayer services are accepted 10:00 AM–3:00 PM, and the official site says company-related prayers, purification of bad luck, first shrine visits for babies, and the like need advance booking. As for goshuin, the official site does not state whether they are offered, the fee, or the hours, so this is unconfirmed as of November 2026. Please ask the shrine office (03-3431-0327).

It is a short 5-minute walk from Kamiyacho Station or Toranomon Hills Station, and 20 minutes on foot from Shimbashi Station. The parking lot is worshippers-only and listed as 9:00 AM–4:00 PM, and parking for other purposes is reported to the police. The stairs are very steep, so if you are not confident on your feet, the gentler Onna-zaka on the right is the safer choice.

## Annual Festivals

These are the main events on the official site's festival page. Dates can change by year, so please check the official site for the latest.

- **Saitan-sai (January 1, 8:00 AM)**: Prayers for a safe new year, national peace, abundant harvests and business prosperity.
- **Nanakusa Hitaki Festival (January 7, 11:00 AM)**: Last year's amulets and charms are burned, and people warm themselves at the fire to pray for good health; the official site says seven-herb rice porridge is served to attendees.
- **Setsubun Ofukuwake Festival (February 3, 12:00 noon)**: A rite to purify bad luck and invite good fortune.
- **Sennichi-mairi and Hozuki Ennichi (June 23–24)**: It has long been believed that passing through the ring of reeds (chinowa) set up before the hall brings a thousand days' worth of benefits. The official site says the hozuki (ground-cherry) fair began at Atago Shrine. On June 24 at 11:00 AM there is the Chusai-shiki, a rite to purify half a year of bad luck (Nagoshi no Harae).
- **Shusse no Ishidan Festival (September 22–24, every other year)**: The shrine's largest festival, in which the portable shrine goes up and down the Shusse no Ishidan once every two years. The Taisai-shiki is held on September 24 at 11:00 AM. The official site's top page showed the September 2026 schedule (the eve on the 22nd, the portable shrine procession on the 23rd, the Taisai-shiki on the 24th).
- **Joya-sai and Kagami-biraki (December 31, 11:45 PM)**: Held by candlelight, with sake from a barrel served after the rite.
- **Cherry blossom viewing (late March–early April)**: Not a festival, but the official site says the whole hill appears covered in cherry blossoms.

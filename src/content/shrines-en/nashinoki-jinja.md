## History

Nashinoki Shrine (Nashinoki-jinja) is in Somedono-cho, Kamigyo-ku, Kyoto, and is also known as "Hagi no Miya" (the Bush Clover Shrine) after the bush clover that blooms in its grounds. According to the history on the official site, it was founded in October 1885 (Meiji 18) with Sanjo Sanetsumu as its enshrined deity. The name is said to come from the former Nashinoki-cho district, where the Sanjo family residence stood, and the official site says the shrine was later ranked as a Bekkaku Kanpeisha (special government shrine).

According to the official site, Sanjo Sanetsumu was a court noble who served the Emperors Kokaku, Ninko and Komei and worked to restore imperial authority in the late Edo period. His son Sanjo Sanetomi was enshrined as the second deity in 1915 (Taisho 4), at the enthronement of Emperor Taisho. The site describes him as a hero of the Meiji Restoration who went on to serve as Daijo-daijin and in other high posts.

The site of the shrine is also said to be where Fujiwara no Yoshifusa's residence, Somedono-dai, once stood, and the "Somei" water that still flows at the purification basin is said to have been used at the imperial court's dyeing workshop. The official site calls it one of Kyoto's famous waters and says it is the only one of them still flowing.

## Highlights

According to the grounds guide on the official site, the highlights are as follows. The "Somei" well at the purification basin is a famous spring with a mellow, sweet taste said to suit the tea ceremony, and visitors can still collect the water. The sacred katsura tree is nicknamed "Ai no Ki" (Tree of Love) for its heart-shaped leaves, and it is said that wishes come true if you touch it while praying. Near the worship hall stands the "Tenjo Mukyu" stone monument. It is said that Sanjo Sanetsumu wrote the divine decree of Tenjo Mukyu on a scroll, and the official site says Emperor Meiji had a rubbing made and displayed it on February 11 (today's National Foundation Day) as he prayed. There are also a monument to Dr. Hideki Yukawa, Japan's first Nobel laureate, inscribed with a poem about bush clover, and a monument to Ueda Akinari, author of "Ugetsu Monogatari."

The official site says more than 500 bush clover plants grow in the grounds and introduces the shrine as one of Kyoto's leading bush clover spots. The flower is described as the plant most often sung of in the "Manyoshu," and its slender, supple branches and delicate flowers color the grounds in autumn.

The grounds also hold a Noh stage said to have been built in 1915 (Taisho 4), a community hall (Sanshuden) and a tea room. The tea room's "Kyochu-an" was built in 1985 (Showa 60) and has been used for the monthly tea gatherings (tsukigama), which the official site says are currently suspended.

## Visiting Tips

The shrine office is open 9:00 AM–4:30 PM, and no goshuin or amulets are issued from 12:15 to 1:15 PM (official site). Prayer services run 9:00 AM–4:00 PM, require a reservation, and start from ¥5,000 for individuals. Amulets include the Hagi, illness-recovery, academic, good-fortune and misfortune-warding, health, traffic-safety and matchmaking amulets (¥800 each, among others).

The regular goshuin is ¥500, and from Sunday, November 1, 2026 the special "cat goshuin (autumn leaves)" (¥600) is also available. According to the shrine's notices, the autumn 2026 "Kyoto Kodawari Goshuin Three-Shrine Pilgrimage" runs from September 15 to November 30, and collecting goshuin at the three shrines earns a commemorative goshuin (one per person; no mail orders).

You can collect Somei water from the tap at the purification basin, but large containers are not allowed and containers may not be washed at the basin. Parking for worshippers is limited to about 3 spaces, and parking for collecting water or sightseeing is not allowed, so public transport is recommended. The shrine is about a 15-minute walk from Jingu-Marutamachi or Demachiyanagi Station on the Keihan Line.

## Annual Festivals

- **Hagi Festival (September)**: According to the official site, haiku and tanka poem slips are tied to the bush clover each September when it is in full bloom, and the festival is held around the third or fourth Sunday. Offerings include dance, traditional Japanese music, archery, iaido and a tea gathering. The site gives no date for any particular year.
- **Kyoto Kodawari Goshuin Three-Shrine Pilgrimage (autumn 2026)**: September 15 to November 30, 2026, according to the official notice.
- **Special cat goshuin (autumn leaves) (from November 1, 2026)**: The end date is not given in the notice.
- **Cleaning service by the Somei Water Preservation Society**: According to the official site, volunteers clean the well and grounds every month, and anyone can join.

Event dates can change from year to year, so check the notices on the official site for the latest.

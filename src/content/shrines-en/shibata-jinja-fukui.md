## History

Shibata Shrine stands on the site of Kitanosho Castle in Chuo, Fukui, near JR Fukui Station, and enshrines the Sengoku warlord Shibata Katsuie and his wife Oichi no Kata. Its name is read "Shibata-jinja" (the official site gives no furigana; the reading comes from Japanese Wikipedia and the official site's domain name, sibatajinja.jp). The official site lists the enshrined deities as Shibata Katsuie, Oichi no Kata and the martyred warriors (殉難武士). The auxiliary Sanshimai Shrine enshrines the three princesses Chacha, Hatsu and 江与 (the Fukui City tourism site writes 江, Go), and the Inari Shrine enshrines Ukanomitama no Kami, which the official site says Katsuie invited from Fushimi Inari in Kyoto as the guardian of Kitanosho Castle and the tutelary deity of the castle town.

According to the official history, after Kitanosho Castle fell on the 24th day of the 4th month of Tensho 11, a stone shrine was built on the site of the castle keep to enshrine Katsuie's spirit, and the clansmen of the time held rites there every year on April 24. In Meiji 6 the "shrine" was granted shrine rank and gained parishioners and devotees, which is described as the start of Shibata Shrine. The Fukui City tourism site "Fuku-iro," on the other hand, introduces it as founded in Meiji 23 by the former Fukui domain lord Matsudaira Shungaku and others, so the founding date differs by source. The same site explains that although it is often assumed the shrine was built where Katsuie and Oichi died, it actually began when townspeople built a stone shrine to comfort Katsuie's spirit.

According to Fuku-iro, Katsuie is said to have built Kitanosho Castle after Oda Nobunaga granted him 490,000 koku, and in 1583 (Tensho 11), attacked by the forces of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, with whom he was in conflict, he set fire to the castle and met his end together with Oichi and his clan. The castle is said to have lasted only eight years. The official site's March 2022 notice says that the re-roofing of the three shrine buildings and the enlargement of the worship hall were completed, and that the main hall's rite of enshrinement was held on October 4 of Reiwa 2. Fuku-iro also says that Katsuie and Oichi appear in the 2026 NHK taiga drama 豊臣兄弟！.

## Highlights

The grounds hold statues of Shibata Katsuie, Oichi no Kata and Oichi's three daughters (Fuku-iro). Fuku-iro says the Kitanosho Castle Ruins Museum beside the shrine preserves relics of the castle, demon-faced roof tiles, a stone pillar said to have been used in the half-stone, half-wood 九十九橋 bridge built in Katsuie's time, and the iron chains that joined the boat bridge over the Kuzuryu River. According to Japanese Wikipedia, the grounds are laid out as the Kitanosho Castle Ruins Park, where excavated stonework is displayed. The site also has a bridge modeled on the 九十九橋 and a small lawn (Fuku-iro).

The official site lists many kinds of amulets, including the ema-shaped 勝守 showing Katsuie and Oichi, the 幸福守 showing the three sisters, and the きずな守, 夫婦守 (for couples), 親子守 (parent and child) and お市御守. There is also an ema showing Katsuie, who was called "Kamewari Shibata," and a 福石守 whose inner charm is shakudani stone (笏谷石) that was used in the stone walls around the main hall. A monthly-changing five-color amulet (五色御守) is also introduced, issued from the monthly rite on the 24th. According to Fuku-iro, there is also a "Bi (Mote) Kigan" prayer inspired by Oichi's beauty, in which you write a wish on a paper from the shrine office and float it on water.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds are open to visitors freely, and the shrine office's regular hours are 9:00 AM–5:00 PM (the official site's 2026 New Year notice says "9:00 AM–5:00 PM (regular)"). The official site lists prayer service fees as 3,000 yen or more for entering the hall to worship, 7,000 yen or more for household safety, protection from misfortune, safe childbirth and similar prayers, and 10,000 yen or more for a first shrine visit for a baby (all as a donation, お志). The New Year notice says prayers are held for one party at a time and can be held at your preferred time if you book.

The goshuin and castle stamp are issued at the shrine office, and Fuku-iro recommends getting both as a set. The goshuin is ¥500 (浄書) or ¥600 (奉書) and the castle stamp is ¥1,000; the official site also shows goshuin books with designs of Katsuie, "Kamewari Shibata" and Oichi no Kata.

It is about a 2-minute walk from JR Fukui Station (official site; Fuku-iro says 5 minutes) and about 10 minutes by car from Hokuriku Expressway Fukui IC. Parking is not mentioned on the official site. Please check on site, including the free Kitanosho Castle Ruins tourist parking lot that Japanese Wikipedia mentions.

## Annual Festivals

The official site's annual events are as follows.

- **Saitan-sai (January 1)**: In the 2026 notice, it begins at 9:00 AM, and at 11:00 AM a branded dorayaki was handed out (first 150).
- **Sagicho Festival (January 15)**
- **Yakujin-sai (Setsubun) and Kamewari Shinji (February 3)**
- **Reisai, the annual festival (April 24)**: The official site says rites have been held on this day every year since the fall of the castle on the 24th day of the 4th month of Tensho 11.
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (June 30)**
- **Inari-sha Festival (September 1)**
- **Autumn Grand Festival (October 24)**
- **Toshikoshi no Oharae and Joya-sai (December 31)**: In the notice for Reiwa 8, from 3:00 PM on December 31, Reiwa 7.
- **Tsuitachi-sai (1st of every month) and Tsukinami-sai (24th of every month)**: Held at 10:00 AM each month, and you can attend if you arrive 10 minutes before the start. There is no Tsukinami-sai in April and October because those months have grand festivals.

As of November 2026, a New Year notice for Reiwa 9 (2027) was not found under "News" on the official site. Times change from year to year, so check the official site for the latest.

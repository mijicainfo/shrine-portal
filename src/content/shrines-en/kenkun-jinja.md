## History

Kenkun Jinja was founded in 1869 by imperial decree of Emperor Meiji, to honor the great achievements of Oda Nobunaga in unifying a fractured Japan. Its site was fixed atop Mt. Funaoka in Kyoto, a location tied to Nobunaga's own history, and the present shrine buildings were completed on the summit in 1880. The name "Kenkun" comes from a posthumous title granted to Nobunaga by the imperial court; the shrine is commonly read "Kenkun-jinja," though its formal reading is "Takeisao-jinja."

The principal deity is Oda Nobunaga, worshipped alongside his son and heir, Oda Nobutada, as an associated deity. The shrine honors Nobunaga's role in restoring order to a Kyoto devastated by the Onin War and laying the foundations for the unification of Japan — a fitting tribute to found at the dawn of the new Meiji era.

## Highlights

The single greatest highlight of Kenkun Jinja is its location itself, atop the 112-meter summit of Mt. Funaoka. From the grounds, visitors are treated to a sweeping panorama that takes in the thirty-six peaks of Higashiyama and the cityscape of Kyoto below, making it one of the city's finest viewpoints.

The shrine's main hall enshrines the spirits of Nobunaga and his son Nobutada, while its treasure house preserves swords and other artifacts connected to Nobunaga. The Noh play "Atsumori," said to have been performed by Nobunaga himself before he rode into the decisive Battle of Okehazama, is dedicated at the shrine every autumn during the Funaoka Grand Festival. Mt. Funaoka was itself a battlefield during the Onin War, giving visitors a chance to experience layers of history stacked one atop another.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds are open to visitors at all times, and the shrine office is open 9:00–17:00. There is no dedicated parking, so visitors arriving by car should use a nearby coin-operated lot. Both the walk from Kitaoji subway station and from the Funaokayama bus stop involve a climb, so comfortable walking shoes are recommended. For the best views, a clear morning is ideal.

## Annual Festivals

- **Funaoka Grand Festival (October 19)**: Commemorates the day Emperor Meiji posthumously granted Nobunaga the title "Kenkun." Features a dedication of the Noh play "Atsumori" along with rites connected to Nobunaga.
- **Summer Purification Rite, Chinowa-kuguri (June 30)**: A ceremony cleansing the impurities of the past half-year and praying for good health.
- **Monthly Festival (the 1st of every month)**: A regular rite of thanksgiving for the deity's blessings.
- **New Year's Festival and Hatsumode (January 1–3)**: The shrine's first visits of the new year draw large crowds.

## History

Nakano Numabukuro Hikawa Shrine is a Hikawa shrine in Numabukuro, Nakano, Tokyo, just a short walk from Numabukuro Station on the Seibu Shinjuku Line. The official site presents it as "Nakano Numabukuro Hikawa Shrine," which appears to be read "Nakano Numabukuro Hikawa-jinja" (the official site gives no hiragana reading; this follows its official romanization, "Numabukuro Hikawa" and similar). It is a different shrine from "Nakano Hikawa Shrine" in Higashi-Nakano, which this site covers on a separate page. The enshrined deity is Susanoo no Mikoto, the hero of the myth of slaying the eight-headed serpent.

According to the official history, the shrine is said to have begun in 1346 (Shohei 1, in the Nanbokucho period), when a divided spirit was received from Omiya Hikawa Shrine (now in Saitama City), the ichinomiya of Musashi Province. In April 1477 (Bunmei 9), during a battle with the Toshima clan, Ota Dokan is said to have set up his headquarters at the shrine and planted a cedar at its entrance to pray for victory. The tree later came to be called the "Dokan Cedar" and withered in 1942. All of this is the shrine's own account.

The shrine's chronology records repairs to the buildings by a local magistrate in the Shoho era (1644–48) and a rebuilding of the hall in 1880. It also notes that the hall built in 1988 was destroyed in 1990 by a radical group, that the present hall was built in August 1991, and that the large torii on the rear approach fell in the 2011 Great East Japan Earthquake and was rebuilt the following year.

In recent years, according to the shrine's official site, it has run an official collaboration with the TV anime Tokyo Revengers, handing out goshuin themed on characters who appear in a scene of the manga for which the shrine served as a model.

## Highlights

At the center of the grounds is the present main hall (built in 1991), which enshrines Susanoo no Mikoto. According to the official grounds guide, the purification fountain and the kagura hall were renovated in 2019 as a commemorative project for the Emperor's enthronement ceremonies, and the kagura hall hosts dedicatory dances and festival music at New Year's and festivals.

The best-loved feature is the rare "child-rearing komainu," which holds and soothes a baby. The official site says that stroking it three times is said to bring an easy birth and smooth child-rearing. The sacred tree, the "Three Wish Pines," is touched by many visitors who place their wishes in it. There are also a "chikara-ishi" (strength stone), once used by young men in contests of strength and carved with the weight and name of the lifter, and the site of the Dokan Cedar tied to Ota Dokan.

The grounds also hold the Tenno-sha (dedicated to Gozu Tenno, for recovery from illness), the Inari Shrine (which absorbed Okubo Inari Shrine in 1963; for rich harvests and business prosperity) and Mitake Shrine (against fire and theft). Seven Lucky Gods were enshrined in 2009, and the official site says this is the only place in Nakano Ward where all seven can be worshipped.

## Visiting Tips

The gates open at 6:00 AM and close at 6:00 PM (opening at 7:00 AM from November to February, and open all day over New Year's). The counter for amulets, talismans and goshuin is open daily 9:00 AM–4:00 PM, so visit within those hours if you want a goshuin. Prayer services require no booking from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM (last acceptance 3:30 PM) in January, February (until National Foundation Day) and November, and on weekends, public holidays and dog days outside those months. On other weekdays a reservation is needed. A service is described as taking about 20 minutes.

The regular goshuin is ¥500, and its stamp design and color change every month. Amulets are mostly ¥800 each. Limited-edition and collaboration goshuin have set distribution periods and quantities, and the official site posts the current status.

It is about a 2-minute walk from the north exit of Numabukuro Station on the Seibu Shinjuku Line. The parking lot has about 25 spaces.

## Annual Festivals

According to the official annual events page, the main events are as follows.

- **Saitan-sai (January 1)**: Festival music by local hayashi groups and a lion dance are performed, and old talismans are burned in an otakiage fire in the grounds (December 31 to January 3).
- **Genshi-sai (January 3)**: Sato-kagura is performed at the kagura hall.
- **Hatsuuma-sai (first Horse Day in February)**: A festival of the Inari subshrine on the grounds.
- **Kinen-sai (February 17)**: A prayer for rich harvests and industrial prosperity.
- **Reitaisai (first Saturday and Sunday of June)**: The annual grand festival, with a mikoshi procession and various dedicatory events (the ceremony is on Sunday).
- **Tenno-sai (June 22)**: The festival of the Tenno-sha subshrine, praying for protection from epidemics; red-bean rice is handed out.
- **Nagoshi no Oharae (June 30)**: A purification rite with a chinowa reed ring to pass through. In 2026 it was announced for 5:00 PM.
- **Shichi-Go-San (November 15)**: The official site also has an announcement about 2026 prayers.
- **Niiname-sai (November 23)**: A harvest thanksgiving festival.
- **Toshikoshi no Oharae (December 30)**: A purification rite in which visitors pass through a chinowa ring, praying for good health.

Dates can change from year to year, so check the official site for the latest.

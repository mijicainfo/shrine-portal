## History

Gokonomiya Shrine (Gokonomiya Jinja) in Fushimi Ward, Kyoto, has long been the local shrine of the old Fushimi town area, and locals affectionately call it "Gokon-san." According to the official site, the chief deity is Empress Jingu, called "the greatest guardian deity of safe childbirth in Japan," and the shrine also enshrines Emperor Chuai and Emperor Ojin together with six other deities: Ube Daimyojin (Takeuchi no Sukune), Takisai no Kami, Kawakami Daimyojin (Toyotamahime), Kora Daimyojin, Wakamiya (Emperor Nintoku) and Shirakiku Daimyojin. Because she is said to have led an expedition to the Korean Peninsula while pregnant, then returned safely and bore and raised Emperor Ojin, she is revered as a deity of safe childbirth and child rearing.

The year of founding is unknown. According to the Gokonomiya Engi, fragrant water welled up in the precinct on the ninth day of the ninth month of 862 (Jogan 4), and Emperor Seiwa bestowed the name "Gokonomiya" on the shrine, as the shrine tells it. The Kashii-gu Hennenki, held by Kashii Shrine, also says that in 823 (Konin 14), following an oracle from Empress Jingu, a shrine building was repaired on this site and a chinquapin tree was planted as the sacred tree (both per the official history).

The shrine has deep ties to Fushimi Castle. The official history says that in 1590 Toyotomi Hideyoshi, after destroying the Hojo clan of Odawara, prayed here for victory and presented a sword and a written prayer, and that in 1594, when Fushimi Castle was built, the shrine was enshrined inside the castle as a guardian against the unlucky northeast direction. In 1605 Tokugawa Ieyasu returned the shrine to its original site and built the main hall, and in 1622 Tokugawa Yorifusa, the first lord of the Mito Tokugawa family, received the castle's main gate and donated it as the shrine's front gate, it explains. During the Battle of Toba-Fushimi in 1868 it served as the base of the new government forces (Satsuma domain), but is said to have escaped the fighting.

## Highlights

The main hall was built in 1605 on Tokugawa Ieyasu's orders as a large five-bay "nagare-zukuri" building, with a cypress-bark roof and richly colored carvings. The official site says it was designated an Important Cultural Property in 1985 (Showa 60), and that repairs begun in 1990 restored its vivid colors after about 390 years. The worship hall (haiden) is a "wari-haiden" said to have been donated by Tokugawa Yorinobu in 1625 (Kan'ei 2), and is a Kyoto Prefecture-designated cultural property. The official site highlights the carvings under the front karahafu gable and the tradition that it was granted from Fushimi Castle's carriage entrance.

The front gate (omote-mon) is a "yakui-mon" gate said to have been donated as Fushimi Castle's main gate, and is a national Important Cultural Property (designated in 1906 per the official site). The brackets across its front are carved with the Chinese Twenty-Four Paragons of Filial Piety. The "Ishii no Gokosui" spring that gave the shrine its name is counted among the Seven Famous Waters of Fushimi and is one of Japan's 100 Famous Waters selected by the Ministry of the Environment. According to the official site it dried up after the Meiji era and was restored in 1982.

The grounds guide lists a stone garden, Toshogu, Toyokuni-sha, a Noh stage and an ema hall, among others. The Momoyama Tenmangu on the grounds enshrines Sugawara no Michizane and is presented as a place to pray for academic success and exam success (amulets, goshuin and prayers are handled at the Momoyama Tenmangu's own office). Some guides link the stone garden to Kobori Enshu, but the official site gives no detailed explanation.

## Visiting Tips

The shrine office is open 9:00 AM–4:00 PM, and prayer services are accepted 9:00 AM–3:30 PM (official site). Safe-childbirth prayers are accepted every day, and you cannot book a time. On Taian days, weekends, holidays and Dog Days waits can exceed an hour, so allow plenty of time. The offering is ¥8,000, with options that add an Iwata-obi or maternity belt. Prayers for Shichi-Go-San (by advance reservation outside November), first shrine visits and protection from misfortune are also accepted.

Goshuin are given out at the shrine office. The official site does not state the fee for the regular goshuin, but embroidered, gold-foil and paper-cut limited editions are announced from time to time. An official notice dated August 9, 2026 also lists goshuin for "BLEACH: Thousand-Year Blood War" (three types) and asks visitors to check stock beforehand.

If you come by car, you can use the parking lots on the grounds, except during the Shinko-sai festival. The Gokosui spring can be drawn 7:00 AM–7:00 PM. By train, it is about 4 minutes on foot from Kintetsu Momoyama-Goryomae Station and about 6 minutes from Keihan Fushimi-Momoyama Station.

## Annual Festivals

The official annual event table lists the events below. Some weekdays in the table do not match the real calendar, so treat the dates as a guide and check the official site for the latest schedule.

- **Wakamizu Shinji (January 1, midnight) and Nanakusa Shinji (January 7)**: Per the official event table. A note says serving of seven-herb rice porridge has been discontinued.
- **Omiyumi-hajime Shinji (February 10)**
- **Annual festival (April 17)**
- **Chinowa Shinji (July 31)**: According to the official site, the summer purification rite, which most shrines hold on June 30, is held here on July 31 following old custom. In 2026 it was announced for 3:00 PM in front of the main hall and the large chinowa ring.
- **Sacred Noh (Rosoku-no, candlelight Noh) (September 23)**: A dedication performed on the Noh stage. In 2026 it began at 6:30 PM, and the official site says tickets sold out.
- **Shinko-sai (early October; October 3–11 in 2026)**: The official history describes it as the annual festival of the guardian deity of the nine villages of Fushimi, long known as the "Fushimi Matsuri," and says it enjoys a reputation as the greatest festival in southern Kyoto (the shrine's own description). In 2026 a children's sumo tournament and the Hanagasa Sosangu procession were scheduled for the 3rd, a Hanagasa parade on the 10th and the portable-shrine procession on the 11th, and an unveiling of the Great Lion, repaired for the first time in about 50 years, was also announced. Street stalls are open every day of the festival.
- **Ohitaki-sai (November 15)**
- **Jozo-hatsu Shinji (the Day of the Hare in the middle of December)**
- **Tsukigama tea gathering (November 15, 2026)**: A tea gathering hosted by the Gokosui Preservation Society at Kyukoken (¥1,500 per seat, per the official site).

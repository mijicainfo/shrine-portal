## History

Kamosu Shrine (Kamosu-jinja) stands in the Oba district of Matsue, Shimane Prefecture, and is affectionately called "Kamosu-san" or "Oba no Omiya-san" by locals. The principal deity is Izanami no Okami, with Izanagi no Okami enshrined alongside. Shrine tradition says that Ame no Hohi no Mikoto, ancestor of the Izumo Kokuso (hereditary chiefs of the Izumo region), descended to this land and founded the shrine. However, it does not appear in the Izumo no Kuni Fudoki or in the Engishiki register of shrines, and the actual founding is thought to date to around the mid-Heian period. Its earliest written mention is a 1208 document from the Kamakura shogunate.

Oba was a political and transport center of ancient Izumo, close to the provincial capital and provincial temple, and is said to be where the Izumo Kokuso lived. The Kokuso served at this shrine for 25 generations, but after 798 they moved to Kizuki, where Izumo Taisha now stands. Even so, the Kamibi Sozoku Ceremony (Ohichi-shiki) held when the Kokuso office passed to a new head, and the ancient Niinamesai, continued to be held here until the early Meiji era. In the medieval period the shrine was called one of the "Ryo Kamosu" (the two Kamosu) together with Manai Shrine, and it counts among the Six Shrines of Ou, which have close ties to the Kokuso family.

## Highlights

The main attraction is the National Treasure main hall (together with its inner shrine and old central pillar timber). It was designated a National Treasure on March 29, 1952. Rebuilt in 1583 in the traditional manner, it is regarded as the oldest surviving example of Taisha-zukuri, a style found only in the eastern half of Shimane (the old Izumo province). It measures about 5.5 meters square, with a gabled, entrance-in-the-gable layout facing east and a roof of horse-chestnut shingles, and is valued for preserving a form of Taisha-zukuri older than the main hall at Izumo Taisha. Its interior is of the "mezukuri" (female) type, the reverse of Izumo Taisha's "ozukuri" (male) layout. The interior is decorated with wall paintings attributed to Kano Sanraku and Tosa Mitsuoki, but the inside is normally not open to visitors, and the doors are said to be opened on festival days.

The approach stairs, built of piled-up natural stones, have large steps and a moss-covered look that adds to the solemn atmosphere. Among the subsidiary shrines in the grounds, the Kifune and Inari shrines' main hall (a two-bay nagare-zukuri building) is also a nationally designated Important Cultural Property, alongside Kumano, Ise, and Kizuki shrines and others. There is also the Okama-no-miya, based on the tradition that Ame no Hohi no Mikoto arrived riding a sacred cauldron. The shrine crest is a "yu" character inside a double tortoiseshell pattern. According to Shimane tourism information, the shrine is said to bring blessings for matchmaking, having children, and safe childbirth.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds are open at all hours and there is no admission fee. Goshuin (¥300, handwritten) are handled at the shrine office, but it keeps no fixed hours and may be unattended, so if you want to be sure of getting one, call ahead (0852-21-6379). The approach stairs have large steps, so comfortable shoes are a good idea.

From JR Matsue Station, take the Matsue City Bus bound for "Kanbe no Sato" to the last stop, then walk about 3 minutes (service is infrequent, so check the timetable beforehand). You can also take the Ichibata Bus bound for "Yakumo" to the "Fudoki-no-Oka Iriguchi" stop and walk about 10 minutes, or take a taxi (about 15 minutes). If driving, there is free parking on the grounds.

## Annual Festivals

- **Kinensai and Kamibi Sozoku (Hitsugi) ceremony (April 18)**: The day of the spring Kinensai, also given as the date of the Kamibi Sozoku rite. The Kamibi Sozoku ceremony is known as a special rite performed when the Izumo Kokuso passes the office to a successor.
- **Reisai, the annual grand festival (October 18)**: The shrine's main annual festival.
- **Shinzaisai (November 11)**: Given as the date of the Shinzaisai.
- **Niinamesai and Okama Shinji (December 13)**: The Niinamesai and the Okama Shinji rite associated with the Okama-no-miya.

Note: Dates and viewing access may change. Please check the latest information before your visit.

## History

Kanahebisui Shrine stands in Iwanuma, Miyagi Prefecture. The official site gives no reading for the shrine's name, but Japanese Wikipedia reads it "Kanahebisui-jinja," and the official site gives the reading "Kanahebi Benzaiten-sha" for the Benzaiten shrine within its grounds. According to the official site, the enshrined deity is Kanahebi Daijin (Mizuhayame no Mikoto), with Onamuchi no Mikoto (Okuninushi) and Sukunabikona no Mikoto enshrined alongside. It is presented as a water-deity shrine set at the mouth of the valley called Kanahebi-zawa, where water flows out onto the Sendai Plain; since the water deity appears in the form of a snake or dragon, it has long drawn faith as a shrine of the snake deity. The official site says it is known for the blessings of "business prosperity, financial well-being and protection from misfortune with good fortune," and also lists recovery from illness, a bountiful harvest, maritime safety and a good catch.

The official history states that the founding date is unknown, and that when people began farming here, a water deity was thought to have been enshrined where water flows from the mountains onto the plain. As for the name, it says that in the mid-Heian period, in the reign of Emperor Ichijo, Sanjo Kokaji Munechika of Kyoto came to this area in search of fine water after receiving an imperial order to forge a sword. Moved by the water beside the water shrine, Munechika prayed and began to forge the blade, but the croaking of frogs broke his concentration. When he made a snake figure and released it in the paddy, the frogs fell silent and he forged the sword successfully. In gratitude he offered the snake figure, and it is said that from then on it was revered as the object of worship and the shrine took the name Kanahebisui Shrine. All of this is the shrine's own account.

## Highlights

Passing through the main torii at the entrance, visitors are greeted by the wisteria trellis of the "Kyuryu no Fuji," which the official site says is said to be 300 years old. The wisteria is typically at its best from early to mid-May. Within the grounds is the "Jamon Stone," a stone with a natural pattern that recalls a snake, described as a donated stone. The grounds also hold the "Goreichi," where, it is said, seven-colored water once welled up and was used to divine the will of the gods.

At the Kanahebi Benzaiten shrine in the grounds, Benzaiten, a goddess of water, is enshrined and revered for wealth, wisdom and improvement in the arts and crafts. According to the official site, the statue is unveiled once a year at the annual festival on the first Sunday of July. At the purification area and sacred-water spot you can wash coins and draw a "water fortune slip," and purification rites are also held from spring to autumn.

During the spring flower festival, the grounds hold a peony garden that the official site says blooms with 100 varieties and more than 1,500 plants, and a herbaceous peony garden that opened in Reiwa 7 (2025). An admission fee for upkeep is charged at peak bloom, but the amount is not given on the official site. Yama no Yashiro (Okuni-sha) enshrines the co-enshrined deity Onamuchi no Mikoto as a shrine for good relationships, and the viewing plaza overlooks the town of Iwanuma.

## Visiting Tips

The grounds can be visited 24 hours a day, and the shrine office is open 8:30 AM–4:00 PM (may change with the season or festivals). Amulets, goshuin and prayer requests are handled within office hours. The shrine asks visitors not to walk pets in the grounds, and says some places have steps or slopes (official site).

Prayers start from ¥3,000, and from ¥5,000 for car purification, Shichi-Go-San and kamidana soul-enshrining. Requests are accepted at the shrine front 8:30 AM–3:50 PM, and prayers are held on the hour from 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM. The official site asks for advance reservation by LINE in principle (for members of the supporters' association), but you can also apply at the shrine without a reservation. Telephone reservations are not accepted.

The goshuin are available at the shrine office from 8:30 AM to 4:00 PM, including monthly goshuin, washi-paper goshuin and a Benzaiten goshuin. The fee is not stated on the official site, so please check at the amulet counter. The official notices for November 2026 said that limited goshuin were distributed on the "Mi no Hi" festival day, one type and one sheet per person.

It is about 40 minutes on foot from Iwanuma Station, or about 10 minutes by taxi. During the flower festival, New Year's visits and festivals it gets crowded, with traffic restrictions and temporary parking lots, so check the "News" page on the official site for the latest.

## Annual Festivals

The following is the official site's guide to annual events (check each year's notices for festival times and votive events).

- **Saitan-sai (January 1, from midnight), New Year's visit (January 1–14) and Donto-sai (January 14)**: At Donto-sai, old amulets, charms and New Year decorations are burned.
- **Yakubarai Happo-yoke Grand Festival (January 22 to end of February)**: A grand festival to purify the misfortunes of unlucky years and directional taboos.
- **Flower Festival (late April to late May) and Spring Grand Festival (May 15–21)**: In 2026 the flower festival ran from April 25 to May 31.
- **Natsumode (mid-June to end of August)**: In 2026, Period I ran June 12 to July 5 and Period II ran July 18 to August 16. Votive performances and other events are held on the Kagura stage.
- **Kanahebi Benzaiten Festival (first Sunday of July)**: The statue is unveiled once a year.
- **Akimode (mid-September to mid-October) and Autumn Festival (second Sunday of October)**: In 2026 Akimode ran September 19 to October 12, and the Autumn Festival was held on October 11.
- **Shichi-Go-San visit (from early November)**: Centered on November 15, with prayers also accepted at any time afterward (official notice, November 2026).
- **Kamidana Fair (December 5–21, 2026)**: An event announced in the official notices of November 2026, held at the outer-grounds gallery.
- **Saki-mode (from early December) and Year-end Great Purification (late December)**: End-of-year events.

Dates change from year to year, so check the "Festivals" page and the "News" page on the official site for the latest.

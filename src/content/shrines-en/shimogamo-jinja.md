## History

Shimogamo Jinja's formal name is Kamo Mioya Jinja, and together with Kamigamo Jinja it is one of Kyoto's oldest shrines. Kamotaketsunumi no Mikoto is enshrined in the west main hall, and his child deity, Tamayorihime no Mikoto, in the east main hall. Together with Kamigamo Jinja, the two are known as the "Kamo Shrines," and have long been deeply revered as guardian deities of the Heian capital. In 1994, the shrine was registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto."

The shrine enshrines the tutelary deity of the Kamo clan, who is said to have ruled the area of Yamashiro Province even before the capital moved to Kyoto. After the move, the shrine was held in particularly high regard by the imperial court as a guardian of the capital, and was counted among the "Upper Seven" of the twenty-two shrines (Nijūni-sha), a rank second only to Ise Jingu. The shrine is also known for its long tradition of periodic rebuilding (shikinen sengu), and its present buildings are kept in beautiful condition through regular restoration.

## Highlights

The "Tadasu no Mori," a primeval forest that envelops the shrine grounds, covers about 120,000 square meters — roughly the size of three Tokyo Domes. Despite sitting in the middle of the city, it has preserved ancient, untouched nature, making it one of Kyoto's most soothing spaces.

## Visiting Tips

"Aioi no Yashiro," known as a shrine for good relationships, is a popular spot especially among women visitors. The Aoi Matsuri, held in May, is widely known as one of Kyoto's three great festivals.

## Annual Festivals

- **Aoi Matsuri (May 15)**: One of Kyoto's three great festivals, a grand procession of participants in Heian-period court dress traveling from Shimogamo Jinja to Kamigamo Jinja, one of the city's most celebrated events.
- **Mitarashi Festival / Ashitsuke Shinji (July)**: Held around the midsummer Day of the Ox, worshippers wade knee-deep into the Mitarashi Pond to pray for good health, a quintessential summer tradition in Kyoto.
- **Yatori Shinji (August)**: Held at the end of summer, in which human-shaped paper effigies (hitogata) are set adrift on the Mitarashi River as bare-chested men compete, torches in hand, to grab arrows from the water.

## History

Ogu Hachiman Shrine stands in Nishi-Ogu 3-chome, Arakawa Ward, Tokyo, and its name is read "Ogu Hachiman Jinja." Arakawa Ward's website introduces it simply as "Hachiman Jinja," and the shrine's own site calls it the guardian shrine of the whole Ogu area. The deity enshrined is Emperor Ojin (Hondawake no Mikoto), along with others.

The year of founding is unknown. The Arakawa Ward website says it likely goes back to around 1312, when the Ogu area was donated to Tsurugaoka Hachimangu in Kamakura, and that ridgepole tablets kept at the shrine confirm that a building was built in 1385. The shrine's official site likewise notes that the oldest surviving tablet bears that date, which means Hachiman was enshrined here at least before the Nanbokucho period. The shrine has been loved locally as a god of farming, industry and commerce, and the ward lists blessings including academic success, traffic safety, business prosperity, protection from misfortune, recovery from illness and financial luck.

The shrine appeared on TV Tokyo's "Shutsubotsu! Ad-machic Tengoku" (Adomachi) in its Saturday, May 21, 2022 broadcast on Arakawa Yuen, when the program's official page listed Ogu Hachiman Shrine at No. 2 (this was in 2022). The page describes it as a neighborhood guardian shrine more than 600 years old, popular for goshuin that feature seasonal flowers and a Toden tram.

## Highlights

The Arakawa Ward website lists the following cultural properties held by the shrine: the "Kamiogu-mura Murae-zu" (a village map) and the shrine's ridgepole tablets (both ward-designated), and a stone water basin inscribed with a date in 1729 (ward-registered). According to an article in the ward's former portal archive, the village map shows the old irrigation channel called "Hachimanbori" that once surrounded the area; it was designated a ward tangible cultural property in 1990 and is now kept at the Arakawa Furusato Bunkakan. Some items are not open to the public, so contact the owner or administrator in advance if you wish to see them.

According to a visitor's account, the present worship hall was rebuilt in 1955 after the original was lost in the 1945 air raids; past the stone torii, the hall stands at the top of a flight of stone steps. Beside the hall is a small subsidiary shrine, Itsukushima Shrine, which is said to enshrine Ichikishimahime no Mikoto. The shrine offers amulets for traffic safety, safe childbirth, recovery from illness, financial luck, victory, exam success, pets and more, including an "Ogu Benten" amulet (per the amulet list on the official site).

## Visiting Tips

**Access**: It is right next to the Miyanomae stop on the Toden Arakawa Line (Tokyo Sakura Tram), about a 1-minute walk per Arakawa Ward. The official site also lists about 5 minutes on foot from Kumanomae Station (Nippori-Toneri Liner), about 15 minutes on foot from JR Tabata Station or Ogu Station, and about 5 minutes on foot from the "Kodai" stop of Toei Bus route Higashi 43.

**Hours**: The opening hours of the grounds are not stated on the official site. The shrine office gives out amulets and goshuin 10:00 AM–4:00 PM and accepts prayer requests 10:00 AM–3:00 PM; these may change at year-end and New Year. Prayers for Shichi-Go-San, first shrine visits for babies, protection from misfortune and the blessing of school bags are offered. Same-day requests are accepted, but they may not be possible when they overlap with festivals or events, so booking ahead is recommended (per the official site). The office's phone number is 03-3893-1535.

**Goshuin**: Given out 10:00 AM–4:00 PM as pre-written sheets only; the shrine does not currently write into goshuin books or send them by mail. Some days have none, so check the calendar on the official site. At four shrines along the Toden Arakawa Line (Nanasha Shrine, Sugamo Tenso Shrine, Otori Shrine and Ogu Hachiman Shrine), a Toden stamp is added to the goshuin, and each shrine has a "Toden Jinja Meguri" pamphlet. The official site lists seasonal and special designs but does not state the offering; some visitor accounts report ¥500 (¥1,000 for the paper-cut design), so please confirm when you receive one.

**Parking**: The grounds have parking for up to 7 cars, for people visiting for prayer or worship only. It is not available at year-end, during the first three days of January, or on festival days. The lot right by the entrance is a monthly rental, and the official site asks visitors not to park there.

## Annual Festivals

- **Annual festival (first Saturday and Sunday of August; August 1–2 in 2026)**: According to the Arakawa Ward website, every four years the festival becomes the grand "Shinko-sai," with a procession of horses, floats and mikoshi through the Ogu neighborhood. The shrine's official site also carries notices about the 2026 festival, including road closures for the mikoshi procession and a call for mikoshi bearers.
- **Kinensai (February) and Niiname-sai (December)**: Listed in the ward's archive article as annual rites alongside the August festival.
- **Summer purification rite (June)**: Announced on the official site's 2026 notices. Please check the official site for the date.
